Funds are crowded short, and a sustained deterioration of Southern Plains conditions through the critical March–April green-up window would carry meaningful short-covering risk.

**Macro / Policy** — The macro backdrop contributed modestly to grain market softness this week without providing any structural shock. The dollar index held near 104.2, effectively unchanged on the week, leaving U.S. export competitiveness broadly stable relative to competing origins. WTI crude fell $1.80 to $72.40/bbl, softening the margin support for corn-based ethanol demand at the margin but not yet reaching a level that would materially alter ethanol plant operating economics. No major U.S. grain trade or agricultural policy announcements emerged during the week, and the market's attention remained focused on the WASDE balance sheet rather than macro catalysts. ## Weather & Crop Conditions

The U.S. winter wheat belt is the one location in the global grain map where weather is actively building a risk premium — and it is not yet large enough to override the bearish WASDE backdrop, but it is growing. The national winter wheat crop rating of 44 percent good/excellent as of March 8 represents a two-point decline from the prior week and sits 11 percentage points below the same date last year. More pointedly, the poor/very poor category has expanded three points in a single week to 21 percent, and Kansas topsoil moisture rated 41 percent short-to-very-short identifies the hard red winter wheat belt as the specific stress zone. The 7-day forecast reinforces the concern: below-normal precipitation and above-normal temperatures are projected across Kansas, Oklahoma, and the Texas panhandle — the precise geography that carries the largest hard red winter wheat acreage. If this pattern extends into late March and early April, when HRW wheat breaks dormancy and enters jointing, the market will begin embedding a yield discount that is currently absent from the balance sheet.

The Soft Red Winter corridor — Ohio, Indiana, and the lower Midwest — is rated 58 percent good/excellent, well above the national average, and is not experiencing any comparable moisture stress. The divergence between SRW and HRW crop conditions is relevant to basis and spread dynamics: Kansas City HRW futures may begin to outperform Chicago SRW on a relative basis if southern Plains dryness persists, potentially narrowing the HRW-SRW spread from its current 26-cent premium.

In South America, Brazil's soybean harvest at 71 percent complete as of March 7 is running approximately 13 points ahead of last year's pace, confirming CONAB's 127.8 MMT production estimate and validating USDA's 127 MMT figure in the March WASDE. The accelerating harvest removes the weather-related price risk premium that had provided intermittent support to U.S. soybean futures through February. Second-crop corn safrinha planting is 96 percent complete, but late-planted fields in Mato Grosso carry a compressed yield window if dry conditions develop during grain-fill in April. Argentina's corn crop, 18 percent harvested at 41 percent good/excellent, reflects stress that the market has already partially discounted: the BCBA has reduced its production estimate to 49.5 MMT, down from 51 MMT. Argentine soybean pod-fill is progressing after recent rains of 20–40mm across Buenos Aires province — welcome moisture, though arriving late for the earliest-planted fields, with the BCBA estimating output at 48.2 MMT.

## Corn — Deep Dive

### Futures & Spreads

May corn's 11-cent decline to $4.42/bu traced the contours of a market that absorbed a broadly bearish WASDE without collapsing — a behavioral signal that is as informative as the price move itself. The two-cent post-WASDE rally was short-lived: once traders worked through the global balance sheet revision, which added 2.4 MMT to world corn stocks to reach 297.4 MMT — 1.9 MMT above the trade consensus — selling resumed and the market settled weaker on the session. The U.S. carryout at 1.540 bbu was 30 mbu above expectations, but traders had largely neutralized this risk through pre-WASDE positioning; the domestic figure was not the catalyst. New-crop December corn at $4.51/bu reflects a 9-cent premium over May, a calendar structure of 68 percent of full carry over the July/December interval — wide enough to indicate the market is comfortable with storage economics into fall, but not so inverted as to signal new-crop demand urgency competing with old-crop supply. The compression of the May/July spread to 41 percent of full carry is the more notable structural development: front-end spreads are tightening despite bearish fundamentals, a pattern consistent with physical commercial demand pulling on old-crop barges rather than speculative positioning. That divergence between near-term spread tightening and a supply-adequate balance sheet is the core tension in corn futures heading into spring, and it deserves commercial attention even within a broadly bearish framework.

### Funds & Positioning

Managed money's net short of 108,400 contracts as of Tuesday, March 3, represents the most committed bearish speculative stance in corn since November 2025, and the addition of 9,200 contracts to the short in a single week signals continued institutional conviction in the downside narrative. The risk embedded in this positioning is asymmetric: funds have built a position large enough to amplify any bullish catalyst — a weather disruption in Argentina, a surprise Chinese purchase announcement, or a sustained acceleration in weekly export inspections — into a sharp, self-reinforcing short-covering rally. The May corn WASDE reaction was the first stress test of this positioning under a bearish fundamental print, and the market passed without triggering a cascade of fresh selling. That resilience does not invalidate the bearish thesis, but it does suggest the easy money on the short side may already be captured. Commercial hedgers and merchandisers with exposure to corn prices should treat the current managed money short as a volatility amplifier in both directions; the risk of a violent short-covering event is highest in corn and does not require a bullish fundamental catalyst — only a reduction in fund conviction sufficient to trigger position liquidation.

### Cash & Basis

Gulf corn basis firming four cents to -14¢ under May is the week's most counterintuitive signal in the grain complex: basis strengthened while futures fell, a divergence that reflects genuine physical demand rather than speculative price action. The Illinois River at -26¢ under May, up two cents, confirms the firming is not isolated to the Gulf export corridor. Iowa ethanol plant bids holding at +6¢ over May indicate thin local corn availability and sustained processing demand despite marginally weaker crude oil. The one soft node in the corn basis complex is the Kansas feedlot bid at -18¢ under May, down two cents — a weakening consistent with the Southern Plains functioning as a less active demand center at current price levels. The Gulf basis recovery to -14¢ represents the top end of a short-term logistical window created by barge pre-positioning ahead of inspection ramp-up, and whether it sustains depends directly on whether weekly inspections maintain the improvement begun in the March 5 data; a return to sub-1.1 MMT weekly inspection pace would quickly erode the basis recovery.

### Export Flows

Weekly inspections of 1.214 MMT for the week ending March 5 improved 11.5 percent from the prior week's 1.089 MMT — a constructive directional move, though the year-over-year comparison remains 9.5 percent below the same week in 2025. The cumulative YTD pace of 22.4 MMT represents 35 percent of the 63.5 MMT USDA annual export target with approximately 28 weeks remaining, requiring a sustained weekly run rate of approximately 1.5 MMT to close the gap — meaningfully above the recent weekly average. Mexico's 0.31 MMT purchase anchors the U.S. corn export program as the largest single-week destination, consistent with its structural role as the anchor buyer throughout the marketing year. Japan's 0.22 MMT and South Korea's 0.18 MMT round out the top destinations. Notably absent from the inspection data is any material Chinese volume — a demand channel that, if activated, could materially change the annual trajectory. Weekly export sales of 892,000 MT for the week ending February 27 were within the 700K–1.1 MMT market expectation, confirming that the forward sales pipeline is not deteriorating materially even as the annual target recedes from reach.

### So What?

Elevator operators holding unpriced old-crop corn should treat the current Gulf basis recovery to -14¢ under May as a merchandising window with a defined expiration. The basis firming reflects barge logistics and pre-positioning activity rather than structural demand acceleration, and USDA's decision to cut the annual export forecast by 50 mbu formalizes the underlying weakness in the trade flow. Operators with old-crop corn in commercial storage who have not yet established a sales basis should price in the next two to three weeks before the May contract rolls and the logistical demand impulse fades. Merchandisers running corn and soybean origination simultaneously should recognize that corn Gulf basis is outperforming soybean Gulf basis on a relative basis this week — a rotation that may justify increasing corn merchandising activity while soybean export prospects remain under Brazilian harvest pressure. Hedgers evaluating new-crop Dec '26 at $4.51/bu against production costs should note that the 76 percent of full carry in the Dec/Mar spread provides almost no storage incentive for new crop; if cost of production is below $4.51, pricing a portion of anticipated production is defensible at current carry structure.

## Soybeans — Deep Dive

### Futures & Spreads

November soybeans fell 14 cents to $9.71/bu, breaching the prior week's low and confirming that the market is in active price-rationing mode: lower prices are needed to stimulate demand or discourage planted area in the face of a global supply surplus. The March WASDE delivered two compounding bearish signals — U.S. ending stocks 20 mbu above trade expectations at 390 mbu, and world ending stocks at 124.8 MMT, 2.8 MMT above consensus and representing a substantial upward revision from February's 122.6 MMT. The Brazilian production upgrade to 127 MMT supplied all of the world stock increase; Argentina was held at 49 MMT, consistent with the BCBA's 48.2–49.5 MMT range. New-crop November at $9.71/bu trades at a 13-cent discount to May, implying the new-crop curve is pricing in the full weight of anticipated Brazilian and Argentine supply before U.S. harvest begins. The Nov/Jan '27 spread at 79 percent of full carry confirms the market's assessment that new-crop soybeans will be ample and priced to store — not priced to move. For a commercial operator evaluating forward pricing of new-crop soybeans, the current carry structure provides almost no reward for storing into late 2026, reinforcing the case for pricing early rather than holding for a post-harvest basis improvement that is unlikely to materialize given the global supply posture.

### Funds & Positioning

The soybean managed money short of 41,600 contracts sits within the 38,000–50,000 range that has defined the oscillation band over the past four weeks. The 3,800-contract short covering in the March 3 reporting period — a small but measurable retreat — suggests that fund managers are not adding conviction to the bearish soybean trade at the same rate they are in corn. The divergence between corn (aggressive short-building) and soybeans (mild short-covering) may reflect a thesis that soybeans have already discounted more of the South American supply overhang than corn has, or that domestic crush demand support provides a more robust price floor. Either way, the soybean fund short is not yet at levels that would generate a mechanically large short-covering event — unlike corn — which means that if bearish fundamental pressure continues from Brazilian harvest acceleration, soybeans are more vulnerable to an orderly grind lower than to a spike reversal. The positioning picture therefore does not provide the same cushion against further price erosion that the corn short creates in the opposite direction.

### Cash & Basis

Gulf soybean basis softened three cents to -38¢ under May, a directional move that accurately reflects the competitive reality: Brazilian FOB offers are undercutting U.S. Gulf origin by $1.12–$1.18/bu on a delivered basis to key Asian destinations including China and Indonesia. At that premium for U.S. origin, Chinese buyers face a clear economic choice, and the data confirms they are exercising it — while China's 0.41 MMT inspection figure for the week ending March 5 represents continued activity, the forward sales figure of 411,000 MT for the week ending February 27 was below the 450,000–700,000 MT market expectation, signaling that the sales pipeline is not rebuilding at a pace that could offset the impending Brazilian export surge. Illinois crush plant bids at -22¢ under May and Iowa crush plant bids at -19¢ under May are both marginally weaker than a week ago but remain sufficiently supportive of origination activity; crush margins are covering variable costs for well-positioned processors, and the domestic demand pull continues to provide a backstop to interior soybean values that is absent on the export side. This 16–19 cent premium of interior processor bids over Gulf export basis is the most important current structural feature in the soybean cash market.

### International Competition

Brazil's harvest at 71 percent complete — 13 percentage points ahead of last year — means the window of maximum Brazilian soybean export competition is arriving earlier and with greater intensity than the 2025 marketing year. CONAB's March production estimate of 127.8 MMT, essentially in line with USDA's 127 MMT, removes the scenario of a production shortfall that had been a tail risk as recently as six weeks ago. Santos FOB offers for April–May shipment are already undercutting U.S. Gulf origin by over $1.10/bu, and that differential is expected to widen through April as Brazil's full harvest volume enters the export pipeline. Argentine soybean pod-fill, while benefiting from recent rainfall, reflects a crop already partially stressed: the BCBA's 48.2 MMT estimate is below USDA's 49 MMT, suggesting potential for further Argentine production downgrades that could modestly tighten the South American supply contribution in the second half of the marketing year — but not enough to materially change the global balance sheet given Brazil's confirmed 127 MMT crop.

### So What?

Crush plant operators evaluating Q2 and Q3 soybean coverage should consider that the window of purchasing interior basis below -20¢ under nearby futures is likely to widen, not narrow, over the next six to eight weeks as Brazilian harvest pressure expands. Interior crush bids are currently firm relative to Gulf basis, but this divergence is not indefinitely sustainable: if Gulf basis softens another 5–10 cents as Brazil accelerates its export program, interior processor bids will follow lower. Operators who have not locked in forward crush margins for April–June should assess whether current margin levels justify forward coverage before the export basis correction spreads inland. Exporters with forward sales commitments to Asian destinations should benchmark current U.S. Gulf values against Santos FOB offers weekly; the present -38¢ Gulf basis already represents a significant price concession, and an additional widening could make it rational to seek basis release from buyers or explore origin-swapping arrangements. Merchandisers positioning soybean futures should recognize that the Nov/Jan '27 spread at 79 percent of full carry offers almost no carry incentive — any long soybean position established for storage purposes carries full storage costs with minimal spread compensation against a bearish global supply backdrop.

## Wheat — Deep Dive

### Futures & Spreads

May SRW wheat's eight-cent decline to $5.32/bu and new contract low is the clearest expression of the WASDE's bearish verdict on the wheat complex: USDA increased U.S. ending stocks by 25 mbu to 840 mbu, 20 mbu above the 820 mbu trade estimate, and lifted world ending stocks to 260.4 MMT — 1.9 MMT above expectations. The new contract low removes technical support from prior price action and leaves May SRW in uncharted territory, a development that typically reduces commercial selling urgency while increasing speculative short interest from traders initiating new positions below prior lows. The SRW curve carries the heaviest carry structure in the complex: the Jul/Dec spread at 86 percent of full carry communicates that the market perceives no urgency to move wheat, sees adequate new-crop supply, and requires near-maximum storage incentive to hold physical wheat through the next nine months. HRW May at $5.58/bu carries a 26-cent premium over SRW May — a spread that partially reflects the weather-risk premium building in the southern Plains HRW crop, though the magnitude remains modest relative to the scale of moisture stress that would justify a larger divergence if the current dryness pattern persists through April.

### Funds & Positioning

The CBOT SRW managed money short of 74,200 contracts — expanded by 2,100 contracts in the most recent COT period — has not reversed to the long side since July 2025. The persistence and size of this position create a specific risk profile: it is not vulnerable to a fundamental improvement in the wheat balance sheet, which would require either a large unexpected demand catalyst or a significant production shortfall. It is, however, acutely vulnerable to a weather event in the southern Plains that the market has not yet priced into futures. A two-week period of crop stress reporting — declining good/excellent ratings, expanding poor/very poor acreage, and credible yield reduction forecasts — would likely trigger a short-covering event disproportionate to the fundamental adjustment, given how crowded the position is and how long funds have been short without a catalyst to reverse. The KCBT HRW short of 22,800 contracts is less extreme and reflects the market's ability to partially price weather risk through the Kansas City contract while the Chicago SRW position remains structurally anchored to the global supply-heavy narrative.

### Cash & Basis

Gulf SRW basis at -42¢ under May, deteriorating five cents on the week, is approaching levels that historically signal demand destruction rather than price competition. At this basis level, U.S. Gulf SRW is already at a significant discount to the USDA's implied FOB price, yet it is still not cheap enough to displace Black Sea origin into the Philippines, Nigeria, and other traditional U.S. wheat markets — the Philippines took 0.11 MMT in the March 5 inspection week, a constructive volume, but Black Sea origins continue to offer aggressively into Southeast Asian tender programs and are winning a disproportionate share of new business. Flour mill bids in the Midwest at -28¢ under May softened two cents in step with futures; domestic milling demand is not providing a meaningful backstop to SRW values. The Ohio River basis at -35¢ under May, down three cents, confirms the deterioration extends beyond the Gulf export corridor into interior origination channels — a broad-based weakening that reflects both the structural oversupply and the absence of any domestic industrial demand channel comparable to ethanol for corn or crush for soybeans.

### So What?

Commercial hedgers holding unpriced SRW wheat in on-farm or commercial storage should seriously evaluate forward sales or basis contracts at current levels. May at $5.32/bu represents a new contract low, and the fundamental backdrop — WASDE bearish, funds crowded short, global stocks elevated at 260.4 MMT — does not support a near-term recovery without a specific weather catalyst. The deferred SRW carry at 86 percent of full carry provides a narrow positive return for those who can store cheaply, but the carry premium does not compensate for the basis erosion risk if Black Sea competition intensifies through summer. Elevator operators originating SRW wheat in the Ohio corridor, where basis is relatively stronger at -35¢ versus the Gulf's -42¢, should consider basis selling at current levels as a defensive merchandising strategy. The one scenario that changes this guidance is a rapid deterioration of the HRW crop rating in Kansas — if good/excellent breaks below 35 percent in the next two to three weeks, the entire wheat complex would reprice higher on short-covering, offering a better selling opportunity that patient operators should be prepared to execute quickly and without hesitation.

For soybean interior basis, the 16–19 cent premium of Iowa and Illinois crush plant bids over Gulf soybean basis represents the crushing sector's ability to compete above export parity, sustaining domestic flow even as export channels soften. This inversion between interior soybean processing bids and Gulf export basis is the domestic demand signal currently preventing a more severe break in soybean flat price, and it will persist only as long as crush margins support aggressive processor bidding.

In corn, the South American origin picture is more constructive for U.S. competitiveness in the near term: Brazil's safrinha corn crop is still in the field, and meaningful Brazilian corn export competition for new-crop volume will not emerge until June–July at the earliest. This creates a window through May in which U.S. corn can compete on price and logistics in destinations where Mexico, Japan, and South Korea have active demand. The Gulf basis firming this week is consistent with commercial buyers pre-positioning in that window before South American corn export availability expands. Russia's Black Sea wheat program continues to undercut U.S. Gulf SRW at key destination tenders — the Philippines, Nigeria, and Indonesia — with competitive FOB offers reflecting Russia's large 2025/26 wheat crop and its strategic use of export pricing to maintain market share. Euronext wheat at €196.50/MT, down 1.2 percent on the week, confirms that European milling wheat is weakening in step with global supply fundamentals and provides no upside catalyst for U.S. wheat export competitiveness.

## Key Fundamentals & Reports

### March WASDE — Full Review

The March 10 WASDE release was the defining fundamental event of the week, and its verdict was consistent across all three commodities: USDA added supply at both the U.S. and world level, with no offsetting demand upgrades sufficient to neutralize the balance sheet revision.

For corn, U.S. ending stocks held at 1.540 bbu — unchanged from February and above the 1.510 bbu trade estimate — while the adjustment composition revealed an important internal shift: exports were cut 50 mbu to 2.275 bbu (below expectations of 2.300 bbu) and feed/residual was raised 50 mbu to offset. This rotation from export to domestic demand acknowledges that the shipment pace is failing to track toward the prior forecast while domestic consumption remains solid. World corn stocks at 297.4 MMT, up 2.4 MMT from February and 1.9 MMT above the trade consensus, exceeded expectations by a magnitude sufficient to shift the post-report market direction from a brief initial rally to a lower close.

The soybean WASDE was the most unambiguously bearish of the three. U.S. ending stocks were raised 10 mbu to 390 mbu — 20 mbu above the 370 mbu trade estimate — on the combination of a 50 mbu export reduction and a 10 mbu crush increase. The crush upgrade is a constructive signal for domestic processor demand, but it was fully overwhelmed by the export reduction and the global stocks revision: world soybean ending stocks at 124.8 MMT were 2.8 MMT above expectations and 2.2 MMT above the February figure, driven entirely by Brazil's 2 MMT production upgrade to 127 MMT. The stocks/use ratio expanded to 10.1 percent from 9.8 percent — a directional shift that the market had not fully anticipated and that the -9¢ reaction in November soybeans properly reflected.

Wheat saw U.S. ending stocks raised 25 mbu to 840 mbu against an 820 mbu expectation, while world stocks at 260.4 MMT exceeded consensus by nearly 2 MMT. The combined signal — higher domestic and global stocks with reduced export prospects — produced the new contract low in May SRW and reinforced the structural thesis that has defined the wheat market's bearish posture throughout Q1 2026.

## Synthesis & Outlook

The March WASDE consolidated and extended the bearish fundamental narrative that has characterized the grain complex through Q1 2026. All three commodities enter spring with global balance sheets larger than either the February WASDE or trade expectations anticipated, and prices have adjusted accordingly: May corn at $4.42/bu, May soybeans at $9.84/bu, and May SRW wheat at a new contract low of $5.32/bu each reflect a supply-adequate framework with no near-term catalyst sufficient to drive a sustained rally. The base-case outlook for the next two to four weeks is continued rangebound-to-lower trading, with the primary bearish pressure coming from accelerating Brazilian soybean export competition and the structural weight of the global corn and wheat balance sheets.

The most important non-fundamental variable — and the one creating the most asymmetric risk — is the managed money positioning complex. Funds hold a combined net short across corn (108,400), soybeans (41,600), and SRW wheat (74,200) that represents a substantial bearish commitment relative to historical norms. This positioning is consistent with the fundamental thesis but creates a fragile equilibrium: if any one commodity receives a genuinely bullish catalyst, the self-amplifying short-covering will be disproportionate to the fundamental change. Corn is the highest-conviction short — and therefore the highest short-covering risk. SRW wheat's crowded short, layered against a deteriorating southern Plains weather pattern, represents the most credible path to a disorderly short-covering event.

**Bullish triggers that would break the current range:**
- A second consecutive week of corn export inspections above 1.3 MMT, combined with new Chinese purchase announcements, would begin to challenge USDA's reduced export forecast and support a basis recovery
- Kansas topsoil moisture ratings deteriorating to 50 percent short-to-very-short in consecutive weeks, accompanied by a crop rating decline below 38 percent good/excellent, would activate short-covering in HRW with potential spillover to SRW and corn
- An Argentine soybean production downgrade of 2 MMT or more from current USDA estimates, tightening the world soybean balance sheet below the current price-rationing threshold
- Dollar index weakness below 102.0, narrowing the competitive gap between U.S. and Brazilian origin across all three export channels

**Bearish triggers that would accelerate the decline:**
- Continued acceleration of Brazilian soybean harvest toward 85 percent complete within two weeks, with active Santos loading data confirming full export pipeline availability, would push Gulf soybean basis another 5–8 cents lower and retest May soybeans below $9.70
- Chinese soybean purchases shifting entirely to Brazilian origin, evidenced by zero U.S. registrations in weekly inspection data for two or more consecutive weeks
- Corn weekly inspections reverting below 1.0 MMT, confirming last week's rebound was logistical noise rather than genuine demand improvement
- Dollar index strength above 106.0, amplifying the price disadvantage of U.S. origin across all three export channels simultaneously

The base case is a $4.30–$4.55/bu range for May corn, a $9.60–$10.10/bu range for May soybeans, and a $5.10–$5.45/bu range for May SRW wheat over the next three to four weeks. The probability-weighted skew is modestly to the downside for corn and soybeans, reflecting the weight of the Brazilian harvest overhang and the confirmed WASDE supply revision. For SRW wheat, the probability distribution is more symmetric: the fundamental bearish case is established, but the weather-driven short-covering risk is live and growing week by week. Operators who act as if prices can only go lower from here are underestimating the positioning risk in corn and wheat; operators who position for a sustained rally in soybeans are underestimating the structural weight of 127 MMT of Brazilian production entering export channels over the next 90 days.
