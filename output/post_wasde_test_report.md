## 2. Corn Market Deep Dive

**Post-WASDE Balance Sheet.** USDA held U.S. corn ending stocks at 1.540 billion bushels, marginally above the 1.510 billion trade estimate. The domestic number was overshadowed by the world stocks print of 297.4 MMT — 1.9 MMT above the pre-report consensus and 2.4 MMT above February — driven by Brazilian production upgrades. The market's initial 2-cent rally evaporated within the session. At a 10.2 percent stocks-to-use ratio and a world carryout expanding for the third consecutive WASDE, corn's price floor rests on demand, not supply risk.

**Futures & Spreads.** May corn settled at $4.42/bu, off 11 cents or 2.4 percent; December closed at $4.51/bu, down 9 cents. The May/July spread at +$0.09 (41 percent of full carry) tightened from +$0.12 last week — a firming of the nearby spread against weaker flat prices pointing to short-term logistical demand on old-crop. The Dec/Mar '27 spread at +$0.20, or 76 percent of full carry, compensates adequately for storage but is not widening, indicating no market incentive to extend carry beyond current cost.

**Funds & Positioning.** Managed money extended its corn short by 9,200 contracts to 108,400 — the largest since November 2025 — with selling in each of the past four consecutive weeks. The risk is asymmetric: a weather shock or unexpected demand surge could force rapid short-covering in a relatively illiquid market, producing a sharp rally without any change in the underlying balance sheet.

**Cash & Basis.** Gulf CIF corn basis firmed 4 cents to -14 cents under May; Illinois River tightened 2 cents to -26 cents; Iowa ethanol held at +6 cents over May. Kansas feedlot bids softened 2 cents to -18 cents. The export-channel firming reflects pre-inspection barge activity, not a demand inflection — and should not be read as a structural shift in buyer urgency.

**Export Sales & Inspections.** Weekly inspections of 1.214 MMT improved 11.5 percent over the prior week but remained 9.5 percent below year-ago. Mexico led at 0.31 MMT, Japan 0.22 MMT, South Korea 0.18 MMT. YTD cumulative of 22.4 MMT represents 35 percent of the 63.5 MMT annual target with 28 weeks remaining. New export sales of 892,000 MT were within expectations. Closing the YTD gap requires sustained weekly inspections above 1.5 MMT — a pace not yet established.

**Global Context.** Brazilian corn FOB is discounting U.S. Gulf by $1.12 to $1.18 per bushel, winning virtually every price-sensitive import tender. Argentine early-harvest material is similarly competitive. U.S. corn export viability is narrowed to Mexico and Northeast Asian buyers whose logistical or quality preferences favor Gulf origin — a structurally smaller market than twelve months ago.

**So What.** The Gulf basis recovery to -14 cents under May is a selling window, not a floor. The countertrend firming is logistical and short-lived; world stocks at 297.4 MMT and the crowded fund short are unchanged. Elevator operators with bin space should price grain against current Gulf bids rather than rolling into carry, and preserve optionality for a weather-driven rally that has not yet materialized.

## 3. Soybean Market Deep Dive

**Post-WASDE Balance Sheet.** USDA raised U.S. soybean ending stocks 10 million bushels to 390 million — 20 million above the trade estimate — by increasing projected crush 10 million bushels while cutting exports 50 million. The export cut explicitly acknowledges Brazilian competition has structurally reduced U.S. market share. Brazil's production was raised 2 MMT to 127 MMT; world stocks reached 124.8 MMT against a 122.0 MMT consensus. November soybeans fell 9 cents and undercut the prior week's low.

**Futures & Spreads.** May soybeans settled at $9.84/bu, off 18 cents or 1.8 percent; November at $9.71/bu, down 14 cents or 1.4 percent. The May/July spread at +$0.10 (52 percent of full carry) was little changed week-over-week, reflecting no shift in old-crop demand urgency. The Nov/Jan spread at +$0.17 (79 percent of full carry) signals market comfort compensating storage well into the new marketing year — consistent with ample South American supply bridging the gap to next U.S. harvest.

**Products & Crush.** Domestic crush margins remain the one genuine support pillar in the soybean complex. Interior crush plant bids held at -22 cents under May in Illinois and -19 cents in Iowa — essentially unchanged — confirming processors are not stepping back from purchasing programs even as Gulf basis softens. Soybean meal and oil values remain well-bid relative to flat bean prices, sustaining the crush margin that underpins interior basis stability.

**Funds & Positioning.** Managed money covered 3,800 contracts, trimming the net short to 41,600. The mild covering reflects pre-WASDE position management rather than a fundamental rethink; the four-week trend has been range-bound between 38,000 and 50,000 contracts net short. At current levels the soybean short is not at historical extremes, but any Brazilian supply disruption or unexpected Chinese demand acceleration could catalyze a more significant unwind.

**Cash & Basis.** Gulf CIF soybean basis softened 3 cents to -38 cents under May; Illinois crush eased 1 cent to -22 cents; Iowa crush held at -19 cents. The Gulf weakening against stable interior bids is characteristic of a market where offshore competition specifically affects the export channel while domestic processing demand provides a stable floor. Basis recovery at the Gulf requires either a reduction in Brazilian FOB offers or a surge in Chinese demand for U.S.-origin beans — neither is imminent.

**Export Sales & Inspections.** Weekly inspections of 0.842 MMT fell from 0.914 MMT the prior week but exceeded last year by 8.9 percent. China took 0.41 MMT. YTD of 38.7 MMT represents 74 percent of the 52.2 MMT annual target — a pace built primarily on October-through-January sales when Brazilian supply was limited. New export sales of 411,000 MT missed the 450,000–700,000 MT expectation, confirming forward booking has shifted toward Brazilian origin.

**Global Context.** Brazilian Santos FOB undercuts U.S. Gulf by $1.12 to $1.18 per bushel, routing spot Chinese buying entirely to South American origin. Argentine Up-River FOB is competitive for meal-targeted crushers. U.S. soybean export viability rests on logistical relationships and long-term contracts rather than price competitiveness — a structurally weaker position that USDA's 50 million bushel export cut has now formally recognized.

**So What.** For soybean crushers, firm interior basis against weakening Gulf basis is a structural origination opportunity: buy from country elevators at domestic prices and process at margin. For merchandisers with unpriced old-crop beans, the 74 percent YTD pace is front-loaded — the remaining 26 percent must ship against a Brazilian export surge through April and May. Price unhedged old-crop soybeans against current firm interior basis while it holds.

## 4. Wheat Market Deep Dive

**Post-WASDE Balance Sheet.** USDA raised U.S. wheat ending stocks 25 million bushels above February to 840 million — 20 million above expectations — by cutting projected exports 25 million bushels. The export reduction is an explicit acknowledgment that Black Sea and EU origin have displaced U.S. wheat in key markets. World stocks at 260.4 MMT exceeded consensus by 1.9 MMT. May SRW fell 8 cents on the session and set a new contract low; HRW May closed at $5.58/bu, off 6 cents.

**Futures & Spreads.** May SRW settled the week at $5.32/bu, a new contract low. July at $5.41/bu. The May/July spread at +$0.11 (58 percent of full carry) and the July/December spread at +$0.23 (86 percent of full carry) collectively describe a curve rewarding storage generously through year-end — a strong signal that the market expects no demand urgency and is content to pay near-full carrying charges to defer movement.

**Funds & Positioning.** Managed money added 2,100 contracts to its SRW short, reaching 74,200 — with no net long since July 2025. The HRW short sits at 22,800 contracts, roughly unchanged. Eight consecutive months of net short positioning in SRW reflects sustained conviction that supply is adequate and demand insufficient. The concentrated short creates a meaningful squeeze risk specifically if Kansas crop conditions deteriorate materially before green-up.

**Cash & Basis.** SRW Gulf CIF basis fell 5 cents to -42 cents under May — the sharpest basis move across all three commodities. Ohio River declined 3 cents to -35 cents; Midwest flour mill bids eased 2 cents to -28 cents. The uniform deterioration across all SRW buying points confirms a directional signal, not regional noise. At -42 cents under May, U.S. SRW needs Black Sea to soften or a domestic weather premium to emerge before basis can recover.

**Export Sales & Inspections.** Weekly inspections improved to 0.378 MMT from 0.291 MMT but remained 8.2 percent below year-ago. Philippines at 0.11 MMT, Mexico 0.09 MMT, Nigeria 0.07 MMT. YTD of 17.6 MMT represents 75 percent of the 23.4 MMT annual target — technically on pace, contingent on maintaining current weekly volume against Black Sea competition. New sales of 263,000 MT were in line with expectations, confirming the absence of any large-buyer accumulation in U.S. origin.

**Global Context.** Russian wheat FOB remains near multi-year lows, capturing North African and Middle Eastern tenders at prices SRW Gulf cannot match at -42 cents under May. Euronext May wheat fell 1.2 percent to €196.50/MT. EU soft wheat is competitive in Mediterranean destinations. The only U.S. wheat advantage is protein-differentiated HRW into baking-quality markets — a niche that limits volume potential regardless of price.

**So What.** Flour millers underhedged on second- and third-quarter requirements should treat any weather-driven SRW rally as a buying opportunity rather than a signal to wait. The asymmetry favors coverage: the downside is bounded by contract lows with no obvious fundamental catalyst for further collapse, while the upside from a Kansas weather event between now and jointing is non-trivial. For country elevators handling HRW, a hedge ratio review is warranted given the 22,800-contract fund short that could unwind violently on weather news.

## 7. Key Fundamentals & Reports

**USDA WASDE.** The March WASDE delivered a uniformly bearish message: world stocks exceeded expectations in all three commodities, South American production was upgraded, and U.S. domestic balance sheets were either unchanged or above consensus. The market's failure to decline sharply against these surprises — particularly in corn — reflects an existing fund short that is too large to add to aggressively, not a fundamental buying conviction. The aggregate tone is supply-adequate, demand-fragile.

**Export Inspections.** Weekly inspections showed improvement in corn (1.214 MMT, +11.5 percent week-over-week) and wheat (0.378 MMT, +30 percent week-over-week), with soybeans slightly lower at 0.842 MMT. Year-over-year, all three commodities remain below last year's pace. The cumulative YTD shortfall is most acute in corn at 35 percent of target; soybeans at 74 percent are most advanced relative to annual target.

**Export Sales.** New sales for the week ending February 27: corn 892,000 MT (within expectations), soybeans 411,000 MT (below the 450,000–700,000 MT range), wheat 263,000 MT (in line). The soybean miss is the most significant data point — it confirms forward booking has shifted to Brazilian origin faster than the inspection data alone would suggest, consistent with USDA's 50 million bushel export reduction.

**NOPA Soybean Crush.** The most recent available NOPA data showed a January crush rate consistent with strong domestic processing demand, supported by biodiesel mandates and meal export commitments. February NOPA (due mid-March) will test whether crush economics are holding at the level embedded in USDA's upgraded 2.400 billion bushel annual crush projection. Interior crush basis holding at -19 to -22 cents under May suggests the trade expects the February number to validate the upgrade.

**CFTC Positioning.** Funds added 9,200 contracts to the corn short (108,400), added 2,100 to SRW wheat (74,200), and covered 3,800 soybean contracts (41,600 net short remaining). The corn short is at a multi-month extreme; the SRW short has been sustained for eight consecutive months. The aggregate speculative short creates acute squeeze risk if any one market receives a bullish catalyst — weather, Chinese demand, or a production downgrade — that spreads across the complex.

**So What.** This week's government data reinforced supply adequacy across all three markets while confirming soybean demand is migrating to Brazil. The one constructive signal is the NOPA crush pace, which suggests domestic processing demand is absorbing beans at a rate that prevents the domestic balance sheet from deteriorating faster than USDA projects. Absent a demand inflection in the inspection or sales data over the next two weeks, the fundamental tape remains bearish.

## 10. Synthesis & Outlook

**Introduction.** The March WASDE's simultaneous misses in world corn stocks (+1.9 MMT above consensus), world soybean stocks (+2.8 MMT above consensus), and U.S. wheat ending stocks (+20 million bushels above consensus) delivered the clearest bearish supply signal of the marketing year — yet May corn finished the week at $4.42/bu rather than probing new lows. That price resilience, against a fund short at 108,400 contracts and a bearish WASDE, is the most analytically significant development of the week: markets well-supplied and well-short rarely fall further without a demand disappointment to catalyze the next leg down.

**Corn Outlook.** Base-case range: May corn $4.25–$4.55/bu, December corn $4.35–$4.65/bu over the next two to three weeks. The lower bound is defined by the crowded fund short and the psychological difficulty of adding aggressively to a position at multi-month extremes; the upper bound is defined by world stocks at 297.4 MMT and a YTD export pace requiring 1.5 MMT-plus weekly inspections to close the gap to the annual USDA forecast. The single most important factor is the export pace: sustained inspections above 1.5 MMT erode the short narrative; a second consecutive week below 1.1 MMT tests the lower bound.

**Soybean Outlook.** Base-case range: November soybeans $9.40–$9.90/bu. The lower bound is defined by USDA's new 390 million bushel ending stocks figure; the upper bound by domestic crush margin support embedded in interior basis. The key variable is Chinese import demand over the next six weeks: acceleration above 0.7 MMT per week in U.S.-origin inspections breaks the range higher; continued measured buying while Brazil flows freely tests the lower bound before month-end. The April WASDE treatment of Argentine soybeans — currently 0.8 MMT above BCBA's estimate — is the secondary watchpoint.

**Wheat Outlook.** Base-case range: May SRW $5.10–$5.50/bu, biased toward the lower bound. World stocks at 260.4 MMT and Gulf basis at -42 cents under May leave U.S. wheat without a price-discovery argument from the demand side. The upside scenario is weather-driven: a Kansas good-to-excellent decline below 38 percent nationally before green-up would attract weather premium and trigger short-covering in the 74,200-contract SRW fund short. The HRW May contract at $5.58/bu is defensively better-positioned due to protein premium and a narrower fund short.

**Bullish Triggers.** Three specific developments would push prices materially above the base-case ranges. First, Kansas winter wheat good-to-excellent falling below 38 percent nationally by April 1 with continued below-normal precipitation — forcing yield reduction assumptions into both HRW and SRW balance sheets and triggering broad short-covering. Second, Chinese soybean weekly inspections accelerating above 0.7 MMT for two consecutive weeks before month-end, signaling front-running of Brazilian availability that would directly narrow the WASDE's 50 million bushel export reduction. Third, a disruption to Brazil's safrinha corn crop from April dryness in Mato Grosso reducing CONAB's second-crop estimate by 3 MMT or more — abruptly cutting the Brazilian corn competition that caps U.S. futures below $4.50/bu.

**Bearish Triggers.** Three developments would push prices through the lower bounds. First, corn export inspections below 1.0 MMT for a second consecutive week, widening the YTD gap and prompting questions about whether the 2.275 billion bushel annual forecast requires a fourth consecutive WASDE reduction in April. Second, a smooth Brazilian safrinha establishment through March and early April, removing the one remaining weather risk in corn and extending Brazilian export competition into the June-August window. Third, a U.S. spring planting season that develops without disruption — an early, rapid corn and soybean planting pace in April and May reinforces the new-crop supply narrative and prevents any weather rally from sustaining.

**Summary.** The grain complex is structurally bearish — world stocks well-supplied in all three commodities, fund shorts near marketing-year extremes, South American supply competitive across the board — but is not in free-fall because the short is too large and the bearish news is now fully on the tape. Prices require a specific catalyst to rally and require a demand disappointment to fall materially further. The bias is sideways-to-lower within the defined ranges absent weather, Chinese demand, or a crop failure in South America. Manage risk accordingly: sell into basis strength, cover wheat forward, and preserve optionality for the weather event that has not yet arrived.
