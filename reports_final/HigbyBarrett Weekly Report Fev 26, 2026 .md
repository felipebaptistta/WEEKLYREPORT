Corn Market Deep Dive
Futures & Spreads: Corn futures firmed slightly this week, with March settling near $4.27 3/4, up 2 cents
from February 19. While flat price moved only marginally, the March–May spread widened to roughly an
11 cent carry, consistent with approximately 72 percent of full carry under the two-month storage
framework. The market remains comfortably in carry, signaling adequate nearby supply and stable
commercial coverage rather than tightening. Volatility remains contained, and price action continues to
reflect consolidation rather than directional momentum. The curve is stable even as the market continues
to weigh strong execution against an otherwise ample global supply backdrop.

Funds & Positioning: Managed Money holds a net short position near 42,313 contracts as of the
February 17 report, reflecting a defensive but not extreme speculative stance. The shift was driven
primarily by modest additions to gross shorts rather than aggressive long liquidation. While the net short
is meaningful, it remains below prior cycle extremes. Importantly, spreads have not collapsed in response
to the heavier positioning, suggesting that speculative pressure is being absorbed without structural
deterioration. Funds appear positioned defensively but not aggressively pressing the downside.

Cash & Basis: Cash markets strengthened modestly. CIF Gulf corn improved to +92H, reflecting firm
export demand and minor freight friction tied to draft constraints along portions of the river system. Interior

river bids firmed as well, while processor bids held steady. Basis is not surging, but the tone is
constructive. The physical market continues to signal steady movement rather than distress, with logistics
normalized enough to prevent bottlenecks but tight enough to keep freight supportive. Nearby commercial
flow remains orderly.

Export Sales & Inspections: Export inspections for the week ending February 19 accelerated to
approximately 2.01 MMT, a clear step higher from the prior week and reinforcing strong execution.
Cumulative shipments remain comfortably ahead of last year and continue tracking in line with USDA’s
export projection. Weekly export sales remain active, with forward commitments sustaining program
visibility into late winter. Shipments represent a large share of USDA’s export projection, underscoring
that demand performance remains the primary stabilizing force in the balance sheet. The export channel
continues to offset softer flat price dynamics.

Global Context: Brazil’s February corn export pace, while active, is running below last year’s level,
reducing immediate competitive pressure at the margin. Argentine production estimates remain steady,
though recent rainfall variability introduces localized uncertainty. Black Sea corn offers remain competitive
but not decisively undercutting U.S. Gulf into core Western Hemisphere destinations. Mexico continues to
anchor U.S. demand, and Latin American flows remain consistent. Global supply remains ample, but U.S.
origin retains competitive footing in key channels.

So What? Corn’s structure remains more stable than the board implies. Flat price has softened, and
speculative shorts remain meaningful, yet spreads hold near around 72 percent of full carry and basis has
firmed modestly. Export execution remains the anchor. Absent a deterioration in shipments or a renewed
surge in global competition, the market continues to trade within a carry-defined range. For now, structure
is steady, positioning is defensive, and downside momentum remains contained by physical flow.

Soybean Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: Soybeans softened modestly this week, with March settling near $11.34. The
March–May spread widened to roughly a 16 cent carry, near 70 percent of full carry. The structure
remains clearly in contango but not at full storage economics, suggesting adequate nearby supply without
signaling pressure. Price action reflects stabilization rather than momentum, with the front end supported
but not urgent.

Products & Crush: Domestic processing continues to anchor the complex. January NOPA crush at
221.56 million bushels reaffirmed historically strong throughput, even as soybean oil stocks remain
elevated near 1.9 billion pounds. Crush margins continue to lean meal-driven, with soybean meal demand
providing the bulk of processing support. The product complex has not accelerated meaningfully, but
neither has it deteriorated. Domestic use remains the primary structural pillar for U.S. soybeans as export
competitiveness fluctuates.

Cash & Basis: Cash markets softened slightly at the Gulf, with CIF values easing toward +95H as
Brazilian harvest pressure builds. Interior processor bids slipped modestly as well, reflecting reduced
urgency amid improving South American logistics. The nearby market remains supported by crush pull
rather than export-driven tightening. Basis tone is stable but not strengthening, underscoring the
divergence between steady domestic demand and heavier global supply availability.

Funds & Positioning: Managed Money holds a net long position near 92,000 contracts as of the
February 17 report. Positioning is constructive rather than defensive, which helps explain why breaks
have been absorbed even as Brazil dominates the export window. The tradeoff is that length introduces
liquidation risk if South American flow stays smooth and demand fails to re-accelerate. In short, soybeans

are not set up like a crowded short market; they are supported, but more vulnerable to sentiment shifts if
the fundamental tape softens.

Export Sales & Inspections: Weekly export inspections for the week ending February 19 totaled
approximately 0.67 MMT, a noticeable step down from the prior week. Cumulative inspections remain
materially behind last year’s pace, reflecting Brazil’s dominant early-season window. ANEC projects
February soybean exports near 11.4 MMT, well above last year’s February pace and reinforcing South
America’s pricing influence. U.S. export flow remains functional but secondary in the global trade rotation.
The export channel continues to operate, but it is not the primary demand driver at this stage of the
marketing year.

Global Context: Brazil’s soybean production estimate remains near 178 MMT, with harvest advancing
under broadly favorable conditions across central regions. FOB Santos values remain competitive into
Asia, effectively setting the ceiling for U.S. pricing absent a freight or currency shift. Argentina’s soybean
outlook holds near 48.5 MMT, though renewed dryness across second-crop areas reintroduces weather
sensitivity. For now, Brazil defines the global supply narrative, while Argentina remains the volatility
variable rather than a confirmed disruption.

So What? Soybeans remain supported by domestic processing but capped by Brazil’s seasonal export
dominance. The board finished slightly lower on the week and the March–May spread widened versus
last week, reinforcing a carry-driven tone as nearby coverage stays comfortable and South America sets
the external ceiling. Export flow remains functional but secondary, while crush continues to provide the
structural floor. With managed money net long near 92,000 contracts, the complex is more sensitive to an
upside surprise in demand or South American weather than to a liquidation-driven break. Absent a
material shift in export competitiveness or a realized supply disruption in South America, price discovery
is likely to remain anchored by carry economics and the pace of Brazilian flow.

Wheat Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: Wheat futures recovered modestly this week, with March Chicago SRW settling
near $5.63 1/2, up 4 cents from February 19. The March–May spread widened toward a 10 cent carry,
roughly 61 percent of full carry, reinforcing comfortable nearby supply without signaling a market fully
rewarding storage. The rally continues to read as more technical than fundamentally driven, with structure
remaining in carry and consistent with adequate coverage. Kansas City and Minneapolis followed a
similar tone, with no meaningful divergence within the complex.

Cash & Basis: Cash markets were largely steady. CIF Gulf SRW basis softened modestly toward +65H,
while interior levels showed little evidence of tightening. Plains HRW basis remained stable near -20H
despite localized weather variability, and Pacific Northwest values held firm, reflecting routine coverage
rather than scarcity. The absence of basis acceleration alongside widening carry underscores a market
that is adequately supplied. Physical flow remains orderly, and commercial coverage appears sufficient.

Export Sales & Inspections: Weekly export inspections for the week ending February 19 increased to
approximately 0.54 MMT, a firmer pace relative to the prior week. Export sales remain routine but not
accelerating, and cumulative shipments continue to track broadly in line with USDA projections. While
export flow is functioning, volumes remain insufficient to materially alter the broader balance sheet
narrative. The program is steady, but not dynamic.

Global Context: Russian 12.5 percent FOB values firmed toward $235/MT, offering modest support to

global pricing, and Euronext wheat rebounded nearly 3 percent on the week. However, Black Sea supply
remains structurally available, and competitive offers into North Africa and the Middle East continue to
anchor world values. Ukrainian exports remain active through established channels, and European origin
has re-entered select tenders. No logistical or policy disruption has emerged to materially tighten
availability. Global supply remains ample, even if short-term pricing has stabilized.

Funds & Positioning: Managed Money maintains a substantial net short position near 69,567 contracts
as of the February 17 report. While exposure is below prior extremes, it remains meaningful and
continues to reflect a defensive stance. The recent price recovery has not triggered meaningful short
covering, suggesting that speculative conviction remains intact. Positioning offers latent upside risk, but
absent a structural catalyst, funds appear comfortable maintaining exposure.

So What? Wheat’s structure has grown heavier even as flat price improved. The expansion deeper into
carry reinforces comfortable coverage, though still short of full storage economics. Export flow has
strengthened modestly, and global pricing has stabilized, but neither development has tightened the
balance sheet meaningfully. With funds still meaningfully short and spreads rewarding storage, the
market remains rangebound. Until a material production shock or export disruption emerges, wheat is
likely to trade within carry-defined boundaries rather than trend decisively higher.

improved to approximately +92H, supported by strong export inspections and slight freight friction tied to
draft constraints along the Illinois River system. Interior river bids firmed toward +24H in St. Louis,
reflecting continued export execution and limited farmer selling. Midwest ethanol plant bids remained
stable, reinforcing steady grind demand. While flat price drifted lower on the board, the physical market
tone was constructive. The nearby cash market signals orderly movement rather than liquidation
pressure, with export demand continuing to underpin basis strength.

Soybeans: Soybean basis softened slightly at the Gulf as Brazilian harvest momentum increased. CIF
Gulf values eased toward +95H, reflecting steady but less urgent export demand relative to prior weeks.
Interior processor bids across the Midwest slipped modestly, indicating adequate nearby coverage amid
strong January crush but rising South American competition. River logistics remain normalized, reducing
transportation friction. The cash market remains supported by domestic processing, yet lacks the
tightening tone seen earlier in the month. Basis stability persists, though the directional bias has softened
marginally.

Wheat: Wheat cash markets were broadly steady. CIF Gulf SRW basis held near +65H, while HRW basis
across the Plains remained anchored near -20H. Pacific Northwest values were unchanged, reflecting
routine export coverage rather than incremental demand acceleration. Despite modest improvement in
futures, basis has not strengthened meaningfully, underscoring comfortable nearby availability. The
widening calendar spreads reinforce the view that inventory coverage is adequate. Physical wheat
markets continue to signal sufficiency rather than scarcity.

So What? Cash markets continue to trade with greater stability than flat price suggests. Corn basis has
firmed alongside strong export flow, soybeans show mild softening as Brazil accelerates harvest, and
wheat remains structurally comfortable. Logistics are functioning efficiently, and no corridor signals stress.
Physical markets remain balanced, with corn showing relative strength, soybeans steady but capped, and
wheat reflecting ample supply.

weather escalation in Argentina, a logistics hiccup in Brazil, or a shift in export competitiveness, but it

So What? The complex is getting more uneven. Corn is the only leg clearly outperforming on execution
(2.01 MMT inspections), yet funds are still leaning short. Soybeans keep leaning on crush for a floor, but
weekly inspections cooled again, which reinforces the seasonal Brazil cap. Wheat flow improved, but
carry rebuilt and basis stayed calm, a combination that usually reads as “stabilization,” not “tightening.

Weather & Crop Conditions

United States
U.S. weather remains largely neutral from a production standpoint at this stage of the calendar. The
USDA national Crop Progress report remains on seasonal pause, with full updates resuming in April,
leaving the market to rely on state-level updates and winterkill monitoring. Snow cover across key HRW
wheat areas in Kansas, Oklahoma, and Colorado remains adequate, limiting immediate freeze risk
despite intermittent cold. NOAA’s 6–10 and 8–14 day outlooks continue to favor above-normal
temperatures across much of the central U.S., reducing logistical friction along river systems and allowing
freight conditions to function more consistently relative to earlier winter disruptions. With winter wheat still
in dormancy and no active fieldwork underway, U.S. weather remains an operational variable rather than
a balance-sheet catalyst.

Brazil
Brazil’s weather continues to reinforce production expectations, though logistics are beginning to feel the
pressure of record volume. National soybean harvest has advanced to roughly 25 percent complete, with
Mato Grosso leading near 47 percent and reporting strong early yields that sustain aggregate production
expectations near 178–180 million metric tons. Soil moisture remains favorable for safrinha corn
establishment across the Center-West, preserving the second-crop outlook. However, frequent rainfall in
Mato Grosso and Pará has narrowed loading windows along the Arco Norte corridor, extending barge
wait times along the Tapajós system. In the South and Southeast, heavy rainfall in Paraná and Santa
Catarina has introduced localized flood risk, potentially slowing loading operations at Paranaguá and
Santos. Logistics remain functional, but the system is stretched, and the risk has shifted from theoretical
to operational as export flows accelerate.

Argentina
Argentina has moved from monitored stress to measurable deterioration. Soybean conditions have fallen

to 40 percent rated Good/Excellent, down sharply in recent weeks, with the Rosario Grain Exchange
projecting production near 48 million metric tons and warning that losses in the most affected zones could
reach 20–60 percent. Corn conditions stand at 44 percent Good/Excellent, prompting the Buenos Aires
Grain Exchange to reduce its production estimate to 57 million metric tons amid persistent dryness and
thermal stress. Rainfall coverage has been uneven and insufficient to fully stabilize second-crop
soybeans entering grain fill, while late-planted corn remains highly sensitive to short-term precipitation.
Unlike Brazil, where weather variability has not altered aggregate expectations, Argentina’s production
profile is actively being trimmed, keeping downside supply risk elevated.

Global
Outside South America, global weather remains largely stable. Winter wheat across Russia, Ukraine, and
the European Union remains dormant under broadly normal conditions with no significant winterkill threat
reported. Black Sea logistics continue without disruption, and no secondary exporter has introduced
meaningful supply risk. As a result, global weather volatility remains geographically concentrated rather
than systemic.

So What?
Weather risk is no longer evenly distributed. Brazil continues to support global supply with strong harvest
progress despite tightening logistics, the United States remains neutral during dormancy, and Argentina
has become the primary volatility node with declining crop ratings and downward revisions underway.
Unless Argentine conditions deteriorate further or Brazilian port congestion materially disrupts export flow,
weather remains a developing driver rather than a confirmed balance-sheet shock, but the asymmetry
has increased meaningfully compared to prior weeks.

Synthesis & Outlook

The defining feature of this week was divergence beneath stable flat price. Corn finished modestly higher
with March near $4.27, soybeans edged lower with March near $11.34, and Chicago SRW wheat firmed
to roughly $5.63, yet the more important signal came from structure and flow rather than the closes
themselves. Calendar spreads across the complex widened week over week and remain in carry: corn’s
March–May spread sits near an 11-cent carry (roughly 72 percent of full carry), soybeans near a 16-cent
carry (about 70 percent), and wheat near a 10-cent carry (about 61 percent). That structure continues to
reflect adequate coverage and limited urgency, even as cash and export execution send more nuanced
signals underneath the board.

Corn continues to behave like a market in equilibrium, but this week the export channel did more of the
heavy lifting. Inspections surged to about 2.01 MMT for the week ending February 19, reinforcing a
shipment pace that remains comfortably ahead of last year and aligned with USDA’s forecast. Gulf basis
firmed toward +92H and interior river bids strengthened modestly, suggesting that physical demand
remains intact even as the curve stays comfortably in carry. Funds remain net short near 42,000
contracts, keeping a defensive speculative overlay in place, but the combination of stronger inspections
and firmer cash tone helped prevent the board from slipping despite a market structure that still pays
storage.

Soybeans remain bifurcated between domestic strength and global weight. January NOPA crush at

Wheat remains the heaviest leg of the complex despite this week’s modest recovery. Export inspections
improved to roughly 0.54 MMT, but the market’s structure moved deeper into carry, with the March–May
spread widening toward 10 cents. That combination, firmer futures alongside a heavier carry, reads as
stabilization rather than tightening. Black Sea pricing remains the global anchor, with Russian 12.5
percent FOB values near $235/MT continuing to cap U.S. competitiveness into price-sensitive
destinations. Basis was steady to slightly softer across Gulf and interior markets, consistent with
adequate coverage rather than urgency. Managed money remains meaningfully net short near 69,500
contracts, creating latent upside risk through short covering, but that risk still requires a catalyst beyond
routine weekly flow.

Weather risk is increasingly asymmetrical. Brazil remains abundant but operationally stretched as harvest
advances and export systems absorb large volumes, while U.S. winter conditions remain neutral during
dormancy and logistics have improved relative to earlier winter constraints. Argentina, however, has re-
entered focus with renewed moisture stress during critical reproductive stages, and crop conditions have
deteriorated relative to earlier weeks. For now, the market is acknowledging that risk without fully
repricing it into balance sheets, but the distribution of weather risk has shifted meaningfully compared
with mid-February.

Bullish triggers: Upside momentum would likely require confirmation that Argentine deterioration is
balance-sheet relevant rather than localized, particularly if soybean production slips materially below
current estimates or corn losses expand beyond the latest trims. A move from “stretched” to disruptive in
Brazilian export logistics could tighten the near-term pipeline and force a short-term repricing of
availability. Additional support could come from continued strength in U.S. corn export execution, an
acceleration in ethanol grind beyond recent levels, or any disruption in Black Sea logistics that shifts
wheat competitiveness and triggers short covering in a structurally short market.

Bearish triggers: Downside pressure would build under a smooth, uninterrupted Brazilian harvest and
export program that reinforces South America as the seasonal pricing ceiling for soybeans and limits U.S.
participation into Asia. A meaningful stabilization in Argentine rainfall and crop conditions would deflate
the weather premium that is beginning to re-enter the narrative. Domestically, softening U.S. interior basis
would signal weaker nearby demand, while the complex remains vulnerable to renewed pressure if
spreads continue to widen and commercial coverage stays comfortable. In wheat, persistent Black Sea
competitiveness and the absence of a supply shock would keep structure rewarding storage and continue
to cap rallies.

In summary: the grain complex remains structurally comfortable but increasingly uneven beneath the
surface. Corn is outperforming on execution and cash tone even as the curve stays in carry, soybeans
remain supported by domestic processing but capped by Brazil’s export dominance, and wheat remains
rangebound under heavy global availability despite a modest rebound. Until supply is materially disrupted
or demand accelerates in a measurable way, price discovery is likely to remain governed by carry
economics, cash-market signals, and incremental flow rather than conviction-driven trend.
