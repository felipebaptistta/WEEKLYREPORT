Corn Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: December 2025 corn consolidated after the late-September break, settling at $4.22
on Oct 8 (+6¢ w/w). The USDA Grain Stocks shock (larger-than-expected Sep 1 stocks, published Sep
30) kept a bearish overhang, but follow-through selling faded as the market marked time into WASDE.
The Dec ’25–Mar ’26 spread held near +17¢ carry on Oct 8, roughly two-thirds of full carry on seven
percent interest + $0.05/bu/month storage/insurance, continuing to reward hedge-and-store programs.
Realized vol eased from the post-report spike as ranges tightened.

Pre-WASDE Expectations: Consensus centers on a U.S. corn yield of 184.5-185.0 bpa and production
of 16.5-16.6 bbu, with 2025/26 U.S. ending stocks at 2.2 bbu. On the global balance sheet, world corn
ending stocks are 281-283 MMT. For South America benchmarks prior to this window, USDA
(September) predicted the Brazilian corn crop at 131.0 MMT and forecast Argentinian production at 53.0
MMT, while CONAB’s most recent projection expected Brazilian corn production to total 139.7 MMT.

Funds & Positioning: With CFTC’s Commitments of Traders report suspended during the shutdown, the
CFTC published the last report available on Sep 26 covering positions as of Sep 23, showed managed
money net short 95,000 corn contracts, with commercials largely on the other side. Open interest rose
into month-end, consistent with fresh shorting after the stocks surprise; the heavy speculative short keeps
the market sensitive to any bullish nudge.

U.S. Cash & Basis: Harvest pressure plus river logistics kept basis soft in the barge markets. Illinois
River bids commonly 35–45¢ under Dec; Mid-South/Lower Mississippi locations 70–80¢ under, reflecting
draft limits and barge surcharges that persisted through this week (late-September quotes around
$19.53/ton, roughly 53¢ per bushel, remained indicative). CIF Gulf eased accordingly, weighing on export
elevators. By contrast, ethanol plants and Plains feeders held comparatively firmer bids (many near parity
but quite a few bids just under the Dec contract), pulling grain away from export channels. Nationally, the
corn basis index averaged 40¢ under Dec, consistent with seasonal harvest pressure but not yet
signaling distress.

Export Flows: With USDA’s weekly updates paused, the last official prints guide the view. The FAS
Export Sales report for the week ended Sep 18 and published on Sep 25 totaled 1.92 MMT, including
0.88 MMT to Mexico. FGIS export inspections (week ended Sep 26; published Sep 29) were 1.53 MMT;
FGIS operations continued into the week ended Oct 2, indicating ongoing loadings even as barge costs
stayed high. Pre-blackout cadence, led by Mexico and Japan, helped keep Gulf values nominally viable
despite competitively priced supplies from Brazil.

International Trade: Brazil remained the low-cost anchor; FOB Santos near $205/MT ($5.20/bu
equivalent) preserved a $25–30/MT edge over typical U.S. Gulf offers into Asia. Argentina briefly
undercut both during its late-September tax holiday (Up-River $200–205/MT) before the window closed
and offers firmed. China stayed largely absent from US books; domestic coverage and alternative origins
filled nearby needs. In the futures market, Dalian Jan corn held a premium to import parity, but

policy/biotech constraints continued to limit US access.

So What? Corn remains storage-driven: wide carries and weak river-linked basis encourage commercials
to own time, while interior demand (ethanol/feed) quietly supports floor values near $4.22. The heavy
speculative short and steady pre-blackout export cadence (Mexico/Japan) argue for range-bound trade
into WASDE, with direction hinging on the potential US yield update and any relief in Mississippi logistics.
However, if the government does not reopen, traders will continue to rely on anecdotal reports for yield
estimates. The increase in uncertainty due to the information gap during the shutdown means that
speculative traders are unlikely to make large, directional bets until USDA releases its next assessment of
US production.

Soybean Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: November 2025 soybeans recovered modestly from the late-September drop,
ending $10.29/bu on Oct 8. The curve stayed wide, with Nov ’25–Jan ’26 at a 19¢ carry, about three-
quarters of full carry based on the following assumptions, seven percent interest + $0.05/bu/month
storage cost. The wide but steady spreads are consistent with ample nearby supply and limited urgency
from exporters.

Pre-WASDE Expectations: Consensus looks for a U.S. soybean yield of 53.1 to 53.2 bpa (down slightly
from prior estimates), production of 4.26 to 4.27 bbu, and 2025/26 US ending stocks of 0.290 bbu. On the
global balance sheet, traders expect USDA to report new-crop world soybean ending stocks at 124 MMT.
In the September WASDE, USDA’s analysts predicted the Brazilian crop at 175.0 MMT and Argentinian
production at 48.5 MMT, while CONAB’s latest estimate prior to Oct 8 has Brazil at 177.6 MMT.

Products & Crush: Board crush margins improved slightly to $1.485/bu as of October 8, supported by
steadier meal and a firmer soybean oil prices after September weakness. The Dec CME board crush
contract dropped from a recent high of more than $2.25 in early August to almost $1.40/bu in late
September. Positive crush economics helped keep processor bids comparatively firmer than river-
exposed elevators and maintained a stable domestic offtake into early October.

Funds & Positioning: With the Commitments of Traders reporting suspended during the shutdown, the
last available report (as of Sep 23; published Sep 26) showed managed money near flat to slightly short (-
29,000 contracts) after covering shorts into month-end. Commercial hedging remained aligned with a
carry structure; speculative participation was light into the report week.

Cash & Basis: The barge market weighed on river basis levels with CIF Gulf premiums of +70–100¢ for
Nov, down from roughly +140¢ a month earlier amid elevated barge costs and harvest inflows. Midwest
elevators trimmed bids as farmer movement increased, while processors stayed firmer relative to futures
given positive crush economics; Eastern Corn Belt crushers continued to pay +50–70¢ vs. Nov.
Nationally, an average basis near 78¢ under Nov sits among the weaker early-harvest prints in recent
years and is consistent with the low water levels and constrained export pull.

Trade Flows: The last official USDA prints before the blackout showed export sales during the week
ended Sep 18 of 0.72 MMT (led by Egypt, Taiwan, Mexico) and export inspections for the week ended
Sep 26 at 0.59 MMT. China remained largely absent from the US market as heavy late-September buying
from Argentina during its short-lived zero-duty window diverted demand just as the US harvest
accelerated.

International Competition: Argentina’s temporary suspension of export taxes triggered Chinese bargain
hunting, allowing buyers to secure up to 2.1 MMT of Argentine soybeans at steep discounts. After the

window closed, Argentina’s advantage faded, but Brazil remained the global price setter: FOB Santos
$440-470/MT ($10.20/bu) versus US Gulf $495/MT, while Argentine Up-River normalized near $480/MT
after the tax holiday ended. That pricing stack kept US offers at a disadvantage into Asia even as
crushers supported domestic demand.

So What? Currently the soybean market is harvest- and storage-driven: a wide nearby carry and weak
barge markets encourage commercials to own time, while positive crush margins keep processors
bidding and provide a domestic floor near $10.30/bu. Export headwinds persist given Brazil’s FOB
advantage and September’s Argentine sales to China; near-term price discovery hinges on realized US
yields, the continuity of central-Brazil planting rains, and any sign of Chinese buying returning to US
origin.

Wheat Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: The Dec wheat contract traded quietly during the week ending Oct 8 with prices
remaining close to $5.00, consolidating after the late-September decline. The Dec/Mar spread held near
an 18¢ carry on Oct 8, roughly 80 percent of full carry under a 7 percent interest + $0.05/bu/month
assumption, consistent with abundant nearby supply and a market still rewarding storage. Implied
volatility stayed firm but orderly as participants awaited new balance sheet guidance.

Pre-WASDE Expectations: Trade consensus anticipates USDA will predict US 2025/26 ending-stocks at
875 million bushels, with world wheat ending stocks near 265-266 MMT. Traders do not expect a
significant yield revision, though analysts will watch for adjustments to exports and feed use after the
increased carry in suggested in the September Grain Stocks report. Internationally, attention centers on
whether USDA analysts trim the Russian export forecast, currently near 51 MMT, or adjusts Australian
output following recent dryness.

Global Supply & Demand: The tone remains shaped by USDA’s Grain Stocks and Small Grains – 2025
Summary assessments. Sep 1 U.S. wheat stocks of 2.12 bbu and 2025 production of 1.985 bbu
confirmed a larger-than-expected supply base. Abroad, Russian exporters continue to ship grain at a
record pace, EU and Black Sea output are ample, and Argentinian new crop, estimated near 21 MM,
adds further weight to global availability heading into 2025/26.

Cash & Basis: Physical markets mirrored the board’s weakness. US FOB SRW near $250/MT, HRW
11.5 percent, near $270/MT, while Russian 12.5 percent protein wheat held near $230/MT FOB Black
Sea, keeping a lid on US competitiveness. Gulf basis stayed under pressure as exporters struggled to
generate fresh demand, and domestic mill bids softened in line with ample stocks.

Trade Flows: With USDA weekly series paused, the last official readings guide sentiment. US export
sales during the week ended Sep 18 totaled 0.54 MMT, while inspections through week ended Oct 2 left
cumulative shipments modestly above last year’s depressed pace but still insufficient to absorb the higher
carry-in. Russia’s continued dominance in world tenders and steady EU participation keep the US as a
residual supplier.

Speculative Positioning: With CFTC Commitments of Traders report suspended during the shutdown,
the last available report published on Sep 26 showed managed money heavily net short. As of Sep 23,
the CFTC estimated the net fund position short 98,000 contracts of SRW and 43,000 HRW contracts. The
entrenched short bias has capped rallies, but its size leaves the market exposed to abrupt short covering
– if a trigger appears.

So What? The wheat market remains well supplied, rewarding storage. Ample domestic and global
inventories, persistent Black Sea competition, and wide carries continue to define trade. Futures appear
locked in the low-$5 range until demand improves or a weather or geopolitical shock alters export flows.
Near-term volatility will likely stem more from positioning shifts than from any immediate tightening in
fundamentals.

U.S. Cash Market & Basis

Corn: Harvest and logistics continued to weigh on corn basis across river markets. Illinois River bids
ranged from 35¢ to 45¢ under December, while Lower Mississippi levels slipped to 70–80¢ under,
reflecting persistent draft limits and elevated barge tariffs that carried over from late September. CIF Gulf
premiums eased modestly, and export elevators reported slower spot movement as high freight costs
erased much of the nominal advantage from lower board prices. In contrast, ethanol plants and feeders
kept bids comparatively firm, with several processors paying near parity to December futures amid strong
margins and seasonal grind demand. Nationally, the corn basis index averaged roughly 41¢ under
December, consistent with harvest-season pressure but not yet signaling distress.

Soybeans: Soybean basis remained under pressure where river freight dominates. CIF Gulf bids
hovered near +50–75¢ vs. November, down from roughly +140¢ a month earlier, while Midwest elevators
trimmed bids to manage heavy harvest inflows. Processors maintained stronger premiums, particularly in
the Eastern Corn Belt, where crushers continued to bid as much at -151¢ under the November contract
(in the southeastern market) as ample supplies and recovering crush margins sustained domestic buying
interest. Nationally, basis levels near 79¢ under November are among the weakest early-harvest readings
in recent years, consistent with a futures curve near full carry and subdued export pull following the
Argentine sales surge in late September.

Wheat: Cash wheat markets softened alongside futures as exporters struggled to generate fresh
demand. HRW 11.5% FOB Gulf hovered near $270/MT, while SRW offers slipped toward $250/MT,
undercut by Russian 12.5% protein wheat near $230/MT FOB Black Sea. Basis levels at the Gulf
remained weak, reflecting both the larger-than-expected carry-in and competitive global offers from
Russia, France, and Ukraine. Domestic mill bids in the southern Plains stayed steady but offered little
counterweight to soft export margins.

So What? Cash markets across all major grains continue to reflect a bifurcated structure: export corridors
remain the weak link, pressured by costly barge freight and intense global competition, while processors
and inland consumers maintain comparatively firmer bids supported by strong crush and ethanol
economics. The wide carry across futures curves reinforces commercial storage behavior, keeping grain
in the interior and limiting Gulf recovery until freight normalizes or export demand materially improves.

International Cash & Trade Flows

Brazil: Brazil remained the global price setter in soybeans and a key low-cost origin for corn. FOB Santos
soybeans were indicated near $440/MT ($11.97/bu), keeping a clear advantage over US Gulf offers
($403/MT) into Asia. FOB Santos corn held around $205/MT ($5.20/bu equivalent), preserving a $25–
30/MT edge versus typical US Gulf corn into long-haul destinations. With Brazil’s old-crop soy program
largely pre-sold, nearby liquidity focused on spot beans and meal/oil parcels; the price leadership
persisted nevertheless, limiting US optionality in Asia and pressuring Gulf differentials despite harvest-
season board weakness.

Argentina: The late-September temporary suspension of export taxes triggered a burst of farmer selling
that allowed China to secure roughly two million tonnes of soybeans at steep discounts. That policy
window has since closed, and Up-River (Rosario) soy FOB normalized near $406/MT, erasing most of the
extreme discount versus Brazil/US For corn, Up-River FOB briefly printed $200–205/MT during the zero-
duty window before firming after the reinstatement of taxes. The quick policy swing diverted near-term
Chinese demand away from US origin just as US harvest accelerated, and while the price advantage
from Argentine supplies faded after the tax-holiday window closed, some of that nearby business will
work through lineups into early Q4.

China: Chinese buyers remain largely absent from the US soybean market instead opportunistically
sourcing soybeans from Argentine suppliers during the tax holiday. On feed grains, Dalian Jan corn near
¥2,143/MT ($7.76/bu) continued to sit above US import parity, but policy/biotech constraints and tariff
structure kept US corn exporters from translating that theoretical advantage into actual flows. The net
effect for early October was a soft US export pull in soybeans driving US shippers continued reliance on
Western Hemisphere demand (Mexico/Japan) to keep US corn loadings moving.

EU / Black Sea: Russian 12.5 percent protein wheat FOB Black Sea hovered in the low $230s/MT,
anchoring global wheat pricing and undercutting US SRW/HRW FOB (roughly $250/MT and $270/MT,
respectively). Competitive EU offers (France/Poland) and steady Black Sea availability kept US wheat
largely residual in discretionary tenders, even as CBOT printed fresh lows. The same pricing stack also
constrained US corn and soymeal into North Africa and the Mediterranean, where Black Sea/EU origins
filled a significant share of nearby demand.

Other Origins: The pre-blackout US corn export cadence remained centered on Mexico and Japan, as
shown by the 1.92 MMT sales total for the week ended Sep 18 (including 0.88 MMT to Mexico) and 1.53
MMT inspections for the week ended Sep 26, with FGIS activity continuing into Oct 2. That pattern helped
Gulf corn stay nominally competitive within the Americas even as Brazil held the edge into Asia. US
soybeans, by contrast, faced sub-0.6 MMT inspections (week ended Sep 26) and a sales slate led by
Egypt/Taiwan/Mexico, reflecting the Argentine diversion and Brazil’s ongoing price leadership.

So What? The global pricing stack this week was unchanged in direction: Russia sets the floor in wheat,
Brazil sets the pace in soy and remains aggressive in corn, and Argentina’s tax-holiday cargoes
temporarily diverted China away from U.S. beans. With U.S. Gulf soybean offers still at a premium to
Brazilian sources in the Asian markets and Black Sea wheat more competitive than US supplies, export
demand remains the weak link for US values even as domestic processors and hemisphere demand
(Mexico/Japan) provide a floor. Until relative FOB spreads narrow, or freight/logistics ease enough to
improve netbacks, US export share in key tenders will lag, keeping basis weaker at the Gulf and
reinforcing the carry-and-store incentives signaled by CME spreads.

Key Fundamentals & Reports (Oct 2 – Oct 9)

government reopens, USDA does not publish its slate of weekly reports (Export Sales, Crop Progress),
and CFTC has suspended Commitments of Traders updates. Historically, brief shutdowns see data being
backfilled quickly; longer ones have resulted in gaps that are not retro released, with agencies
interpolating time series after the fact. The market impact remains more about near-term signal loss than
fundamentals: cash and global price relationships continue to transmit the key information.

stocks: 1.53 bbu (+190 mbu vs. expectations, +12% y/y), soybeans: 316 mbu (below expectations, above

the anchor for pre-WASDE positioning in this blackout window. Small Grains Annual Summary (USDA,
published Sep 30): No new update this week. U.S. wheat production for 2025 at 1.985 bbu (up from 1.927
bbu prior), driven by larger HRW/HRS crops. This continues to frame the wheat market’s supply heavy
bias into October.

Crop Progress (USDA NASS, not published Oct 2–Oct 9): Latest available is week ending Sep 28
(published Sep 29): corn 18 percent harvested (vs. 15 percent 5-yr avg), soybeans 19 percent (vs. 16
percent), ratings steady (corn 66 percent G/E, soy 63 percent G/E). With warm/dry Oct 2–8 conditions,
harvest likely advanced further, but we anchor to the last official print.

Export Inspections (USDA FGIS, fee-funded operations continued): Last consolidated official totals
pre-blackout: week ending Sep 26 (published Sep 29), corn 1.53 MMT, soybeans 0.59 MMT, wheat 0.66
MMT. FGIS operations continued into the week ending Oct 2, indicating ongoing loadings, but we do not
introduce new unaudited totals.

Export Sales (USDA FAS, not published Oct 2–Oct 9): Latest available week ending Sep 18
(published Sep 25), corn 1.92 MMT (incl. 0.88 MMT to Mexico), soybeans 0.72 MMT
(Egypt/Taiwan/Mexico; China absent), wheat 0.54 MMT (Philippines/Italy among buyers). These remain
the reference points for demand into the shutdown.

Ethanol Production & Stocks (EIA, week ending Oct 3): Production increased w/w to 1.07 mbpd, with
stocks down slightly to 22.72 mb; margins remained positive enough to keep processor bids
comparatively firm versus river-exposed bids. For context, the last pre-window print was week ending Sep
27 (reported Oct 1) at 1.024 mbpd production and 23.456 mb stocks.

CFTC Commitments of Traders (publication suspended): Latest available snapshot as of Sep 23
(published Sep 26), managed money net short corn 95k, near flat/slightly short soy (-29k), heavily short
wheat (−98k SRW, −43k HRW). No new official positioning this week.

So What? With USDA weekly’s dark this week, the balance-sheet narrative is unchanged from late
September: corn retains support from Mexico/Japan shipments and steady ethanol runs, soybeans face
persistent export headwinds after Argentina’s late-September sales and Brazil’s FOB advantage, and
wheat remains constrained by Black Sea price leadership. Futures curves advertising carry, plus weak
river basis and relatively firmer processor bids, continue to direct grain toward storage and domestic use
rather than the export channel until either freight normalizes or relative FOB spreads move decisively in
the U.S. favor.

China – Exchange Context (DCE): Market monitoring noted Dalian Jan corn near ¥2,225/MT ($7.88/bu)

Sea. Russia continued to cap world wheat values, undercutting US SRW ($250/MT) and HRW 11.5

Global – IGC / AMIS / FAO Multilateral Balances: AMIS published the October edition of its monthly

signals, reinforcing the abundant supply backdrop.

So What? International reports this week highlighted persistent US export challenges, with Chinese
buyers sourcing soybeans almost exclusively from South America, compounded by the Argentine tax-
holiday, resulting in one of the slowest starts to a US export season that was not a non-drought year. In
addition, Black Sea wheat shipments have also remained more competitive than US sources. While the
wheat market reflects a more traditional competitive landscape, the distortions of Chinese trade flows due
to political tension continues to erode US market share in oilseed trade. The AMIS update added no
major shifts, leaving trade keyed to spot dynamics where US Gulf offers trail competitors into key
destinations. Yet, Brazil's slowing farmer sales and Argentina's aggressive tax policy moves suggest that
cracks may be forming in the pressure and US suppliers could play a larger role in world shipments in the
coming months. Currently, US corn remains competitive into Mexico, but broader share of world trade
flows hinge on South American pacing or Black Sea hiccups, keeping basis weaker at Gulf while and
widening the carry.

United States
Crop Progress & Rainfall: Warm, dry conditions dominated the central U.S. through Oct 8, accelerating
harvest progress and field dry-down. With USDA data suspended during the shutdown, the last official

South America: Early-October rains improved soil moisture across central Brazil, allowing soybean
planting to accelerate after a delayed start, particularly in Mato Grosso and Goiás. Southern Brazil, which
had been excessively wet earlier, turned more balanced, improving conditions for fieldwork. In Argentina,
the Pampas trended drier again after late-September relief; wheat is filling under marginal moisture, and
early corn planting is proceeding slowly while producers wait for more consistent precipitation. The next
ten days will be critical for soil recharge before full soybean sowing begins later in the month.

Global: No major weather deviations were reported in other key origins. Russia and the EU maintained
favorable late-season conditions supporting large crops, while Australia remained dry under El Niño,
trimming wheat yield potential as filling advanced. Ukraine continued to benefit from late-September rains
that improved soil conditions for winter sowing.

So What? In the United States, weather remains harvest-friendly and price-neutral to bearish, adding
supply to the pipeline while leaving river-linked basis under pressure. The focal risk now shifts south:
central Brazil’s rains must stay consistent to sustain planting momentum, while Argentine dryness
threatens early-season crop establishment. Until those patterns diverge meaningfully, the U.S. backdrop
stays supply-heavy, and South American rainfall remains the key wildcard for grain and oilseed price
direction into mid-October.

Synthesis & Outlook

This week underscored the pivot from harvest-friendly US weather and steady demand in corn to acute
export headwinds in soybeans and a still-burdensome global wheat supply. Futures and cash are broadly
aligned: corn is anchored by strong Mexican demand and firm ethanol grind, soybeans are weighed down
by the lack of demand for US exports, and wheat remains capped by competitive Russian sources.
Spreads in all three markets are in carry, but do not reflect burdensome extremes, due to the robust
domestic demand, signaling orderly commercial hedging rather than distressed selling. Basis shows the
same split: exporters under pressure from freight and South American competition, while domestic
processors keep bids firm.

Speculative positioning reflects this mixed setup. Funds trimmed shorts in corn and wheat, reducing
downside momentum, while soybeans saw outright long liquidation as Argentina’s tax holiday flooded the
market with cheap offers. The balance of positioning leaves markets sensitive: with fewer shorts to

squeeze, rallies will need true catalysts, disappointing yield reports, tighter stocks data, or fresh export
demand. Without such triggers, the path of least resistance remains sideways to slightly lower into early
October.

The backdrop going into the Oct 9 WASDE is balanced but fragile: trade consensus calls for corn yield at
184.5-185.0 bpa, soy 53.1-53.2 bpa, and only modest stock adjustments. Base case: December corn
consolidates at $4.22, November soybeans hover near $10.29, and December wheat trades around
$5.05. Carries remain moderate and storage returns positive, favoring elevators holding grain while end
users cover hand-to-mouth.

Bullish triggers: Any sustained Chinese soybean purchase, additional U.S. corn flash sales to Mexico,
or a shift in Russian export policy would prompt a stronger reaction given light liquidity. Improvement in
Mississippi River levels would also firm Gulf basis and narrow spreads, providing incremental board
support.

Bearish triggers: Ideal U.S. harvest weather and yields meeting USDA expectations could push prices
marginally lower. December corn risks testing $4.05, November soybeans $9.90–$9.95, and December
wheat could ease further if Russia continues to discount aggressively. A stronger dollar or a retreat in
crude oil would add weight to biofuel-linked demand.

Watchlist (Oct 9 – Oct 15):

USDA WASDE (Oct 9): Benchmark for new yield and stock guidance; assess whether the agency
confirms or diverges from trade expectations.

Harvest Pace: Monitor field progress into mid-month for logistical and basis implications.

River Logistics: Observe rainfall in the Ohio/Tennessee basins for potential draft relief and impact on
Gulf premiums.

Brazil Planting: Central Brazil rainfall consistency will determine whether early-season momentum is
sustained or delayed.

South American Policy & Black Sea Flows: Watch Argentina’s export rhythm post-tax window and
Russian FOB moves for any shift in global wheat pricing.

In summary: The grain complex enters WASDE week in a neutral-to-cautiously bearish posture. Supplies
are ample but manageable, spreads orderly, and cash flows differentiated by freight and domestic use.
Without a decisive catalyst, weather shock, policy disruption, or data surprise, price direction into mid-
October will likely remain rangebound, with corn and wheat favoring carry capture and soybeans defined
by South American competition and crush economics.
