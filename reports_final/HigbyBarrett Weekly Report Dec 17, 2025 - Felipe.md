Macro / Policy – Dollar Slips, Argentina Deregulates, China Remains Cautious

The US Dollar Index settled near 98.3–98.4 on Dec 17, up modestly on the day but slightly lower on the
week. Crude oil steadied near $60/barrel, offering a mixed backdrop for input and freight costs. The
Brazilian real held near R$5.50/USD. In Argentina, the policy focus this week was on fresh export-duty
reductions announced Dec 9: soybeans to 24 percent (from 26 percent), corn to 8.5 percent (from 9.5
percent), and wheat to 7.5 percent (from 9.5 percent), framed as permanent cuts to improve
competitiveness and accelerate FX inflows into Q1 2026.

In China, tariff suspensions for US corn and wheat technically remain in place, but commercial
engagement has been minimal. Recent soybean purchases are widely believed to be for state reserve
use rather than commercial restocking. Meanwhile, US biofuel policy remains unresolved, as the EPA’s
final 2026 RFS mandate has been postponed to early 2026, extending the window of regulatory ambiguity
for corn ethanol and soybean oil demand.

Weather – Brazil’s North-South Divide Persists as Argentina Holds Steady

Brazil’s north–south split remains the clearest weather signal in the market. Central and northern areas
continued to benefit from timely rains, supporting early soybean and full-season corn development in
Mato Grosso and Goiás. Meanwhile, southern states, particularly Rio Grande do Sul and western Paraná,
remain short of moisture, and crop scouts warn that without meaningful rain in the next 10–14 days, yield
risk rises quickly as later-planted soybeans enter reproductive stages. Argentina remains on firmer footing
with generally solid moisture supporting early corn and soybean development, though forecasts lean drier
for Córdoba and southern Santa Fe and bear watching into January. In the US, winter wheat is settling
into dormancy under mostly stable conditions, with lingering soil-moisture concerns across parts of the
HRW belt but no widespread winterkill threats on the radar. Overall, the market’s near-term weather
sensitivity is increasingly tied to whether southern Brazil can stabilize conditions before January yield
potential starts to firm up.

Corn Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: March corn futures closed at $4.36/bu, down 2¢ w/w, extending a multi-week
consolidation as paper markets reflect comfort while physical flows stay firm. The Mar–May spread
narrowed to 10¢, or roughly 46 percent of full carry, pointing to steady front-end pull from processors and
exporters. Implied volatility held near 12 percent, consistent with a market lacking directional urgency.
Open interest rose modestly, signaling that commercials continue to layer in Q1 hedges without

prompting broader fund activity.

Cash & Basis: CIF Gulf basis held in the low-70s over March (around +72¢), supported by winter
logistics frictions (tow delays/ice risk/holiday scheduling) and tighter spot barge availability, even as river
levels have improved from the fall lows. Interior basis was firmer in pockets, with Western Corn Belt
processors generally ranging from a few cents under to the teens under March. National average basis
sits near 40¢ under March, roughly 20¢ firmer than normal for mid-December and consistent with ongoing
nearby demand.

Funds & Positioning: Positioning turned modestly more supportive in the latest Disaggregated COT
snapshot. Managed money was still slightly net long (+23k) in CBOT corn, and the weekly shift reflected
meaningful short-covering alongside a small add in longs, leaving funds more net-long than the prior
week. Producer/merchant hedging increased on the other side, consistent with steady commercial selling
into carry. Net, flows were supportive, but the market still appears primarily driven by cash signals, basis,
spreads, and demand execution.

Export Sales & Inspections: Export sales data remain the latest available through Nov 20, with corn net
sales at 1.936 MMT. More current flow signals stayed strong, while inspections for the week ending
December reached 1.583 MMT, down from last week but still strong, keeping YTD volumes approximately
69 percent ahead of last season. Mexico, Japan, and Colombia continue to lead, with steady PNW
loadings to South Korea and Taiwan underpinning late-Q4 execution. Despite Brazil’s competitive FOB
offers, the US program into Latin America remains resilient.

So What? Corn sits in a steady stalemate: futures pricing reflects abundant stocks and South American
optimism, while cash markets continue to tell a different story. Strong Gulf basis, active ethanol grind, and
firm export inspections all point to tighter-than-assumed front-end supply. Commercials are gradually
building coverage, while speculative pressure remains muted. With logistics under strain and Argentine
offers still slow to materialize, dips are well-bid and rallies still capped. Until new data emerges, the
market appears anchored in the $4.25–$4.50 range, with spreads remaining the clearest signal of nearby
tightness.

Soybean Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: January soybean futures fell 29¢ this week to settle at $10.66/bu, breaking lower
after two weeks of range-bound trade. The Jan–Mar spread widened to –11¢, or roughly 52 percent of full
carry, showing some softening in front-end tightness while still reflecting firm commercial interest. The
pullback followed improved rainfall across central Brazil and underwhelming US export data, prompting
speculative longs to exit. Even so, the forward curve remains compressed by historical standards for
December, supported by persistent domestic crush demand and slow farmer movement.

Speculators are beginning to unwind long positions built up during recent US–China trade optimism,
particularly after Trump-era rhetoric suggested large Chinese demand that has not materialized.
Improved rainfall in Brazil and CFTC data revealing net longs near decade highs have prompted position
cleanup, reinforcing the complex’s downside bias.

Cash & Basis: CIF Gulf soybean basis was in the low-80s over January (around +82¢), supported by
firm nearby demand and river logistics. Processor bids remained supportive but mixed, stronger in key
Eastern Corn Belt locations (examples near flat to small premiums), while many interior markets still
posted discounts. National average soybean basis was roughly 25–30¢ under January, still firm for mid-
December. Cash is helping stabilize the front end even as export execution lags.

Funds & Positioning: Managed money remains heavily skewed to the long side in soybeans, sitting
roughly +215k net long contracts, little changed on the week. That matters because it keeps the board
liquidation-prone on favorable Brazil weather or soft US export execution, there’s still length in the system
that can be reduced. At the same time, the lack of a big unwind this week suggests the break lower was
more fundamentals-led than flow-driven. Downside pressure can persist if exports stay sluggish and
Brazil improves, while any durable recovery likely needs a fresh catalyst, weather deterioration in South
America or a clearer demand re-acceleration, especially from China

Crush Margins & Domestic Demand: Soymeal continues to do the heavy lifting for crush economics.
NOPA’s November crush printed 216.0 mbu, a new record, consistent with steady soymeal offtake and
strong processor run rates into year-end. Meal demand has kept interior bids supported, while soybean
oil has remained a weaker leg of the complex, limiting the contribution from oil to the board crush. Even
so, crush margins have stayed attractive enough to keep plants pulling hard for nearby coverage, which
continues to underpin soybean basis despite soft export execution.

Export Sales & Inspections: Export sales data remain the latest available through Nov 20, with soybean
net sales at 2.455 MMT, including 1.707 MMT to China (2025/26). Still, total commitments remain 35
percent behind last year’s pace, with new demand heavily concentrated in Mexico and smaller Asian
markets. Inspections for the week ending Dec 11 fell to 0.796 MMT, down 24 percent from the prior week
and 46 percent below the same time last year. China's recent activity appears limited to strategic reserve
purchases, with no evidence yet of broad-based commercial buying. Forward visibility remains murky.

So What? Soybeans are stuck between strong domestic crush dynamics and persistent export drag.
Commercials remain active buyers in the cash market, and crushers are still bidding aggressively. But
without a clear signal of sustained Chinese demand, or meaningful weather-driven supply risk out of
Brazil, futures are likely to stay under pressure. Basis strength and firm spreads continue to anchor the
front end, but for the board to move meaningfully higher, the market will need either a deterioration in
South American yield potential or a broader demand recovery out of China.

Wheat Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: Chicago March SRW futures softened 7¢ on the week to close at $5.23/bu,
retracing part of last week’s modest recovery. Kansas City HRW held steady around $5.07, while
Minneapolis HRS edged marginally higher to $5.63. The Mar–May SRW spread remained stable at –12¢,
or about 55 percent of full carry, indicative of a storage-led market with little urgency on either side. US
futures remain pressured by heavy global supplies and unrelenting Black Sea competition, though recent
signs of improved US execution have helped prevent further breakdown.

Cash & Basis: Wheat basis held steady in mid-December, consistent with a carry-driven market and
limited nearby urgency. Gulf SRW FOB values were in the low-$6s/bu range (roughly $220–$235/MT
equivalent), still carrying a premium to key Black Sea offers and limiting US competitiveness in tenders.
Southern Plains HRW basis firmed slightly on localized feed demand, while PNW soft white bids stayed
supported by steady Southeast Asian interest. National basis averages remain near –60¢ to –72¢ under
March for SRW and –74¢ under KC March for HRW, typical of a surplus environment where movement
stays tactical rather than structural.

Export Sales & Inspections: Export sales data remain the latest available through Nov 20, with all-
wheat net sales at 381,100 MT. Weekly inspections rose to 488,000 MT for the period ending Dec 11, up
23 percent week-over-week and running ahead of the same week last year. Season-to-date inspections
now sit at 14.125 MMT, keeping shipments on a 22 percent year ago advantage. Soft white wheat from

the Pacific Northwest remains the bright spot into Southeast Asia, while HRW movement from the Gulf is
steady. Even so, US FOB offers remain priced above key competitors, keeping the export book tactical
rather than transformational.

Funds & Positioning: Managed money in Chicago SRW wheat remains net short about –44k contracts,
but the week showed a mild de-risking as funds added length and trimmed the bearish edge by roughly
10k. Positioning is still negative, so rallies remain fundamentally capped by global surplus, but the market
is less “one-way” than it would be if shorts were actively building, leaving more room for stabilization and
occasional short-covering pops on export or logistics surprises.

So What? Wheat markets continue to operate under the weight of global surplus. Russian and Argentine
export programs are defining the price ceiling, leaving US wheat to fill niche demand at a relative
premium. While US execution has quietly improved, the lack of sustained momentum and structural
pricing disadvantage remain headwinds. However, the scale of speculative short interest leaves the door
open to short-covering rallies on any shift in global logistics, weather, or currency. Until then, futures are
likely to stay anchored near recent lows, with commercial activity focused on breaks rather than
breakouts.

US Cash Market & Basis

Corn: Corn basis remains resilient for December, with CIF Gulf bids quoted in the low-70s over March
(around +72¢), still historically firm for the season. Ice buildup and winter logistics frictions have slowed
Gulf arrivals at times, lending support to spot premiums. Interior bids remain strongest in the Western
Corn Belt, where ethanol grind continues at near-record levels. Iowa and Nebraska processors were
generally near flat to modestly under the board for spot delivery, as plants compete for limited available
bushels. National basis now averages around –41¢ under March, roughly 20¢ stronger than the five-year
average for this date. Farmer sales remain minimal, particularly in areas with firm local basis and low flat-
price motivation. End-user competition for immediate ownership is keeping the front end tight, even with a
nominal 2.1 bbu carryout on paper.

Soybeans: Soybean basis continues to reflect tight nearby supply, underpinned by record domestic
crush and a reluctant farm seller. CIF Gulf barges for January shipment held steady in the low-80s over
Jan futures (around +82¢), with limited give despite recent board weakness. Processor bids across key
Eastern Corn Belt locations (Decatur, Lafayette, Morristown) were quoted between +65¢ and +75¢ over
Jan for prompt delivery, supported by firm soymeal pull. River basis improved modestly as barge traffic
stabilized, but freight remains a volatile input. National average basis sits near –26¢ under Jan, roughly a
dime firmer than the seasonal norm. While export demand remains soft, crushers are still bidding
aggressively, and the Jan/Mar spread continues to suggest commercial willingness to carry inventory.
Brazilian forward offers remain cheaper into March, keeping a cap on Gulf premiums heading into Q1.

Wheat: Wheat basis remains stable but directionless. Gulf SRW FOB values were in the low-$6s/bu
range (roughly $220–$235/MT equivalent), still carrying a premium to key Black Sea offers and limiting
US competitiveness in tenders. Russian 12.5 percent protein wheat remains anchored near $230/MT,
keeping global benchmarks subdued. HRW basis in the Southern Plains saw marginal strength as feed
users sought alternatives to expensive corn, but broader movement remains muted. In the Pacific
Northwest, soft white bids held firm on continued interest from Southeast Asia. Overall, the US cash
wheat market continues to reflect a carry-driven dynamic: flat prices are too low to incentivize sales, but
basis lacks the momentum to flip the narrative. The USDA’s 900 mbu export forecast remains feasible,
but only if FOB spreads stay defensible through late Q1.

International Cash & Trade Flows

Brazil: Grain exports from Brazil remain seasonally active but are beginning to slow as the old-crop cycle
winds down. ANEC now pegs December soybean exports at 3.57 MMT, roughly 200,000 MT above last
week’s estimate, while corn shipments were nudged up to 6.35 MMT (vs. 6.30 MMT previously), still
elevated relative to historical norms given the size of the safrinha crop. Soymeal loadings were also
raised to 2.0 MMT for December, reinforcing Brazil’s strong protein export program into year-end. FOB
Santos soybean offers were still hovering near $10.05–10.10/bu for January loading, keeping Brazil the
preferred origin for Jan/Feb shipments, while US Gulf values held a $12–15/MT premium. Corn FOB at
Paranaguá traded near $204/MT, preserving a pricing advantage into Southeast Asia and the Middle
East. Logistical flows through southern ports remain fluid, with no significant congestion reported. Recent
rains improved soil profiles in Mato Grosso and Goiás, supporting early soybean development, while
conditions in the south remain mixed, Rio Grande do Sul continues to battle dryness, though safrinha
timing has yet to be compromised. For now, Brazil retains the competitive edge across corn, soybeans,
and soymeal heading into Q1.

Argentina: Argentina's post-election policy overhaul is beginning to reshape its export dynamics. A sharp
peso devaluation and lower export taxes have reset FOB pricing: corn offers fell to $178–182/MT,
soybeans to $428–432/MT. However, volumes remain thin as growers await further currency stabilization.
Farmers are reportedly cautious amid ongoing regulatory recalibration and uncertainty around new fiscal
policy. Domestic crush plants around Rosario continue to draw on pre-election inventories, while planting
progress is solid, soybeans and corn at 59 percent, helped by decent moisture, though recent forecasts
show emerging dryness in Córdoba and central production areas. The wheat harvest is over 60 percent
complete, with local analysts lifting the production estimate toward 27.7 MMT. If logistical and policy
conditions stabilize, Argentina is well positioned to become a more aggressive exporter in early 2026.

China: China remains deliberate and price-sensitive in its grain purchasing, with crushers still favoring
South American origin for early-2026 arrivals given the premium in US Gulf offers versus Brazil. The
latest confirmed US export sales report (still only through Nov 20) included a highlight showing 1.707
MMT of soybeans to China for 2025/26 activity the trade continues to interpret as largely state-directed
rather than broad-based commercial restocking. On corn and wheat, the tariff suspension has not
translated into material new buying, and commercial participation remains minimal. Domestic Dalian corn
values remain elevated, but no sustained arbitrage window has opened. Soymeal futures stayed firm near
¥3,570/MT on livestock feed demand, but until commercial margins improve and buying broadens beyond
tactical coverage, US exporters are unlikely to see sustained Chinese engagement across the grain
complex.

EU / Black Sea: Russian FOB wheat offers held firm near $232–237/MT this week, supported by ruble
stabilization and renewed regional demand. Ukraine's grain corridor continues to function, with Danube
and rail flows moving steadily into the EU. Ukrainian corn offers around $205/MT remain a key pressure
point for EU feed markets. While French wheat (FOB Rouen) held near $229/MT, EU exporters continue
to trail Black Sea pricing in key North African and Middle Eastern tenders. Russian and Ukrainian
soymeal has also made inroads into Mediterranean feed markets, thanks to advantageous freight rates
and softer currency terms.

Other Regions: Australia’s wheat harvest progressed across eastern states, with yields holding steady,
though drought in Western Australia has trimmed national output potential. Total production is expected
below 25 MMT, down sharply from the 2022 record but still a contributor to global supply. Canadian
spring wheat exports continue into Asia and Latin America on a routine basis. India’s grain export
restrictions remain unchanged, and no easing is expected in the near term. South Africa’s summer crop
season is on track, and no significant disruptions have been reported from other minor origins.

So What? Global flows continue to be led by Brazil and Russia, whose aggressive pricing and logistics
networks define the world’s marginal grain supply. Argentina is poised to regain competitiveness, but
farmer behavior and logistics will dictate pace. US exporters remain essential to traditional partners but
lack pricing power in global tenders. Recent Chinese buying does little to change this equation. Without a
major weather or policy catalyst, US export market share will remain opportunistic, not directional. For
now, global FOB spreads continue to anchor world price direction outside the US

Key Fundamentals & Reports (Dec 10 – Dec 17)

Crop Progress: With US harvest fully complete, weekly updates have paused for the season. Winter
wheat condition ratings remain the focus, last reported at 48 percent good/excellent. Dryness persists in
key HRW zones across western Kansas and central Oklahoma. With crops now entering dormancy, the
spotlight shifts to subsoil recharge potential ahead of spring.

Export Inspections (USDA FGIS – week ending Dec 11): Corn inspections held firm at 1.583 MMT,
sustaining a pace well above last year and the five-year average. Cumulative corn exports now exceed
22.5 MMT, tracking comfortably toward USDA’s 3.2 bbu projection. Soybean inspections declined to
0.796 MMT, the lowest in four weeks, reinforcing broader concerns around export slack, even with recent
Chinese flash purchases. Cumulative soybean loadings remain approximately 46 percent behind last
year’s pace. Wheat inspections rose modestly to 488,000 MT, lifting the season-to-date total to 14.124
MMT, or 22 percent ahead of last year, keeping the 900 mbu annual target within reach.

Export Sales (USDA FAS – latest report available through week ending Nov 20): Weekly export
sales reporting remains delayed, so the most recent confirmed figures are still for Nov 20. Corn net sales
were 1.936 MMT, reflecting steady demand led by Mexico, Japan, and “unknown” destinations. Soybean
net sales totaled 2.455 MMT, and while reporting detail has been uneven, the export “highlights” table
flagged 1.707 MMT of soybeans to China for 2025/26, volume the market is still treating cautiously as
potentially tactical/state-directed until the backlog and buyer mix normalize. All-wheat net sales were
381,100 MT, consistent with routine business skewed toward HRW/soft wheat programs.

Ethanol Production & Stocks (EIA – week ending Dec 12): Ethanol production stayed elevated at
1.131 mbpd, reflecting a strong early-December grind. That implies weekly corn use of roughly 100–102
mbu on the high end of seasonal norms. Stocks rose slightly in line with holiday-season builds, while
margins remained positive, underpinned by firm corn basis and steady DDGS values. Processor bids in
Iowa and Nebraska continue to reflect aggressive commercial pull.

CFTC Positioning (latest available Disaggregated COT snapshot): Managed money positioning
remains split across the grain complex. In corn, funds are still slightly net long (about +23k CBOT
contracts), with the latest weekly shift reflecting meaningful short-covering alongside a small add in
longs—supportive flow, but not the primary driver versus cash signals. In soybeans, managed money
remains heavily net long (roughly +215k), which keeps the board liquidation-prone on favorable Brazil
weather or continued soft US export execution; the fact that length did not unwind sharply this week
suggests the break lower was more fundamentals-led than purely flow-driven. In Chicago SRW wheat,
funds remain net short (around –44k), though the week showed mild de-risking as shorts were trimmed
by roughly 10k, leaving positioning less “one-way” and keeping the door open to short-covering pops on
export, logistics, or headline surprises even as the global surplus still caps sustained rallies.

NOPA Crush (November – released Dec 16): November soybean crush reached 216.0 million bushels
a new monthly record and toward the upper end of trade expectations. Soymeal continues to lead
demand, with export shipments holding steady and domestic livestock use remaining robust. Soybean oil

yield and ending stocks were slightly above forecasts, but SBO remains a secondary driver relative to
meal. The strong print reinforces firm processor bids and supports continued strength in front-end
spreads.

So What? Markets continue to lean on real-time commercial signals, with WASDE now in the rearview
and positioning data offering only a partial snapshot of risk appetite. Corn demand remains the standout:
ethanol production and inspections are validating current board levels despite a nominally comfortable
carryout. Soybeans are posting historic crush demand but remain constrained by export
underperformance outside of tactical Chinese reserve buying. Wheat’s shipment pace has been quietly
constructive, helping stabilize the board even as global surplus keeps rallies capped. Until broader clarity
emerges, via South American yield signals and clearer speculative conviction, basis, inspections, and
spreads will remain the market’s most reliable directional guideposts

International Reports

Brazil: Brazil remains the focal point of global grain pricing as the country navigates contrasting weather
patterns and maintains smooth logistics. Soybean and corn FOB offers at Santos and Paranaguá remain
competitive into Asia, with corn quoted near $206–208/MT and soybeans holding a $10.05–10.10/bu
equivalent. Favorable rainfall in central and northern regions has kept early planted soy and corn in solid
shape, but southern states, especially Rio Grande do Sul, continue to show signs of mounting dryness.
CONAB revised its soybean production to 177.1 MMT, noting localized replanting and lower stand counts
in the south. Despite this, export flows are running without disruption, with no significant port congestion
reported. With field progress nearing completion, the market’s next focus is the weather in January as
early reproductive stages begin.

Argentina: The Argentine market is responding quickly to the government’s post-election economic reset.
A steep peso devaluation and reduced export taxes have boosted FOB competitiveness and encouraged
farmer selling. Soybeans are offered around $428–432/MT and corn near $178–182/MT, though volume
remains uneven as producers assess inflation and policy uncertainty. Soybean and corn planting stands
at 59 percent, both tracking slightly ahead of last year. Most of the growing belt still enjoys above-average
moisture, but short-term forecasts have shifted drier for Córdoba and central zones. Domestic crushers
continue operating steadily on pre-election inventories, and Argentina remains a reliable supplier of
soymeal, supported by strong export margins and structural processing capacity.

China: After a wave of US soybean purchases earlier in the month, primarily attributed to state reserves,
commercial interest has cooled again. Flash sales in December totaled over 1.5 MMT, but recent buying
appears sporadic and tactical rather than part of a broader restocking effort. US Gulf soybeans still hold a
premium to Brazilian equivalents, and Chinese crushers are cautious amid thin margins and weak
demand for feed. Soymeal futures have softened in response to government stock releases and rising
inventory. Despite the tariff suspension on US corn and wheat, there has been no meaningful pickup in
commercial activity. For now, China remains active on a case-by-case basis, but not a dependable
growth engine for US exports.

EU / Black Sea: Russia continues to set the tone in global wheat pricing, with steady FOB offers near
$230–232/MT. Ukrainian grain movement faces renewed friction after damage to key infrastructure earlier
in December, pushing more volume through Danube ports and rail corridors. Ukrainian corn is offered
near $218–220/MT, adding pressure to EU feed grain markets. EU internal demand remains well-
supplied, and wheat exports, especially from France, are trailing last year. The region is not lacking in
volume but remains secondary in global trade flows as Black Sea origins retain cost advantage and
logistical access. Unless currency dynamics shift or weather introduces disruptions, the EU’s export share

Global: With few surprises from recent government or private estimates, the global supply picture

Weather & Crop Conditions

United States
With harvest now complete, weather’s role has shifted toward supporting winter wheat establishment and
maintaining logistical fluidity. Crop progress updates have paused for the season, but soil moisture
remains a concern in key HRW areas. Kansas and western Oklahoma continue to report abnormally dry
to moderate drought readings, with patchy emergence in some fields. Adequate recharge between now
and February will be essential to avoid early-season stand degradation.

On the logistics front, Mississippi River levels have continued to improve after October’s lows. Gauges at
Cairo and Memphis ticked higher this week, and most draft restrictions have been lifted. Barge
movements are operating near standard winter capacity, a notable relief for export flow. The 10-day
forecast shows seasonal to slightly above-average temperatures across the Midwest and Plains, with
limited precipitation expected, reducing immediate threats, but also offering little help in rebuilding soil
moisture. Natural gas and diesel markets remain steady, with no energy bottlenecks or polar events
currently threatening fertilizer distribution or crop safety.

Brazil
Weather in Brazil continues to show a pronounced north-south divide. Central and northern zones,
including Mato Grosso, Goiás, and western Bahia, received consistent rainfall last week, further
stabilizing early soy and full-season corn. Stand counts and emergence remain strong, and vegetative
growth is advancing rapidly. The story is very different in the south. Rio Grande do Sul and western
Paraná saw only isolated showers, insufficient to offset deepening moisture deficits. Later-planted
soybeans in these zones are now entering reproductive stages under mounting stress. Crop scouts warn
that without meaningful rainfall in the next ten days, irreversible yield losses could begin. Current
forecasts from INMET and private models maintain a below-normal precipitation outlook across southern
Brazil through late December. Nationwide planting is now nearly complete, shifting market attention fully
to reproductive-stage weather and early yield signals in January–February.

Argentina
Argentina’s crop year continues to perform above expectations. Soybean and corn planting are around 59
percent, both slightly ahead of last year’s pace despite some delays from excess rainfall in Buenos Aires
province. Conditions remain exceptional across the primary growing belt: over 90 percent of the
agricultural area is rated as having adequate to optimal moisture, and early planted corn shows strong
emergence, uniform growth, and minimal pest pressure.

Global
Winter wheat in the Northern Hemisphere entered dormancy under broadly favorable conditions. Russia,
Ukraine, and much of Europe wrapped up fall planting with sufficient moisture and no frost exposure.
Stand establishment across these zones is mostly complete and stable. In Australia, harvest is
concluding in New South Wales and Victoria with average to above-average yields, while Western
Australia continues to struggle with drought-driven yield cuts. National output will fall short of last year’s
record but remains a significant contributor to global balance sheets. India’s rabi crop season is
proceeding on schedule, with no major disruptions reported across other minor exporters such as South
Africa.

So What?
Weather risk is becoming more concentrated and nuanced. Brazil’s Center-West looks increasingly
stable, but southern regions are flirting with active yield stress as dryness deepens. With reproductive
stages underway in key soy zones, the next two weeks will carry heavy directional weight. Argentina
offers a bullish offset, ideal moisture and crop health are still holding, but any drying trend into January
will quickly raise flags. In the US, logistical bottlenecks have eased, and winterkill risk remains low,
supporting basis and interior movement. Global conditions remain benign overall, but South America
holds the cards now, how January weather plays out across southern Brazil and central Argentina will
likely determine the first major inflection point of 2026.

Synthesis & Outlook

Grain and oilseed markets remain rangebound but increasingly shaped by real-time fundamentals rather
than macro signals or speculative flows. Corn futures continue to consolidate around $4.35–$4.40,
soybeans have broken lower into the $10.60s amid Brazilian rainfall and export headwinds, and wheat
has drifted back toward $5.20 despite steady US shipments. The consistent thread is a divergence
between weak futures sentiment and firm cash market behavior. Basis strength, resilient end-user bids,
and historically tight nearby spreads all signal that commercial players are still competing for physical
ownership, even as global supply expectations hover near record levels.

The structure of the futures curve reinforces this tone. Corn’s Mar/May spread narrowed to –10¢ (46
percent of full carry), soybeans held Jan/Mar near –11.25¢ (52 percent of full), and wheat’s Mar/May
remains steady near –12.25¢ (55 percent of carry), all pointing to ongoing spot-market firmness. Export
inspections for corn and wheat remain ahead of seasonal pace, ethanol production continues to post
record or near-record weekly prints, and crush margins are still strong enough to keep soybean processor
bids well-supported into year-end. These cash signals are validating board values, even as the paper
narrative remains one of ample stocks and ideal South American potential.

The central axis for volatility now lies firmly in South America. Brazil’s outlook is split: the Center-West is
performing well, but southern states continue to face yield-threatening dryness just as soybeans
approach key reproductive stages. Argentina, by contrast, is still tracking toward trend or better yields,
with moisture conditions among the best seen in years. Over the next two to three weeks, rainfall, or its
absence, in southern Brazil and parts of Argentina will begin to define market direction into Q1. If
meaningful production losses materialize, especially in southern Brazil’s soy and corn areas, the board
will need to reprice risk. If those risks fade, the weight of supply could begin to assert more pressure on
spreads and basis.

Bullish triggers: Upside risks remain clustered around firm US physical demand: ethanol output remains
near record levels, while crush margins continue to support processor bids. Soymeal strength and slow
farmer selling are sustaining interior basis. If weather in southern Brazil worsens, or Argentina’s current
moisture surplus begins to fade, yield potential may face meaningful cuts. Another wave of Chinese
soybean purchases, or a surprise corn tender post-tariff suspension, would likely pull nearby spreads

tighter and support the front end of the board.

Bearish triggers: The downside setup hinges on confirmation that Brazil and Argentina deliver full
season record crops without material weather losses. If production risk fades, global supply pressure will
reassert control and weigh on US export competitiveness. A seasonal drop in ethanol output, pullback in
Chinese demand, or easing Gulf CIF values could loosen basis and compress spreads. Continued
undercutting from Black Sea wheat and growing soymeal flows from Russia and Ukraine would further
cap US recovery potential.

Watchlist (Dec 18 – Dec 24):

Brazil rainfall maps: focus remains on Rio Grande do Sul, western Paraná

Export inspections: especially soybean shipment conversion from recent sales

Ethanol data: do processors maintain +1.10 mbpd grind into holiday slowdown

Argentina’s mid-season moisture profile: any deterioration from current highs

CIF and processor basis: signal shifts in nearby commercial appetite?

In summary: Markets are being pulled in two directions: cash fundamentals remain resilient, while futures
reflect caution and macro heaviness. South America holds the key to resolving this split. If Brazilian and
Argentine weather cooperate, the weight of supply will reassert control. But if conditions worsen,
especially in Brazil’s south, the board will need to adjust upward to reflect diminished availability. Until
then, spreads and basis will continue to anchor price discovery in the absence of fresh supply shocks.

Ethanol for America Act

The major barrier to a higher Renewable Fuel Standard (RFS) is the fuel versus food debate. Now is the
perfect time to move to E15. Corn prices are extremely low, and the latest USDA WASDE reported a record
crop and a record crop expansion.

USDA WASDE forecast corn production at 16.7 billion bushels, with the yield at 188.8 bushels per acre and
ending stocks at 2.1 billion. The season-average corn price dropped 30 cents to $3.90 a bushel. Most
importantly, 202526 crop year is expected to have 1.8 billion more bushels than 2024/25.

With current ethanol production consuming 5.6 billion bushels, a 10 percent increase in consumption would
consume an additional 560 million bushels. The reality is that year around E15 would add approximately
50 million bushels and regulatory streamlining would add another 50 million bushels. At some point over
the next ten years, the additional corn consumption could be 2.8 billion bushels minus lower fuel
consumption resulting from electric vehicles. The ethanol corn consumption increase is likely to be more
of a S curve as new compacity comes online.

With Brazil and the US corn and soybean production increasing, 100 million bushels of ethanol corn
consumption will barely move the needle. The ethanol production capacity expansion that would be three
to four years from now is when the corn consumption impact to the corn market would be felt.
