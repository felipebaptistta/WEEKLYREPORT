Corn Market Deep Dive
Futures & Spreads: Corn drifted modestly lower through the holiday-shortened week, with March futures
settling near $4.27, down roughly five cents from the prior close. The March–May spread widened slightly
to around a 9.25-cent carry, representing roughly 61 percent of full carry under the Mar–May framework.
While the curve remains in carry, it is not pressing toward maximum storage economics. The structure
continues to reflect adequate supply but steady nearby demand, with no evidence of stress in either
direction. Volatility remained muted and price action contained, reinforcing a market that is consolidating
rather than trending.

Funds & Positioning: Managed Money maintained a net short near 57,493 contracts as of the February
10 CFTC report. The position remains meaningful but far from historical extremes and, importantly, is no
longer expanding. Gross shorts have stabilized while longs have edged modestly higher, suggesting a
defensive posture rather than an aggressive bearish build. With futures failing to break meaningfully lower
and spreads holding their ground, speculative pressure appears controlled. Absent a macro catalyst or
deterioration in export pace, funds appear comfortable maintaining exposure without increasing
conviction.

Cash & Basis: Cash markets held relatively steady. CIF Gulf values remained firm in the $5.26–$5.29
range, while interior river bids showed modest regional variation but no broad softening. Freight
conditions have improved from peak winter disruption, though draft limitations continue to influence barge
efficiency. Basis strength is not accelerating, but neither is it eroding. The nearby physical market remains
stable, signaling continued execution rather than liquidation.

Export Sales & Inspections: Export inspections for the week ending February 12 totaled approximately
1.49 million metric tons, maintaining a shipment pace ahead of last year and broadly consistent with
USDA expectations. Weekly export sales for the week ending February 5 totaled 2,105,500 metric tons,
reinforcing that forward commitments remain active. Cumulative shipments now account for roughly 60
percent of the USDA export forecast, indicating that the program remains on pace despite softer flat price
action. The export channel continues to provide structural support even as futures consolidate.

Global Context: Brazil’s export program remains active, though forward pressure has moderated relative
to peak harvest expectations. Argentine corn production remains stable on official estimates, with no new
material downgrade during the week. Russian and Ukrainian corn offers remain present but not
aggressively undercutting U.S. Gulf values into core destinations. U.S. origin retains a competitive
position into Mexico and select Latin American markets, though Brazil continues to anchor broader global
pricing expectations.

So What? Corn remains structurally stable. The board has softened, but spreads are holding near 60
percent of full carry, basis is steady, and export execution remains intact. Funds are short but not
pressing. Without a supply shock or demand deterioration, the market continues to oscillate within a
range defined more by carry economics than directional conviction. For now, structure remains firmer
than flat price suggests.

Soybean Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: Soybeans softened modestly through the week, with March futures settling near
$11.33, drifting lower in holiday-thinned trade. The March–May spread held near a 16 cent carry,
maintaining a clear contango structure that continues to reflect adequate nearby supply. On a full-carry
framework, the spread sits near 72 percent of full carry, signaling neither tightening stress nor
burdensome pressure. The curve remains stable, and price action suggests consolidation rather than
directional conviction. While flat price lacks urgency, the structure has not weakened materially.

Products & Crush: Crush fundamentals remain the anchor. January NOPA crush totaled 221.6 million
bushels, a record for the month and confirmation that domestic demand remains historically strong.
Soybean oil stocks rose to approximately 1.9 billion pounds, up sharply year over year, tempering
enthusiasm on the product side. The crush margin remains meal-driven, with soybean meal demand
providing the primary support for processing economics. USDA’s emphasis on domestic use as the key
demand pillar continues to be validated by execution data.

Cash & Basis: Cash markets held steady despite softer board tone. CIF Gulf values remained near
$12.36–$12.37, reflecting continued export coverage even as Brazil’s harvest advances. Interior
processor bids remained firm across the Midwest, with limited evidence of aggressive farmer selling.
Basis is not accelerating higher, but it is not retreating either. The nearby market remains supported by
crush pull rather than export urgency, reinforcing the divergence between domestic strength and global
supply weight.

Funds & Positioning: Managed Money maintained a sizable net long near 115,896 contracts as of the

February 10 report, elevated relative to recent historical averages. While not at record extremes, the
positioning profile leaves the market more vulnerable to long liquidation than short covering. Gross long
exposure remains substantial, and absent a fresh bullish catalyst, speculative conviction appears tactical
rather than structural. The positioning tilt is constructive, but extended.

Export Sales & Inspections: Weekly export inspections for the period ending February 12 totaled
1,202,617 metric tons. Cumulative inspections for the 2025/26 marketing year now stand at 24,348,735
metric tons, compared with 36,019,599 metric tons at the same time last year — a decline of roughly 32
percent year over year. While weekly flow remains functional, the broader export program continues to
lag materially behind last season due to Brazil’s dominant early-season presence.

Global Context: Brazil’s 2025/26 soybean production estimate remains near 178 million metric tons,
reinforcing expectations for a large exportable surplus. Harvest progress continues without major
disruption in central regions, and FOB offers remain competitive into Asia. Argentina’s production outlook
remains near 48.5 million metric tons, with localized stress but no material downgrade during the
reporting window. Brazil continues to set the global pricing ceiling, limiting U.S. upside absent a supply
disruption or shift in trade flow timing.

So What? Soybeans remain domestically supported but externally weak. Record crush continues to
anchor the nearby structure, yet cumulative export inspections are running roughly 32 percent behind last
year, underscoring Brazil’s dominance in early-season trade flows. The curve reflects comfortable supply,
and funds remain meaningfully long. Absent a shift in export momentum or a South American production
disruption, upside remains capped while domestic demand prevents structural deterioration.

Wheat Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: Wheat futures firmed through the holiday-shortened week, with March Chicago
SRW settling near $5.47, down roughly fifteen cents on the period. The March–May spread held near a
4.75-cent carry, maintaining a storage-led structure but without tightening. The curve remains comfortably
in contango, reflecting adequate supply and limited urgency from commercial buyers. Price action was
largely technical, with no fresh fundamental catalyst emerging. Kansas City and Minneapolis contracts
followed a similar tone, offering little structural divergence within the complex.

Cash & Basis: Cash markets remained broadly stable. CIF Gulf SRW values held near $6.23–$6.28 per
bushel, while interior basis levels showed regional variation but no meaningful tightening. Plains HRW
basis remained steady despite localized dryness concerns, and Pacific Northwest values were largely
unchanged. The absence of basis acceleration reinforces the view that end users remain covered. The
physical market continues to signal sufficiency rather than scarcity.

Export Sales & Inspections: Weekly export inspections for the period ending February 12 totaled
approximately 0.38 million metric tons, a moderate weekly pace. Export sales for the week ending
February 5 reached roughly 488,000 metric tons, consistent with routine demand but not indicative of
acceleration. Cumulative shipments remain aligned with USDA projections and modestly ahead of last
year’s pace, yet volumes remain insufficient to materially tighten the balance sheet. The export program
is functioning, but it lacks momentum capable of shifting broader sentiment.

Global Context: Global supply remains the defining feature. Russian wheat continues to anchor world
pricing, with export offers remaining competitive into North Africa and the Middle East. Ukrainian
shipments remain active through established corridors, and European origin has re-entered select

tenders where freight allows. Argentine supply remains available, adding incremental pressure. With no
logistical disruption or policy constraint emerging across major exporters, global availability continues to
cap rallies and limit speculative rebalancing.

Funds & Positioning: Managed Money maintains a substantial net short near 85,832 contracts as of
the February 10 CFTC report. While positioning is not expanding aggressively, it remains historically
large. Short covering has been limited, suggesting that funds remain comfortable leaning defensively in
the absence of a catalyst. The short profile provides latent upside risk, but without a supply disruption or
export acceleration, that positioning alone has not been sufficient to trigger sustained covering.

So What? February reaffirmed wheat as the loosest balance sheet within the grain complex. Domestic
stocks remain elevated, global inventories are comfortable, and structure continues to reward storage
over urgency. Export demand is steady but insufficient to tighten conditions, and cash markets show little
stress. Without disruption in a major exporting region or a material shift in global demand, wheat is likely
to remain rangebound, driven more by positioning and spread behavior than by fundamental tightening.

near $5.26–$5.29 per bushel, reflecting continued export coverage. Interior river bids showed modest
regional variation but no broad deterioration. Freight conditions have improved from peak winter
disruption, though draft limitations still affect barge efficiency. Ethanol plant bids across the Midwest
remained firm, signaling consistent grind demand and limited farmer selling. The physical market remains
balanced, with no evidence of forced movement.

Soybeans: Soybean basis continues to reflect strong domestic crush pull. CIF Gulf values held near
$12.36–$12.37, indicating steady export coverage even as Brazil’s harvest advances. Interior processor
bids across key Midwest regions remained competitive, reinforcing spot demand. River logistics have
normalized relative to prior constraints, reducing transportation friction without materially loosening basis.
While the board softened, cash markets continue to reflect structural domestic demand.

Wheat: Wheat cash markets were largely unchanged. CIF Gulf SRW values remained near $6.23–$6.28
per bushel, while HRW basis across the Plains showed limited movement. Pacific Northwest bids were
steady, and interior markets lacked directional energy. With no export acceleration or logistical disruption,
basis remains stable but unresponsive. U.S. wheat continues to face competitive global pressure, limiting
tightening in nearby structure.

So What? Physical markets remain steadier than futures suggest. Corn basis reflects export execution
and ethanol demand, soybeans remain anchored by crush, and wheat signals sufficiency rather than
scarcity. Logistics have improved but have not altered structure. Absent a material shift in demand or
supply, cash markets are likely to remain balanced as futures consolidate.

International Cash & Trade Flows

Brazil: Brazil continues to anchor global trade flows through strong execution and competitive pricing.
ANEC projects robust soybean shipments through early 2026 as harvest advances. FOB offers out of
Santos and Paranaguá remain competitive into Asia, sustaining export momentum. Corn exports remain
supported by late-season availability and active vessel lineups across major ports. Brazil’s export
program remains the dominant structural force in global oilseed and coarse grain flow.

Argentina: Argentina remains the primary supply-side uncertainty. Soybean and late-planted corn

conditions show vulnerability amid uneven rainfall and soil moisture deficits, heightening sensitivity to
February precipitation during reproductive stages. FOB offers have adjusted modestly and remain
competitive where available. Export flow continues but lacks the scale to challenge Brazil’s dominance
absent a weather-driven disruption.

China: China’s import posture remains selective. Routine U.S. soybean cargoes have moved, but no
large new purchase announcements emerged during the window. Brazilian origin continues to be favored
due to pricing advantages and abundant supply. Domestic corn price support tempers incremental import
demand, while downstream feed margins remain pressured. China remains tactical rather than
expansionary.

EU / Black Sea: Black Sea origin continues to set the tone for wheat pricing. Russian 12.5 percent
protein wheat remains competitively offered on FOB terms, pressuring U.S. origin into price-sensitive
tenders. Ukrainian corn remains available through established corridors, while EU wheat export pace
trails last year. Lower-cost Black Sea supply remains the primary outlet for global wheat trade.

Other Regions: Australia continues to move wheat into Asian markets following a solid harvest, while
Canadian spring wheat exports proceed in line with seasonal norms. No material trade restrictions, quota
shifts, or policy disruptions have emerged across secondary exporters. The broader international
environment remains stable and well supplied.

So What? Global trade flows continue to reflect price leadership rather than disruption. Brazil dominates
soybeans and remains competitive in corn, Russia and Ukraine anchor wheat supply, and Argentina’s
role remains weather-dependent. China’s demand remains opportunistic. U.S. competitiveness is timing-
driven rather than structural, and absent supply shocks or narrowing FOB spreads, global flows are likely
to remain competitive rather than restrictive.

the program ahead of the pace required to meet USDA’s projection. Soybean inspections reached
1,202,617 metric tons, with cumulative shipments at 24,348,735 metric tons versus 36,019,599 metric
tons last year, a decline of roughly 32 percent year over year, reflecting Brazil’s dominant early-season
export window. Wheat inspections totaled approximately 381,000 metric tons, with marketing-year
shipments modestly ahead of last season and broadly aligned with USDA expectations, though not at a
pace sufficient to materially tighten the balance sheet.

Export Sales (USDA FAS – latest through week ending Feb 5): Weekly export sales for the week
ending February 5 reflected continued forward activity. Corn net sales totaled 2,105,500 metric tons,
marking a strong weekly commitment. Soybean net sales totaled 377,600 metric tons, while wheat net
sales reached 498,000 metric tons. While soybean bookings remain moderate relative to prior seasonal
peaks, corn commitments continue to run firmly. No major daily flash sales were reported during the
window. Due to the Washington’s Birthday Federal holiday, the next U.S. Export Sales report will be
released Friday, February 20, 2026.

barrels per day (down from 1.11), while stocks remained high at 25.25 million barrels. This indicates a

demand upside.

confirmation of historically strong domestic processing demand. Soybean oil stocks increased to
approximately 1.90 billion pounds, reflecting ample product availability even as crush margins remain
meal driven. The data reinforces domestic demand as the primary support within the soybean balance
sheet.

CFTC Positioning: As of February 10 (released February 13), Managed Money held a net short of
57,493 contracts in corn, reflecting stabilization rather than renewed bearish expansion. In soybeans,
funds maintained a sizable net long of 115,896 contracts, elevated relative to recent averages. Chicago
SRW wheat held a net short of 85,832 contracts, remaining the most structurally bearish position across
the grain complex. Positioning remains influential but is no longer accelerating in any single direction.

So What? Core U.S. fundamentals remain stable. Corn exports and ethanol grind continue to validate
steady demand. Soybeans are anchored by record crush despite competitive global supply. Wheat
execution remains routine but overshadowed by ample global availability. Speculative positioning remains
meaningful but is no longer expanding aggressively. Beneath the board’s consolidation, physical
execution remains intact as mid-February progresses.

Weather & Crop Conditions

United States
Weather remains a secondary driver for US grain markets, though short-term patterns have been
operationally supportive. Temperatures across much of the Midwest and Plains have trended near to
slightly above seasonal norms, limiting freeze-related logistics disruptions and allowing river systems to
function more consistently following earlier winter constraints. Snow cover across key HRW wheat areas
in Kansas, Oklahoma, and Colorado remains adequate, reducing immediate winterkill risk despite
intermittent cold shots. NOAA’s 6–10 and 8–14 day outlooks continue to favor moderation rather than
extreme cold, reinforcing dormancy stability for winter wheat and limiting headline-driven volatility. With
harvest complete and no active fieldwork underway, US weather remains neutral from a supply
standpoint, functioning primarily as a logistics variable rather than a production catalyst at this stage of
the calendar.

Brazil
Brazil’s weather profile remains broadly favorable for aggregate production. Central and northern regions
continue to receive adequate rainfall, supporting soybean development and sustaining expectations for a
large national crop near CONAB’s 177.98 million metric ton estimate. Harvest progress continues, though
fieldwork remains uneven in localized areas where rainfall has slowed operations. In southern Brazil,
moisture levels remain elevated in parts of Paraná and Rio Grande do Sul, contributing to localized
variability and increased disease pressure. However, no new national yield revision emerged during the
window, and export logistics continue to function normally. For now, Brazil’s weather remains a variability
story rather than a disruption narrative.

Argentina
Argentina’s crop profile remains the most weather-sensitive element globally. Soil moisture deficits persist
across key soybean and corn regions, and crop ratings remain below seasonal norms. However, during
the reporting window, no material downward revision to official production estimates was issued, with
soybeans near 48.5 million metric tons and corn near 57 million metric tons. Rainfall coverage remained
uneven, offering limited relief without reversing broader stress. With crops advancing through
reproductive stages, yield sensitivity remains elevated, but the market has not materially repriced supply
risk during the week. Weather risk remains present, though not intensifying.

Global
Outside South America, global weather risks remain limited. Winter wheat across Russia, Ukraine, and
the European Union remains in dormancy under generally stable conditions, with no new winterkill threats
reported. Black Sea logistics continue without disruption. Australia and Canada report no new production
concerns. The broader global weather profile remains stable.

So What?
Weather risk remains concentrated but contained. Argentina continues to face structural moisture stress,
while southern Brazil exhibits localized variability. Central Brazil remains productive, and U.S. conditions
remain neutral. At this stage, weather is being monitored rather than repriced. Until either Argentina sees

a measurable production downgrade or Brazilian harvest disruptions materially impact export flow,
weather remains a background variable rather than a directional catalyst.

Synthesis & Outlook

Futures softened through the holiday-shortened week, with corn settling at $4.27 per bushel, soybeans at
$11.33, and Chicago SRW wheat at $5.47. While flat price drifted lower, the underlying structure
remained stable. The March–May corn spread held near a 9-cent carry (60 percent of full carry),
soybeans near a 16 cent carry (72 percent of full carry), and wheat near a 4½–5 cent carry, confirming
adequate supply without structural stress. CIF Gulf and interior basis levels remained steady across all
three complexes. Managed money positioning remains meaningful, approximately –57,500 corn,
+115,900 soybeans, and –85,800 Chicago wheat as of February 10, but is no longer accelerating. The
divergence between flat price softness and steady physical execution remains intact.

Corn continues to reflect balance rather than stress. Export inspections totaled 1.49 million metric tons for
the week ending February 12, ethanol production held near 1.08 million barrels per day, and spreads are
holding near 60 percent of full carry. CIF Gulf values remain near $5.26–$5.29, and interior basis has
stabilized rather than strengthened. Futures remain capped by comfortable ending stocks, yet the front
end continues to resist disorderly widening. The market appears content trading carry economics while
awaiting a catalyst.

Soybeans remain structurally anchored by domestic crush. January NOPA crush totaled 221.6 million
bushels, a monthly record, reinforcing USDA’s emphasis on domestic use. Funds hold a sizable net long
near 115,900 contracts, introducing potential vulnerability to liquidation but also reflecting constructive
demand sentiment. Brazil’s soybean production estimate remains near 177.98 million metric tons,
keeping global supply expectations heavy, while Argentina’s production remains near 48.5 million metric
tons with weather risk present but not escalating. The structure remains firm at roughly 60 percent of full
carry, but flat price lacks urgency absent a shift in trade flow or supply outlook.

Wheat remains the most structurally heavy component of the complex. Export inspections totaled 0.38
million metric tons, consistent but not dynamic. Global supply continues to dictate pricing, with Russian
12.5 percent protein wheat offered near $229 per metric ton FOB. Spreads remain in carry near 5 cents,
basis is stable near $6.23–$6.28 CIF Gulf, and speculative positioning remains heavily short near 85,800
contracts without forced covering. Until global availability tightens or a weather event materializes, wheat
is likely to remain rangebound with rallies constrained.

Bullish triggers: Upside risk would likely require a measurable production downgrade in Argentina,
particularly if soybean output falls materially below 48.5 million metric tons or corn below 57 million metric
tons, or if harvest disruptions in southern Brazil begin to slow export execution. Additional support could
emerge from a sustained acceleration in U.S. corn export pace above the current 1.49 million metric ton
weekly trend, further strength in ethanol production above 1.08 million barrels per day, or meaningful
short covering in wheat triggered by a global supply disruption.

Bearish triggers: Downside pressure would build under a smooth Brazilian harvest with uninterrupted
export flow near 177.98 million metric tons, combined with stabilization or improvement in Argentine crop
conditions. Softening U.S. basis across interior corridors would signal weaker physical demand, while
long liquidation in soybeans, given the extended 115,900 contract net long, could amplify volatility.
Continued Black Sea pricing dominance near $229 per metric ton FOB would further cap wheat rallies
and reinforce the heavy global supply narrative.

Positioning: Fund positioning shifts in corn and wheat

In summary: Corn remains supported by export execution and steady ethanol demand, with cumulative
shipments running 44 percent ahead of last year. Soybeans rely primarily on record domestic crush to
offset a materially weaker export program that is currently 32 percent behind last season. Wheat
continues to trade under the weight of ample global availability and persistent Black Sea competitiveness.
None of the complexes exhibit tightening stress, and until either demand accelerates or supply is
disrupted, price discovery is likely to remain governed by carry economics and incremental flow rather
than conviction-driven movement.

Ethanol for America Act

The major barrier to a higher Renewable Fuel Standard (RFS) is the fuel versus food debate. Now is the
perfect time to move to E15. Corn prices are extremely low, and the latest USDA WASDE reported a record
crop and a record crop expansion.

USDA WASDE forecast corn production at 16.7 billion bushels, with the yield at 188.8 bushels per acre and
ending stocks at 2.1 billion. The season-average corn price dropped 30 cents to $3.90 a bushel. Most
importantly, 202526 crop year is expected to have 1.8 billion more bushels than 2024/25.

With current ethanol production consuming 5.6 billion bushels, a 10 percent increase in consumption would
consume an additional 560 million bushels. The reality is that year around E15 would add approximately
50 million bushels and regulatory streamlining would add another 50 million bushels. At some point over
the next ten years, the additional corn consumption could be 2.8 billion bushels minus lower fuel
consumption resulting from electric vehicles. The ethanol corn consumption increase is likely to be more
of a S curve as new compacity comes online.

With Brazil and the US corn and soybean production increasing, 100 million bushels of ethanol corn
consumption will barely move the needle. The ethanol production capacity expansion that would be three
to four years from now is when the corn consumption impact to the corn market would be felt.
