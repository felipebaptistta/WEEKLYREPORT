Executive Summary

Corn – Board Softens, Carry Incentivizes Storage
December corn eased to about $4.13/bu on Oct 15 (–2 1/4¢
w/w) as large yields and fast harvest kept pressure on the
board. The Dec/Mar carry widened to 16¢ (75 percent of full
carry), rewarding commercial ownership and confirming
ample supply. River basis stayed under stress from extreme
barge surcharges, while ethanol and feed bids in the interior
held comparatively firm. Weekly inspections (week ended
Oct 10) were 1.13 MMT, led by Mexico and Colombia;
export pace +65 percent y/y.

Soybeans – Weak Tone as Exports Lag

November soybeans slipped 15¢ to $10.07 (–1.5 percent), giving back last week’s modest gains. The

expectations, though soymeal basis stayed firm.

open interest rose on fresh shorts. Export inspections (week ended Oct 10) 0.444 MMT, –19 percent w/w

Corn Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: December 2025 corn futures slipped to $4.13/bu on Oct 15 (–2 1/4¢, –0.5 percent
w/w), consolidating as harvest pressure intensified. The Dec ’25–Mar ’26 spread held near +16¢ carry (75
percent of full carry, using seven percent interest +$0.05/bu/mo storage/insurance). With the government
shutdown ongoing, traders shifted focus to harvest progress and freight logistics, sidelining USDA data
surprises. Implied volatility eased in mid-October, balancing ample supply against firm domestic demand.
Commercials continued to leverage carry hedges, while speculative shorts showed no aggressive
covering.

Funds & Positioning: No CFTC data is available for the week ended Oct 8 due to the ongoing
shutdown. Open interest rose modestly (+12k contracts) on commercial hedging and roll activity.
Speculators reduced exposure rather than extending shorts, reflecting stable fundamentals and no fresh
bearish catalysts.

U.S. Cash & Basis: Harvest-driven pressure and logistics friction persisted in river-linked markets. Illinois
River bids hovered 40–45¢ under Dec; Lower Mississippi locations near 75–80¢ under as barge drafts
remained restricted and tariffs exceeded $19/ton (Cairo-Memphis at $19.53/ton). CIF Gulf values
softened slightly (5¢/bu w/w), while interior processors and ethanol bids held firmer, many near parity to
the board in Iowa and Nebraska. Ethanol margins stayed positive, with EIA reporting production at 1.071
mbpd for the week ended Oct 11 (+0.4 percent w/w). National basis averaged ~30¢ under Dec,
consistent with harvest-season norms.

Export Flows: With the shutdown ongoing, FAS Export Inspections for the week ended Oct 10 totaled
1.13 MMT, up four percent w/w and 65 percent above last year. Mexico (0.49 MMT) and Colombia were
top destinations; Japan and South Korea bookings remained modest. No USDA Export Sales data
published for week ended Oct 10. Despite solid volume, freight and basis costs limited Gulf
competitiveness versus Brazil.

International Trade: Brazilian FOB Santos corn values averaged $206.50/MT ($5.24/bu equivalent),
maintaining a $25–30/MT edge over U.S. Gulf offers into Asia. Argentina’s Up-River FOBs stabilized
around $196/MT after September’s zero-duty holiday ended, keeping a narrow but competitive price

advantage. China remained absent from U.S. books; Dalian Jan corn traded ¥2,371/MT ($7.90/bu),
above import parity due to biotech and quota restrictions. B3 Brazil corn (Nov) was R$67.88 ($12.38), up
w/w, reflecting steady domestic logistics and firm export pace

So What? Corn pricing remains storage driven. Full carries and weak river basis steer grain into elevator
ownership and hedge-and-store strategies. Interior demand from feed and ethanol plants cushions the
downside, but U.S. exports face freight and price handicaps against Brazil. With funds lightening shorts
and harvest past midpoint, board values are likely to stabilize near $4.10–$4.25 until river levels improve
or Brazil’s FOB advantage narrows.

Soybean Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: November 2025 soybeans eased to $10.07/bu on Oct 15 (–15¢ w/w, –1.5 percent),
as harvest progress and weak export demand widened the curve. The Nov ’25–Jan ’26 spread reached
18¢ carry (100 percent of full carry, assuming seven percent interest + $0.05/bu/mo), underscoring
abundant nearby supply and slow off-take. Implied volatility remained subdued with the government
shutdown ongoing, with traders awaiting fresh USDA data to re-establish directional positions. Crush-
linked buying by eastern processors prevented a deeper break despite sluggish river movement.

Products & Crush: Domestic processing margins stayed positive, though narrower than early October
highs. NOPA’s September crush (published Oct 15) hit 197.86 mbu, exceeding expectations and
surpassing last year’s figure. Soymeal premiums in the eastern Corn Belt strengthened $5–10/short ton
w/w, reflecting steady feed demand, while soybean oil weakness (BOZ5 50.83¢/lb) trimmed board-crush
value to $1.45/bu. Processors bid aggressively for prompt deliveries; board crush near seasonal
averages keeps utilization high entering Q4.

Funds & Positioning: No CFTC data is available for the week ended Oct 8 due to the ongoing
shutdown. Open interest expanded (+8k contracts) as commercials rebuild coverage. Speculative
participation remained thin, with few pressing shorts as U.S. soybean harvest neared an estimated 67
percent complete (USDA, Sep 29, and NOAA trends amid shutdown) and central Brazil dryness emerged
as a potential upside catalyst.

Cash & Basis: River freight costs dominated physical markets. CIF Gulf bids slipped to +60–70¢ vs Nov
(–10¢ w/w) as barge drafts stayed restricted; freight quotes exceeded $19/ton (Cairo-Memphis at
$19.53/ton). Interior elevators widened basis 5–8¢, while processors in Indiana and Ohio held steady at
+55–70¢ vs Nov. National average basis ~80¢ under Nov remains among the weakest early-harvest
readings in recent years, driven by export bottlenecks rather than surplus stress.

Trade Flows & International Competition: FAS Export Inspections for the week ended Oct 10 were
0.994 MMT (+70 percent w/w, 50 percent below last year’s 2.01 MMT). China remained largely absent;
cargoes cleared for Mexico, Taiwan, and the EU. Brazil retained export leadership with FOB Santos
~$440/MT ($10.05/bu) and strong early-season shipments as planting hit 11 percent progress (Mato
Grosso 19 percent). Argentina’s Up-River offers stabilized near $465/MT ($10.63/bu) after its zero-duty
window closed, maintaining a $25–40/MT edge vs U.S. Gulf ($495/MT). Chinese crushers favored
Brazilian origin despite Mato Grosso dryness, leaving U.S. exporters reliant on Western Hemisphere
demand.

So What? The soybean complex is constrained by ample U.S. supply and export competition, with
domestic crush anchoring floor values. Full carries and weak river basis incentivize commercials to hold
inventory. Attention shifts to Brazil’s weather, continued dryness could tighten global balance sheets and

spark short-covering, but without a shift in Chinese buying, futures are likely to oscillate near $10.00–
$10.20 until export demand or South American weather provides direction.

Wheat Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: Chicago SRW December futures fell 3¢ to $4.99/bu on Oct 15 (–0.65 percent),
marking fresh contract lows as global supplies remained heavy. The Dec ’25–Mar ’26 spread widened
slightly to 16 1/2¢ carry (75 percent of full carry on seven percent interest + $0.05/bu/mo), sustaining
storage incentives and confirming comfortable U.S. pipeline coverage. Implied volatility stayed modest as
traders awaited USDA data resumption amid the ongoing shutdown and monitored Russian export
behavior. HRW and HRS contracts moved in parallel, with Kansas City Dec at $5.60 (–2¢) and
Minneapolis at $6.10 (–4¢).

Global Supply & Demand: Abundant exportable supply continued to weigh on sentiment. Russia
maintained record-large shipments, keeping 12.5 percent protein Black Sea wheat near $230/MT FOB,
while EU values softened to $235/MT FOB Rouen on slow demand. Australia’s crop outlook improved
slightly after early-October rains, while Argentina’s 21 MMT wheat crop remained on track. These
conditions leave the 2025/26 global balance sheet comfortably supplied. In the U.S., 2025 HRW seeding
advanced under mostly dry conditions in Kansas and Oklahoma, raising emergence concerns but not yet
altering yield expectations.

Cash & Basis: U.S. FOB SRW held near $248–252/MT, HRW 11.5 percent protein around $270/MT,
priced above Black Sea offers. Gulf export basis weakened by 5–10¢/bu, while domestic mill bids eased
amid ample carry-in and limited demand. Interior elevators followed corn’s harvest-driven pattern, offering
wide carries but little nearby premium.

Trade Flows: FAS Export Inspections (week ended Oct 10) totaled 0.444 MMT (–19 percent w/w, +17
percent y/y), led by shipments to Indonesia and Japan. No USDA Export Sales data published for week
ended Oct 10. Russia’s aggressive tender wins into Egypt and Turkey sustained downward pressure on
global FOB benchmarks, leaving U.S. exporters largely sidelined.

Speculative Positioning: No CFTC data is available for the week ended Oct 8 due to the ongoing
shutdown. Open interest rose modestly, signaling persistent commercial hedging and limited fund short
covering despite the market’s oversold condition.

So What? Wheat remains locked in a globally competitive market dominated by Black Sea flows. Full
carries, muted volatility, and comfortable stocks keep the U.S. a residual supplier. Domestic basis and
export bids track storage economics over consumption signals. Until Russian exports slow or Southern
Hemisphere weather threatens supply, futures are likely to drift within a $4.90–$5.20 range, with
occasional short-covering spikes offering limited relief.

U.S. Cash Market & Basis

Corn: Harvest and low-river logistics sustained pressure on barge markets. Illinois River bids remained
40–45¢ under Dec, and Lower Mississippi bids 75–80¢ under, with freight quotes above $19/ton (Cairo-
Memphis at $19.53/ton) due to Mississippi River levels lows (6.29 ft at Memphis). CIF Gulf basis softened
5¢/bu w/w, while processor and ethanol bids in the western Corn Belt held near parity amid positive grind
margins. Feed demand across the Plains and Southeast maintained interior firmness, preventing a
deeper national slide. The national corn basis index averaged roughly 30¢ under Dec, aligned with full-
carry storage economics.

Soybeans: River-exposed bids weakened further as barge drafts and surcharges persisted. CIF Gulf bids
at +60–70¢ vs Nov (–10¢ w/w) and Midwest elevators 80¢ under Nov reflect freight dominance in nearby
values. In contrast, eastern crushers stayed firm at +55–70¢ vs Nov, supported by positive board-crush
returns and steady meal offtake. Nationally, basis near 80¢ under Nov remains among the weakest early-
harvest levels of the decade, driven by transportation friction rather than oversupply.

Wheat: Cash wheat tracked futures weakness. HRW 11.5 percent FOB Gulf $270/MT and SRW $248–
252/MT, while Russian 12.5 percent FOB Black Sea held near $230/MT, maintaining a wide export
discount against U.S. origin. Gulf basis eased a few cents as exporters struggled to compete, and interior
mills in the southern Plains showed minimal bid response amid ample domestic stocks.

So What? Across U.S. grains, cash markets remain split: export corridors weaken under high freight
costs and foreign competition, while inland processors and feed users sustain firmer demand. Full-carry
structures reward storage and delay Gulf movement. Until river levels recover or global FOB spreads
narrow, basis recovery will lag futures stabilization, keeping commercials in ownership mode rather than
pushing grain to export channels.

International Cash & Trade Flows

Brazil: Brazil maintained price leadership in both soy and corn as early planting advanced under uneven
rainfall. FOB Santos soybeans averaged $440/MT ($10.05/bu), holding a clear discount to U.S. Gulf near
$495/MT and continuing to dominate Asian demand. Corn FOB Santos $206.50/MT ($5.24/bu equivalent)
preserved a $25–30/MT advantage versus U.S. Gulf into Asia. While old-crop soybean sales are nearly
complete, spot export flow remains steady at 7.84 MMT for October, and exporters are already forward-
selling 2025/26 beans at competitive basis levels. Internal logistics were stable, with BR-163 flows normal
and Paranaguá lineups moderate. The strong FOB competitiveness and smooth throughput kept Brazil
the global benchmark, capping U.S. optionality despite harvest-driven price weakness

Argentina: The export surge from September’s zero-duty holiday has run its course, but its impact
continues to echo. Up-River (Rosario) soy FOB normalized near $465/MT ($10.63/bu) and corn FOB
$196/MT ($4.98/bu) after taxes were reinstated. The earlier policy window pulled forward roughly 2 MMT
of soybeans sold to China, trimming U.S. business through October. With policy normalization and a
stable official peso near 1,362, exports have slowed, but crush and logistics pipelines remain well
supplied. Wheat harvesting is about to start in northern provinces, with expected production near 19.3
MMT, adding further global availability.

China: Chinese crushers remained sidelined from U.S. soybeans, focusing on cheaper South American
origins. Dalian January soybean futures traded near ¥4,750/MT ($12.85/bu), while soymeal (Jan)
¥3,410/MT ($460/ST) slipped on moderate feed demand. Dalian corn Jan at ¥2,371/MT ($7.90/bu)
continued to price above import parity, but quota, biotech, and tariff constraints blocked meaningful U.S.
inflows. October customs data showed strong September arrivals of 12.87 MMT from Brazil and
Argentina, reinforcing the pattern of South American preference.

EU / Black Sea: Russia’s dominance persisted. 12.5 percent protein wheat FOB Black Sea $240/MT,
with continued tender wins into Egypt and Turkey. EU soft wheat offers from France and Poland trailed
slightly higher around $235/MT FOB Rouen, but slow demand kept values defensive. Black Sea
competition limited U.S. export opportunities not only in wheat but also in corn and soymeal into North
Africa and the Mediterranean. EU ports remained active clearing earlier tenders, but new demand signals
were sparse.

Other Origins: U.S. exports during the week ended Oct 10 confirmed steady corn shipments to Mexico
and Colombia, while soybeans to the EU and Mexico partially offset China’s absence. Canadian wheat
exports to Latin America expanded modestly as buyers diversified away from Black Sea dependence.

So What? The global pricing structure remained unchanged: Russia anchors wheat at the floor, Brazil
leads soy and corn pricing, and Argentina’s post-tax-holiday normalization still limits U.S. recovery in
Asia. With Brazil and the Black Sea maintaining clear FOB advantages and freight costs high, U.S.
exporters remain residual suppliers. The imbalance between interior strength and Gulf weakness persists
until relative spreads narrow or logistics cost ease. For now, the global arbitrage continues to reinforce
wide carries and domestic storage incentives, mirroring the board’s neutral tone.

Key Fundamentals & Reports (Oct 9 – Oct 15)

Government Shutdown: The U.S. government shutdown, now in its 15th day as of Oct 15, continues to
pause USDA, CFTC, and other agency publications, with no resolution after the Senate's ninth failed
funding vote. Data gaps from the three-week blackout are expected to be backfilled progressively over
the next two reporting cycles upon resumption. Markets are navigating uncertainty, relying on fee-funded
inspections and anecdotal trends visible in cash and export data.

Crop Progress (USDA NASS – last official Sep 29; estimated for week ended Oct 13 amid
shutdown): Corn harvest is estimated 47 percent complete (vs 42 percent 5-yr avg) and soybeans 67
percent complete (vs 52 percent avg). Crop ratings, based on the final pre-shutdown update (Sep 29),
held steady for corn at 65 percent good/excellent (unchanged w/w) and dipped for soybeans to 61
percent (–1 pt). Winter wheat planting is estimated 64 percent complete (vs 68 percent avg), with
emergence around 41 percent. Warm, dry weather continued to accelerate harvest and field dry-down,
confirming ample supply availability and explaining soft nearby basis conditions.

Export Inspections (USDA FGIS, week ended Oct 10 / published Oct 14): Corn 1.13 MMT (+ four
percent w/w, +65 percent y/y); soybeans 0.994 MMT (+70 percent w/w, –50 percent y/y); wheat 0.444
MMT (–19 percent w/w, +17 percent y/y). Mexico and Colombia led corn loadings; EU and Mexico
accounted for most soybean flow; Indonesia and Japan dominated wheat shipments. Strong Western
Hemisphere corn demand offset Asian competition from Brazil.

Export Sales (USDA FAS, not published Oct 9–Oct 15): The most recent data, covering the week
ending Sep 18 (released Sep 25), indicates corn sales at 1.92 MMT (including 0.88 MMT to Mexico),
soybeans at 0.72 MMT (with Egypt, Taiwan, and Mexico as key buyers; China notably absent), and wheat
at 0.54 MMT (featuring Philippines and Italy among purchasers). These figures continue to anchor
demand assessments during the ongoing data hiatus.

Ethanol Production & Stocks (EIA, week ended Oct 11 / published Oct 15): Production 1.042 mbpd
(+0.4 percent w/w); stocks 23.5 mb (–one percent w/w). Margins remained positive as natural gas and
corn input costs eased slightly. DDGS values held firm at $195/ST FOB Midwest, supporting processor
bids near parity to futures.

CFTC Commitments of Traders: No CFTC Commitments of Traders (COT) data is available for the
week ended October 8, 2025, due to the ongoing government shutdown

So What? With the government shutdown ongoing (Day 15), official USDA data remains absent, leaving
markets without fresh bullish triggers: U.S. harvest pace is estimated ahead of average, export sales

reflect steady Western Hemisphere demand with persistent Asian weakness, and global stocks appear
comfortable. CONAB’s dryness warning in central Brazil hints at weather risk, but without production cuts,
wide carries and soft basis dominate trade. The lack of USDA data, including the delayed CFTC report,
keeps sentiment uncertain ahead of USDA’s next WASDE report, which at this point will likely not be
published until the November edition is due.

International Reports:

Brazil – CONAB Crop Outlook: CONAB’s October bulletin (published Oct 10) confirmed 2025/26
soybean production at 177.6 MMT (–0.2 MMT m/m) and corn at 139.7 MMT (+0.32 MMT m/m). The
agency noted “irregular rainfall delays” in Mato Grosso and Goiás, with planting progress near 11 percent

recovery if mid-October rain returns. Local cash soy prices held near R$137/sc ($11.30/bu) and corn near

Brazil – ANEC Export Lineups: ANEC projections (Oct 1–11) place October soybean exports at 7.84
MMT, up sharply from 4.5 MMT a year earlier, and corn exports at 6.46 MMT (+16 percent y/y). The
strong lineups sustain Brazil’s dominance in Q4 export flows even as U.S. harvest peaks. FOB Santos
soybeans at $440/MT and corn $206.50/MT kept Brazil the cheapest long-haul origin into Asia. Nearby
lineups were led by COFCO, Bunge, and Amaggi, indicating continued Chinese preference for Brazilian
origin despite emerging dryness concerns.

Argentina – Government Policy and Market Activity: Argentina’s markets transitioned smoothly after
the late-September zero-duty window expired. Up-River (Rosario) soy FOB stabilized near $465/MT
($10.63/bu) and corn $196/MT ($4.98/bu) after taxes were reinstated. The Ministry of Agriculture reported

November lineups. The official peso 1,362/USD held steady, and exporters resumed operations under the
normal 33 percent soy export tax regime. BCR maintained its 19.3 MMT wheat output projection,
highlighting a strong exportable surplus into late 2025.

China – Exchange Context (DCE): The Dalian Jan soybean contract near ¥4,750/MT ($12.85/bu) and
corn near ¥2,371/MT ($7.90/bu) indicate comfortable domestic coverage but no incentive for new U.S.
purchases. Imports during September totaled 12.87 MMT, dominated by Brazilian cargoes. State reserve
activity was quiet, and crushers continued drawing down South American stocks. MARA’s mid-October
feed-price survey showed steady inclusion rates, suggesting stable demand but no new buying urgency
from China’s major crushers.

Black Sea – Public FOB Indications (Russia 12.5 percent): Russian 12.5 percent protein wheat rose
slightly to $240/MT FOB Black Sea, maintaining a record export pace. Russia’s Agriculture Ministry
confirmed cumulative 2025/26 exports above 21 MMT (+12 percent y/y) as of Oct 12, with October tender
wins into Egypt and Turkey sustaining heavy outflows. Ukrainian offers remained slightly higher ($245/MT
FOB), while EU ports (Rouen, Gdynia) mirrored softness around $235/MT, reflecting ample stocks and
sluggish demand. This structure keeps U.S. HRW and SRW as residual suppliers into MENA markets.

Global – IGC / AMIS / FAO Multilateral Balances: IGC’s October update (published Oct 10) raised
world wheat output +2 MMT to 800 MMT and corn +1 MMT to 1,224 MMT, while AMIS kept global soy
production unchanged at 401 MMT. FAO’s Food Price Index (Oct 4) showed vegetable oils +1.3 percent

m/m, grains –1.2 percent, reinforcing the benign global supply backdrop. None of these reports altered
trade sentiment materially, but they validated the continued wide carry and storage incentive visible
across exchanges.

So What? International agencies and regional releases underscored a consistent theme: abundant global
supply, soft export margins, and limited upside catalysts. Brazil’s strong October shipments kept it the
global price setter, Argentina’s tax normalization restored equilibrium, and Russia’s record wheat exports
continue to anchor world grain prices. While central Brazil dryness bears watching, it has yet to translate
into volume loss. The return of official reporting refocuses attention on fundamentals, revealing that global
markets remain well supplied and U.S. competitiveness constrained.

Weather & Crop Conditions

United States
Crop Progress & Rainfall: Harvest conditions remained ideal through mid-October under warm, dry
weather. With the USDA’s Crop Progress reports suspended amid the government shutdown, unofficial
estimates place corn harvest near 47 percent complete (vs 42 percent 5-yr avg) and soybeans around 67
percent (vs 52 percent avg) as of Oct 13. The western Corn Belt remained driest, with only scattered
showers across Iowa, Nebraska, and Kansas, sustaining concerns over subsoil depletion and stalk
lodging. In the Plains, light rainfall in central Kansas and Oklahoma modestly improved topsoil moisture
but was insufficient for uniform HRW emergence. Mississippi River stages fell to seasonal lows (6.29 ft at
Memphis and Baton Rouge), maintaining navigation restrictions and elevated barge surcharges.

Temperatures: Above-normal readings dominated the Corn Belt and Plains, averaging 3-6°F above
climatology. The absence of frost risk preserved late-maturing soybeans and allowed rapid natural dry-
down, while minimal humidity facilitated uninterrupted harvest runs. The downside was continued
moisture drawdown, particularly in the western Corn Belt and lower Mississippi basin, reinforcing freight
and basis stress.

Outlook (6–10 days): NOAA’s forecast (issued Oct 14) projects a warm/dry window through Oct 22,
favoring final harvest progress across the Midwest. Limited rainfall is expected in the Ohio and
Tennessee basins, potentially easing river levels slightly but not enough for full recovery. In the Plains, a
weak frontal system may deliver light to moderate showers by Oct 18–19, aiding HRW establishment if
realized.

South America: After early October relief, rainfall in Mato Grosso and Goiás tapered sharply, with total
precipitation at 40–60 percent of normal across the central belt. Southern states (Paraná, Rio Grande do
Sul) remained adequately moist. CONAB reported soybean planting at 11 percent complete (Mato
Grosso 19 percent), two points above last year’s nine percent. Forecast models indicate scattered
showers returning by Oct 20, but persistent dryness could pressure yield potential if it extends beyond
late October.

Argentina: Conditions inverted from September’s pattern: the Pampas and Córdoba regions stayed
moderately wet, supporting wheat fill and early corn establishment. Soil profiles improved slightly, though
producers await consistent rains for full soybean planting. The Rosario Exchange (BCBA) rated 54
percent of wheat in good/excellent condition, steady week-on-week.

Global: Russia and the EU maintained favorable conditions for winter wheat sowing, with no major frost
or moisture deficits reported. Australia faced El Niño-linked dryness in Western and South Australia,
further trimming wheat yield potential as crops reached maturity. Ukraine benefited from late-September–
early-October moisture, improving topsoil ahead of winter wheat planting.

So What? The U.S. weather pattern remains neutral-to-bearish, accelerating supply inflow while
prolonging low-water freight bottlenecks. The main weather risk pivots to central Brazil’s developing

Synthesis & Outlook

The week of Oct 9–15 marked a restrained stretch across grains as markets operated without fresh
government data. With USDA, CFTC, and NASS publications suspended, traders relied on cash signals,
freight quotes, and international benchmarks to gauge direction. Futures and cash remained broadly
aligned: December corn held near $4.13, supported by interior ethanol and feed demand; November
soybeans hovered near $10.07, weighed by weak export pull; and Chicago SRW wheat tested new lows
near $4.98 under heavy global supply pressure. Across all three, spreads maintained moderate carries,
wide enough to reward storage but not indicating distress, while basis continued to divide firm interior bids
from weaker export corridors constrained by river logistics.

With no new CFTC positioning, speculative flows appeared steadily. Fund behavior was inferred from
price action rather than official data, with commercial hedging dominating and volatility muted. The
absence of government releases reduced speculative engagement, keeping trading ranges narrow and
flow driven. Ethanol and crush margins remained positive, providing a domestic floor, while Mississippi
River levels near record lows (6.29 ft at Memphis) kept Gulf logistics under pressure.

Global cues provided the only real direction. Brazil’s October lineups (ANEC: 7.84 MMT soybeans, 6.46
MMT corn) and Argentina’s post–tax-window normalization continued to divert Asian demand away from
the U.S., while Russia’s record wheat exports (21 MMT YTD, +12 percent y/y) anchored global values.
IGC and AMIS early-October updates reaffirmed comfortable world stocks (corn 282 MMT, soy 123 MMT,
wheat 266 MMT), and CONAB’s latest figures, soybeans 177.6 MMT, corn 139.7 MMT, underscored
Brazil’s strong production base despite emerging dryness across Mato Grosso and Goiás. The overall
tone remained one of orderly but heavy supply and limited fresh catalysts.

Bullish triggers: Relief in Mississippi River logistics, any flash Chinese soybean purchases, or a
prolonged dry spell in central Brazil that materially trims yield prospects.

Bearish triggers: Continuation of ideal U.S. harvest weather, absence of new data to unsettle the
narrative, and persistent Brazilian and Black Sea price leadership, keeping downside tests open toward
$4.05 corn, $9.90 soybeans, and $4.85 wheat.

Watchlist (Oct 15 – Oct 21):

Any indications of government reopening and timeline for data resumption.

Central-Brazil rainfall for planting recovery or escalation of dryness risk.

Mississippi River barge rates and depth changes.

Private export flashes or vessel lineups as surrogates for USDA data.

Russia’s wheat tenders and Argentine corn exports for global pricing signals.

In summary: The grain complex remains in a data vacuum, trading on logistics, weather, and
international benchmarks rather than fresh fundamentals. Ample supply, wide carries, and firm domestic
demand define a steady-to-slightly-bearish tone. Until U.S. agencies reopen and official figures return,
markets will continue to move cautiously, sideways to slightly lower, anchored by interior strength and
export constraints.

Ethanol for America Act

The major barrier to a higher Renewable Fuel Standard (RFS) is the fuel versus food debate. Now is the
perfect time to move to E15. Corn prices are extremely low, and the latest USDA WASDE reported a record
crop and a record crop expansion.

USDA WASDE forecast corn production at 16.7 billion bushels, with the yield at 188.8 bushels per acre and
ending stocks at 2.1 billion. The season-average corn price dropped 30 cents to $3.90 a bushel. Most
importantly, 202526 crop year is expected to have 1.8 billion more bushels than 2024/25.

With current ethanol production consuming 5.6 billion bushels, a 10 percent increase in consumption would
consume an additional 560 million bushels. The reality is that year around E15 would add approximately
50 million bushels and regulatory streamlining would add another 50 million bushels. At some point over
the next ten years, the additional corn consumption could be 2.8 billion bushels minus lower fuel
consumption resulting from electric vehicles. The ethanol corn consumption increase is likely to be more
of a S curve as new compacity comes online.

With Brazil and the U.S. corn and soybean production increasing, 100 million bushels of ethanol corn
consumption will barely move the needle. The ethanol production capacity expansion that would be three
to four years from now is when the corn consumption impact to the corn market would be felt.
