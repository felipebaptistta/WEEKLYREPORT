Macro / Policy – Freight Constraints Ease, Biofuel and Trade News Surface

Macro drivers remained secondary to logistics again this week. Ethanol production slipped to roughly 0.96
million barrels per day (week ending January 30), while stocks declined to about 25.14 million barrels,
and blending demand improved modestly to around 890,000 barrels per day, reinforcing stable corn grind
even with a softer weekly print. While energy markets were neutral, market attention shifted to U.S.
Treasury’s proposed biofuel tax credit (45Z), which boosted soyoil and supported soybean futures on
improved renewable diesel margins. On trade, President Trump’s comments around potential trade
discussions kept optimism circulating for agricultural purchases, though no official volumes or
commitments have been confirmed. Freight conditions showed modest improvement as ice cleared along
parts of the Mississippi, but low water levels still limited draft and tow sizes, keeping barge execution
uneven and supporting nearby basis.

Weather – Plains Freeze, South America Dries

In the U.S., the late-January cold snap impacted transportation more than crops. Snow cover across the
Plains remained adequate, minimizing winterkill risk, and no new winter wheat condition updates were
issued. In South America, central and northern Brazil continued receiving favorable rainfall, aiding
soybean development but slowing harvest progress. In the south, conditions have turned more variable,
with recent excess moisture in Paraná and Rio Grande do Sul delaying fieldwork and raising disease
pressure, even as pockets remain uneven in coverage. In Argentina, rainfall remained patchy and
insufficient across major growing areas, leading to further deterioration in crop ratings. With key
reproductive stages underway, February rainfall remains critical, and traders are increasingly weather-
sensitive as Argentine supply risk intensifies.

Corn Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: Corn remained rangebound through the week, with March futures settling near
$4.28, up slightly from the prior week, as trade continued to focus on structure rather than outright
direction. The March–May spread held around minus seven cents, still comfortably in carry but short of
full. On a full-carry framework, that spread is roughly 50 percent of full carry for the Mar–May window. The
curve continues to reflect steady nearby demand without signaling broader balance-sheet stress. Price
action stayed contained and volatility muted, reinforcing the sense that the market is waiting on a clearer
catalyst before committing to a directional move

Funds & Positioning: Speculative positioning continued to ease rather than intensify. Managed Money
trimmed the net short to approximately 72,000 contracts as of January 27, marking a further reduction
from recent highs. While the position remains sizable, the pace of short accumulation has clearly slowed.
With futures unable to extend losses and spreads holding their ground, funds appear increasingly
reluctant to press the downside absent a shift in physical flow or a macro trigger.

Cash & Basis: Cash markets remained firm despite weather-related disruption earlier in the period. CIF
Gulf basis held within a +98 to +105H range, while interior river bids were largely steady, signaling
consistent demand at the destination level. Freight conditions remained the binding constraint, as ice

damage and low-water levels on the Mississippi imposed draft and tow-size restrictions, keeping barge
movement uneven. Basis strength under these conditions continues to reflect execution and logistics
rather than speculative tightening.

Export Sales & Inspections: Export inspections reached 1.12 million metric tons for the week ending
February 1, bringing cumulative shipments to approximately 33.7 million metric tons, or roughly 48
percent ahead of last year. While weekly volumes moderated from the previous peak, the broader
shipment pace remains solid and aligned with USDA’s export expectations. Export sales for the same
reporting week totaled 1.62 million metric tons, with shipments of 1.65 million metric tons, reinforcing the
view that physical flow remains intact even as futures remain subdued.

Global Context: Brazil’s January corn export outlook remains near 3.45 million metric tons, according to
ANEC, reflecting continued late-season shipment strength. In Argentina, crop conditions remain under
pressure due to uneven rainfall, though export offers have yet to adjust meaningfully. Black Sea
availability remains seasonally light, limiting immediate competition into key destinations. U.S. origin
continues to maintain an advantage into Mexico, where execution has remained strong, even as Brazil’s
forward program tempers broader price sensitivity.

So What? The board remains subdued, but the underlying market continues to signal firmness. Spreads
are holding, basis remains supported, and execution is steady. While speculative positioning is still short,
funds are no longer adding aggressively. With logistics acting as a near-term constraint and South
American harvest still developing, the front end of the corn market remains supported, even as flat price
struggles to generate momentum.

Soybean Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: Soybeans staged a modest rally during the week, with March futures settling near
$10.92, up roughly 26–28 cents on the period, as Argentine weather risks intensified and speculative
buying returned. The March–May spread held steady near a 12¼-cent carry, maintaining a contango
structure that continues to reflect adequate nearby supply rather than a breakdown in demand. While the
curve still signals that domestic pull is present but not tightening enough to reprice structure meaningfully,
flat price action gained traction amid rising weather concerns and supportive macro developments.
Trading ranges expanded slightly, reflecting a more directional tone.

Products & Crush: Crush fundamentals remain the stabilizing force within the complex. While no new
NOPA report was released during the window, the market continues to operate against a backdrop of
historically strong crush rates and resilient meal demand. Soybean oil fundamentals remain less
supportive, with ample stocks and policy-related uncertainty continuing to limit upside. The crush margin
remains meal-driven, reinforcing USDA’s emphasis on domestic use as the primary demand pillar for
soybeans in the current balance sheet.

Cash & Basis: Cash markets stayed firm despite softer futures. CIF Gulf basis held in a +116 to +120H
range, signaling that nearby export demand has not fully deteriorated even as Brazil accelerates
shipments. Interior processor bids remained supported across the Midwest, reflecting steady crush
demand and limited farmer selling. River logistics remained uneven due to lingering ice damage and low-
water constraints, reinforcing the firmness in basis despite the lack of price follow-through on the board.

Funds & Positioning: Speculative positioning tilted more bullish. Managed Money expanded its net long
to roughly 17,300 contracts as of January 27, reflecting increased optimism tied to domestic crush
demand and Argentine production risk. Gross exposure remains lighter than prior months, suggesting that
funds are positioning tactically. While not overly aggressive, the profile now leans constructively rather

than neutral.

Export Sales & Inspections: Export inspections improved to 1.31 million metric tons for the week ending
February 1, offering a modest pickup in physical execution. However, cumulative shipments remain
approximately 35 percent below last year, underscoring the ongoing impact of Brazil’s dominant early-
season export program. Export sales for the same week totaled 825,000 metric tons, with shipments of
1.3 million metric tons, signaling decent front-loaded movement but still lagging historical norms. The
export program remains functional but lacks the competitiveness to reshape the broader demand outlook.

Global Context: Brazil’s soybean export pace remained dominant, though ANEC’s January estimate has
been revised down to roughly 3.23 million metric tons following harvest delays. While harvest progress
remains uneven due to persistent rainfall in central and northern regions, logistics have not become
restrictive enough to slow export flow meaningfully. In Argentina, crop conditions deteriorated further amid
uneven rainfall, keeping weather risk elevated and more closely watched by the market. FOB values from
Brazil remain competitive into Asia, maintaining pressure on U.S. origin.

So What? Soybeans remain caught between firm domestic demand and expanding South American
supply. The board reflects caution, spreads remain comfortably in carry, and exports continue to lag.
Cash markets, particularly processors, are still anchoring the front end, while funds have shifted toward a
more balanced stance. Until either Brazil’s export program stumbles or US demand reaccelerates,
soybeans are likely to remain rangebound, with strength expressed more clearly in basis than in flat price.

Wheat Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: Wheat futures posted modest gains during the week, with March Chicago SRW
settling near $5.32, up roughly four cents. The March–May spread tightened slightly to near an 8¾-cent
carry, maintaining a storage-led structure without signaling urgency from end users. Price action
responded briefly to weather-related headlines in the Black Sea but remained largely contained, reflecting
a market still anchored by ample global supply. Kansas City and Minneapolis contracts were
comparatively steady, lending limited support to the complex but failing to alter the broader tone.

Cash & Basis: Cash markets were largely unchanged. FOB Gulf SRW offers held across a wide range,
roughly +65 to +115H, while interior markets showed little movement, indicating adequate nearby
coverage. HRW basis remained steady across the Plains, with no meaningful tightening despite episodic
winter weather concerns. Pacific Northwest values also held flat. Overall, basis stability continues to
underscore a lack of urgency from export buyers, with U.S. wheat still struggling to regain
competitiveness.

Export Sales & Inspections: Weekly export inspections for the period ending February 1 totaled
approximately 0.34 million metric tons, pushing cumulative shipments to around 16.5 million metric tons,
or about 22 percent ahead of last year’s pace. While the weekly volume was routine, year-to-date
performance remains consistent with USDA projections. Export sales for the same reporting week totaled
560,000 metric tons, with shipments of 380,000 metric tons, steady but not dynamic. The export program
continues to function, but without momentum to reshape sentiment meaningfully.

Global Context: Global supply continues to dominate pricing. Russian wheat remains available at
competitive levels, maintaining pressure across major importing regions. Ukrainian shipments through
Danube channels continued without disruption, while European values remained elevated and largely
sidelined from price-sensitive tenders. Argentine wheat offers were steady, adding another layer of low-

cost supply into global channels. With no logistical or policy constraints emerging across major origins,
global availability continues to cap upside potential.

Funds & Positioning: Speculative positioning remains heavily skewed to the short side. Managed
money held a net short near 94,700 contracts as of January 27, still among the largest bearish bets
across the grain complex. While the pace of new short accumulation has slowed, there has been no
significant covering either. Without a clear catalyst, be it weather, freight, or geopolitics, funds appear
comfortable maintaining a defensive stance.

So What? Wheat remains defined by imbalance rather than momentum. Spreads remain in carry, basis is
flat, and global supply continues to dictate trade flows. Despite sizable speculative shorts, the absence of
a structural disruption has limited both downside acceleration and upside opportunity. Until a material shift
emerges, wheat is likely to remain rangebound, with risk skewed toward patience rather than price
discovery.

US Cash Market & Basis

Corn: Corn basis remained firm across key corridors, supported by steady demand and lingering
transportation constraints. CIF Gulf basis held within a +98 to +105H range for nearby positions, with
February values little changed, reflecting continued downstream pull despite uneven execution. Interior
river markets were largely steady, as draft and tow-size restrictions on the Mississippi limited barge
availability but did not materially disrupt coverage. USDA transportation data continued to flag freight
constraints tied to low water and prior ice damage, reinforcing basis resilience even as futures struggled
to gain traction. Interior processor bids held firm across major ethanol regions, underscoring consistent
spot demand and limited farmer selling.

Soybeans: Soybean basis remained supported alongside a stronger board. CIF Gulf basis held at +116
to +120H for nearby loadings, signaling that export channels remain functional even as Brazil accelerates
shipments. Interior markets continued to reflect strong crush-driven demand, with processors maintaining
competitive bids to secure ownership. River logistics remained uneven but showed some improvement,
helping keep basis firm even as barge traffic gradually recovers. The recent board rally, driven by
Argentine weather and speculative buying, has aligned more closely with cash firmness, reinforcing the
role of domestic demand in anchoring the front end.

Wheat: Wheat cash markets were largely unchanged. CIF Gulf basis for SRW held across a broad range,
roughly +65 to +115H, while HRW basis across the Plains showed minimal movement, indicating
adequate nearby supply. Pacific Northwest bids remained steady, and interior values lacked directional
energy. Despite steady basis, U.S. wheat remains challenged on the export front, with global buyers
continuing to favor lower-cost Black Sea supply. The absence of new logistical or policy disruptions has
kept basis stable but uninspired.

So What? Cash markets continue to send a firmer signal than futures. Corn basis remains supported by
strong export execution and steady ethanol demand, soybeans by resilient crush pull and weather-driven
board support, and wheat by stable interior structure rather than export urgency. While some
transportation constraints have begun to ease, low water conditions and logistics frictions still underpin
nearby values. Until interior demand softens meaningfully or global supply disrupts, physical markets are
likely to remain resilient, even as futures reflect broader balance sheet comfort.

International Cash & Trade Flows

Brazil: Brazil continued to dominate near-term global trade flows. ANEC now estimates January soybean
exports near 3.23 million metric tons following downward revisions tied to harvest delays, while corn
shipments remain projected around 3.45–3.5 million metric tons, reflecting continued late-season
availability. FOB soybean offers from Santos remained competitive into Asia, maintaining a clear discount
versus US Gulf equivalents, while corn out of Paranaguá held near $205 per metric ton, sustaining
Brazil’s edge into Mediterranean and select Asian destinations. Vessel lineups across Santos, Barcarena,
and the northern arc remained active, indicating that logistics are scaling alongside harvest, despite
intermittent weather-related delays in central regions.

Argentina: Argentina remains the key source of supply-side uncertainty. Crop conditions continued to
deteriorate, particularly for soybeans and late-planted corn, amid uneven and insufficient rainfall.
Sensitivity to February precipitation outcomes has intensified, as reproductive stages advance. Despite
agronomic stress, FOB offers have only modestly adjusted, with Up-River corn holding near $225 per
metric ton and soymeal offers elevated on tightening meal availability. Farmer selling remains cautious,
influenced by currency factors and policy uncertainty, keeping nearby export flow constrained relative to
capacity.

China: Chinese demand signals remained selective rather than expansive. No major new US grain
purchases were reported during the window, though routine soybean inflows continued. Market indicators
in China showed modest improvement in crush economics, with domestic futures firming slightly, but
downstream feed demand remains pressured by weak livestock margins. State involvement in domestic
corn markets persisted, supporting internal prices but limiting incremental import demand. Overall, China
continues to provide episodic support on breaks rather than sustained demand acceleration.

EU / Black Sea: Black Sea origins continued to set the tone for global wheat pricing. Russian 12.5
percent protein wheat held firm near $229 per metric ton FOB, with January shipment volumes broadly
in line with seasonal norms. Ukrainian corn offers remained available near $235 per metric ton, though
EU feed demand stayed subdued. French wheat values out of Rouen remained elevated near €240 per
metric ton, leaving EU origin priced out of recent tenders. European Union wheat export licenses
remained behind last year’s pace, reinforcing the competitive pressure exerted by lower-cost Black Sea
supply.

So What? Global trade flows remain firmly dictated by price leadership. Brazil continues to anchor
soybean and corn exports, Russia dominates wheat, and Argentina’s role is increasingly contingent on
weather and producer selling behavior. China’s presence provides occasional demand support but lacks
consistency. Against this backdrop, US export competitiveness remains opportunistic rather than
structural, dependent on timing, logistics, or short-term disruptions elsewhere. Until global FOB spreads
narrow or supply risks materially reshape availability, the US remains a secondary origin in the global
trade hierarchy.

totaled 1.12 million metric tons, slightly below the prior week but keeping cumulative shipments near

ethanol continues to provide a steady floor for corn demand into early February.

CFTC Positioning: Managed money positioning continued to unwind. As of January 27, corn’s net short
was reduced to approximately 72,000 contracts, the smallest bearish position since mid-2025. In
soybeans, funds increased their modest net long to around 17,300 contracts, supported by strong
domestic demand and Argentine weather concerns. Chicago SRW wheat remained the most bearish,
with a net short near 94,700 contracts, reflecting persistent global supply pressure.

So What? Core U.S. fundamentals remain constructive beneath the surface. Corn exports continue to run
well ahead of last year, ethanol grind remains steady, and speculative pressure is easing. Wheat
fundamentals are stable but overshadowed by global supply, keeping positioning defensive. Soybeans
remain mixed, with domestic demand offsetting weak exports. Across markets, basis and execution
continue to provide a clearer signal than futures, suggesting that physical markets remain firmer than the
board implies as February begins (no nopa data).

Weather & Crop Conditions

United States
Weather impacts during the period remained more logistical than agronomic. Cold conditions persisted
across parts of the Midwest and Plains in late January, contributing to ice accumulation along inland
waterways and complicating river movement. Snow cover across the Central and Southern Plains
remained generally adequate, limiting winterkill risk for hard red winter wheat, while no new USDA winter
wheat condition updates were released during the window. Precipitation remained limited across much of
the Corn Belt, though no immediate crop stress concerns were identified. River operations continued
under draft and tow-size restrictions tied to low water and prior ice damage, while Gulf loading proceeded
steadily under ongoing limitations.

Brazil
Brazilian weather remained broadly favorable nationally but uneven across regions. Continued rainfall in
central and northern producing areas supported soybean development but further slowed early harvest
progress, particularly in Mato Grosso and Goiás. In contrast, southern Brazil continued to experience
excess moisture, with CONAB reporting increased disease pressure and lodging risks in parts of Paraná
and Rio Grande do Sul, where wet soils delayed fieldwork and clouded yield outlooks. While no major
national yield revision was issued, field-level crop ratings show increasing variability, with southern
conditions now a key factor for second-half supply visibility. So far, weather-related disruptions have not
materially impacted logistics, but regional divergence remains a growing concern.

Argentina
Argentina continued to face challenging conditions. MAGyP’s latest crop bulletin showed a further drop in
good-to-excellent ratings, particularly for soybeans, with only about 19% of the crop now rated G/E, down
from 25% a week earlier. Rainfall coverage remained uneven, offering scattered relief but failing to
reverse widespread moisture deficits. Late-planted corn is also under increasing stress. With crops in
critical reproductive stages, the next two weeks are pivotal for yield retention. Despite the deterioration,
FOB offers have yet to reflect the full extent of production risk, keeping attention focused on whether
exportable surplus will contract meaningfully.

Global
Outside South America, global weather risks remained limited. Winter wheat across Russia, Ukraine, and
the European Union stayed in dormancy under generally adequate snow cover, with no new winterkill
threats reported. Black Sea logistics continued without disruption, and weather conditions across other
major exporting regions, including Australia and Canada, remained stable. No new global production risks
emerged during the reporting window.

So What?
Weather risk is increasingly concentrated in South America. Southern Brazil’s excess rainfall and
Argentina’s entrenched drought have emerged as critical variables for soybean and corn yield potential.
Central Brazil continues to support strong overall production, but the southern impact could shape
March–April availability. In Argentina, the steep decline in crop ratings underscores rising supply risk,
though the market has yet to fully price it in. For the U.S. and other major exporters, weather remains
secondary. As February progresses, yield outcomes in southern Brazil and rainfall patterns in Argentina
will likely set the tone for market direction.

are stable, and spreads continue to resist widening. Futures remain capped, but the front end is well
anchored by execution and downstream demand.

anchor the front end, while the board rallied on Argentine weather risks, strong basis, and rising fund
interest. Exports still lag under the weight of Brazil’s aggressive early-season program, but speculative
momentum and biofuel policy developments have lifted sentiment. The balance between bullish U.S.
fundamentals and bearish global supply keeps structure firm, even as flat price rises

rainfall and Argentina’s intensifying drought are raising production uncertainty, particularly with soybeans
in critical stages. In the U.S., ethanol production remains steady, blending demand is improving, and
basis strength suggests end users remain active. The Treasury’s 45Z biofuel credit proposal has also

Bearish triggers: Global supply remains ample. Brazil’s export program is scaling efficiently, and if
weather conditions stabilize in southern Brazil or meaningful rainfall materializes in Argentina, production
risk could quickly fade. Ethanol production, while stable, has not reaccelerated, and any sustained
softening in grind or basis would challenge corn’s front-end support. In wheat, aggressive Black Sea
pricing continues to limit upside, and without a clear export acceleration, rallies remain vulnerable to
producer selling and speculative pressure.

Watchlist (Feb 05–Feb 12):

Argentina: Do crop conditions stabilize or continue to deteriorate?

Brazil: Does harvest momentum accelerate, particularly in the south?

CIF and interior basis: Early signs of softening versus continued firmness

Ethanol: Can production and blending demand remain stable into February?

China: Any material shift from routine buying to multi-cargo demand

Funds: Further short covering in corn and wheat, or renewed selling pressure?

In summary: The grain complex remains structurally uneven. Futures still suggest theoretical
abundance, but spreads, basis, and physical execution point to localized tightness, particularly in corn
and soybeans. Wheat stays pressured by global supply, despite steady U.S. shipments. Until full carry
emerges or physical demand softens, price discovery will remain shaped more by logistics and cash flow
than by speculative sentiment.

Ethanol for America Act

The major barrier to a higher Renewable Fuel Standard (RFS) is the fuel versus food debate. Now is the
perfect time to move to E15. Corn prices are extremely low, and the latest USDA WASDE reported a record
crop and a record crop expansion.

USDA WASDE forecast corn production at 16.7 billion bushels, with the yield at 188.8 bushels per acre and
ending stocks at 2.1 billion. The season-average corn price dropped 30 cents to $3.90 a bushel. Most
importantly, 202526 crop year is expected to have 1.8 billion more bushels than 2024/25.

With current ethanol production consuming 5.6 billion bushels, a 10 percent increase in consumption would
consume an additional 560 million bushels. The reality is that year around E15 would add approximately
50 million bushels and regulatory streamlining would add another 50 million bushels. At some point over
the next ten years, the additional corn consumption could be 2.8 billion bushels minus lower fuel
consumption resulting from electric vehicles. The ethanol corn consumption increase is likely to be more
of a S curve as new compacity comes online.

With Brazil and the US corn and soybean production increasing, 100 million bushels of ethanol corn
consumption will barely move the needle. The ethanol production capacity expansion that would be three
to four years from now is when the corn consumption impact to the corn market would be felt.
