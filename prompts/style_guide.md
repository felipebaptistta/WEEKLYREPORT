# HigbyBarrett Weekly Report — Style Guide

This guide describes the writing style found in historical HigbyBarrett weekly
commodity market reports. Use it when generating new reports to match the
publication's established voice, structure, and analytical approach.

---

## Tone

**Analytical and confident.** Every paragraph makes a claim and supports it
with data. The writer does not hedge with vague language ("prices were mixed",
"sentiment was cautious"). Instead: name the direction, cite the number, and
explain the cause.

**Narrative, not a list.** Sections are written in full paragraphs that build
on each other. Bullet points appear only in forward-looking watchlists, not in
analysis. The reader should feel they are following a line of argument, not
scanning a fact sheet.

**Institutional in register.** The implied reader is a commodity trader,
merchandiser, or analyst. Technical terms (basis, carry, FOB, MMT, bbu, WASDE,
CFTC managed money) are used without definition. Hedging language is used only
when genuine uncertainty exists.

---

## Report Structure

Reports follow a consistent section order. Not every section appears in every
issue, but the sequence is stable:

1. **Executive Summary** — one tight paragraph per commodity (corn, soybeans,
   wheat). Leads with the most important development of the week. States the
   net price direction and the primary driver. Written to stand alone.

2. **Macro / Policy** — dollar index, crude oil, interest rates, government
   policy (USDA shutdowns, export tax changes). Frames the macro backdrop
   before moving into fundamentals.

3. **Weather** — U.S. crop conditions and harvest pace; South American planting
   progress. Uses USDA Crop Progress data directly. Identifies the key
   regional risk (drought, planting delay, frost).

4. **What's Next / Watchlist** — forward-looking. Lists upcoming reports and
   events (WASDE, Export Sales, Crop Progress) and explains what to watch for
   in each. Written in short, direct sentences.

5. **Futures & Spreads tables** — CME front-month prices with week-over-week
   change, percent change, calendar spreads, and percent of full carry.
   International futures (Dalian, Euronext, B3) included when relevant.

6. **Commodity Deep Dives** — one section per commodity. Covers:
   - Futures & Spreads: price level, weekly move, carry structure, implied vol
   - Funds & Positioning: CFTC managed money net position direction and trend
   - Cash & Basis: interior bids, river markets, processor bids, Gulf CIF
   - Export Flows / Trade: weekly inspections and sales, key destination countries
   - International Competition: Brazil FOB, Argentina FOB, Black Sea FOB prices
   - **So What?** — a closing paragraph that synthesises the section into one
     clear market implication for a commercial operator

7. **U.S. Cash Market & Basis** — cross-commodity basis overview: river markets
   vs. inland processors. Explains the split between export channels and
   domestic demand.

8. **International Cash & Trade Flows** — country-by-country: Brazil, Argentina,
   China, Black Sea, EU, other origins. Each paragraph covers price levels,
   policy context, and trade flow implications.

9. **Key Fundamentals & Reports** — detailed review of government reports
   released during the week (USDA Grain Stocks, WASDE, Crop Progress, Export
   Inspections, Export Sales, EIA Ethanol). Each sub-section names the report,
   states the key figure, and compares it to expectations and last year.

10. **Synthesis & Outlook** — ties all commodities together. States the base
    case (expected price range and market structure), then lists specific
    bullish and bearish triggers. Ends with a one-paragraph summary verdict.

## Analytical Conventions

**Spreads and carry** are always expressed as a percentage of full carry, not
just as a dollar amount. Full carry is calculated at 7% annual interest plus
storage/insurance costs. This gives readers an instant read on whether the
market is rewarding storage or pushing grain to move.

**Basis** is reported as cents over or under the nearby futures contract. The
report distinguishes river/export basis from inland processor basis — these
often move in opposite directions and tell different stories.

**Export data** uses MMT (million metric tons) and bbu (billion bushels) as
appropriate. Weekly inspections and cumulative sales are compared to the same
period last year and to USDA's annual export target.

**Price comparisons** between origins (U.S. Gulf FOB vs. Brazil Santos FOB vs.
Argentina Up-River FOB vs. Russia Black Sea FOB) are central to the export
analysis. The report always explains *why* one origin is winning business.

**"So What?"** is the most important paragraph in each section. It translates
market mechanics into a clear implication for a commercial operator: what
should a merchandiser, elevator, or processor do (or watch for) given this
week's information?

---

## What a New Report Must Do

- Lead each commodity section with the most important fact, not background
- State every price move with its magnitude (e.g. "fell 2.5 percent to $5.09/bu")
- Compare current data to expectations AND to the same period last year
- Name the countries, companies, or policy actions driving trade flows
- End every major section with a "So What?" paragraph
- Close with a Synthesis that gives a base-case price range and names specific
  triggers that would break the range in either direction
- Use current weekly data exclusively — do not import narrative from past reports

## What a New Report Must Not Do

- Copy sentences or phrases from historical reports in the style corpus
- Use vague directional language without a number attached
- Substitute bullet lists for analytical paragraphs in body sections
- Speculate beyond what the data in weekly_data/ supports
- Repeat the same observation in multiple sections without adding new information
