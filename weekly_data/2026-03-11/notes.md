# Analyst Notes — Week of 2026-03-11

## Key Narrative
The March WASDE delivered a broadly bearish message across the grain complex. The market
had already priced in unchanged U.S. corn stocks, but the larger-than-expected world
corn and soybean ending stocks figures — driven by upward revisions to Brazilian production
— gave bears fresh ammunition heading into the weekend.

Funds had already been selling aggressively in corn (now net short 108,400 contracts —
the largest bearish bet since November 2025), yet prices did not collapse after WASDE.
The muted downside response in corn after a bearish report is worth noting: it may
indicate that much of the bearish supply story is already priced in.

## Corn
- Export pace is lagging the annual target, but this week's inspections (1.214 MMT) were
  a meaningful step in the right direction. Mexico continues to be the anchor buyer.
- Gulf basis firmed +4¢ this week — a countertrend move vs. the weaker futures. Watch
  whether this reflects genuine demand or just short-term logistical noise.
- Dec/Mar carry at 76% of full carry is not a green light to store aggressively.

## Soybeans
- Brazil's harvest at 71% complete and running ahead of schedule is the dominant bearish
  overhang. Every week of smooth harvest progress further undermines U.S. export demand.
- The surprise in Nov soybean ending stocks (+10 mbu vs. expectations) combined with the
  world stocks beat (-9¢ reaction in Nov soybeans) confirms the market is in price-
  rationing mode: lower prices are needed to stimulate demand or cut planted area.
- Crush margins are still supportive of domestic processing demand; this is the one
  bullish counterweight to the export headwinds.

## Wheat
- Southern Plains dryness is the one genuine weather risk in the complex right now.
  Kansas topsoil at 41% short/very short is not yet at crisis levels but warrants
  close monitoring through the March/April green-up period.
- Black Sea and EU competition continues to shut U.S. wheat out of traditional markets.
  U.S. Gulf SRW basis at -42¢ under May is already at a significant discount but still
  not cheap enough to displace Black Sea origin into Asia.
- Fund short in SRW (74,200 contracts) is crowded. A crop-threatening weather event in
  Kansas could trigger violent short-covering.

## Cross-Commodity Themes
- Dollar index (DXY) at 104.2, little changed on the week. A sustained dollar move above
  106 would add further headwind to U.S. export competitiveness.
- Crude oil (WTI) at $72.40/bbl, down $1.80 on the week. Lower energy reduces ethanol
  margin support for corn.
- No major U.S. policy developments this week affecting the grain complex.
