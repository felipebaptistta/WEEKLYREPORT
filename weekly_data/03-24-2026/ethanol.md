EIA Ethanol Market UpdateLatest Report: Week Ending March 13, 2026 (Released March 18, 2026)Production TrendsAverage daily ethanol production dropped to 1.093 million barrels per day (bpd). This is a decrease of 33,000 bpd from the prior week's 1.126 million bpd. The primary driver of this pullback was in the Midwest, where production fell by 43,000 bpd to reach a weekly average of 1.035 million bpd. Despite this weekly decline, the four-week average for production remains healthy, tracking roughly 22,000 bpd ahead of the same period last year.Stock InventoryDomestic ethanol inventories surged to 26.407 million barrels, representing an increase of 827,000 barrels compared to the previous week. This build was heavily concentrated in the Midwest region, which added over 850,000 barrels to its reserves. These stock levels are now at their highest point since April 2025, suggesting a temporary slowdown in blending demand or a backlog in the movement of fuel to the coasts.Market Context and Corn DemandThis data indicates a cooling of the recent aggressive "draw-down" in inventories. The combination of lower weekly production and rising stocks suggests that the immediate, high-intensity demand for corn from ethanol plants softened mid-month. However, because production is still significantly higher than the three-year average for this time of year, the long-term outlook for corn consumption remains supportive.Upcoming DataThe next EIA report, covering the week ending March 20, is scheduled for release tomorrow, March 25. This will be the next key data point to determine if the stock build is a short-term blip or the start of a spring seasonal trend.
