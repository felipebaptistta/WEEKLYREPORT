\### USDA WASDE – March 2026



The March WASDE introduced relatively small revisions to the U.S. balance sheets but delivered a notable increase in global corn ending stocks. Production upgrades in Ukraine and Brazil pushed global corn supplies higher, reinforcing the narrative of comfortable world inventories despite localized crop losses in Argentina.



Market participants also focused on continued strength in U.S. soybean processing demand, where higher crush and meal prices indicate robust domestic feed demand.



\---



\### U.S. Corn Balance Sheet



Production: unchanged  

Exports: unchanged  

Feed \& Residual: unchanged  

Ethanol: unchanged  



Ending Stocks: 2,127 million bushels (unchanged)



Interpretation:



The U.S. corn balance sheet was left unchanged in the March report, with USDA maintaining its existing supply and demand outlook. Market attention is now shifting toward the March 31 Prospective Plantings report, which will provide the first official look at 2026 acreage intentions.



\---



\### U.S. Soybean Balance Sheet



Production: unchanged  



Crush: +5 million bushels  

Imports: +5 million bushels  



Ending Stocks: 350 million bushels (unchanged)



Additional adjustments:



Soybean oil used for biofuels: -800 million pounds  

Food/industrial oil use: higher  



Interpretation:



Although soybean ending stocks were unchanged, the increase in crush reflects strong domestic processing demand. Higher soybean meal prices, with the season-average price raised to $300 per ton, reinforce the view that feed demand remains supportive for the crush sector.



\---



\### U.S. Wheat Balance Sheet



Production: unchanged  

Exports: unchanged  

Domestic Use: unchanged  



Ending Stocks: 931 million bushels (unchanged)



Season-average price: raised $0.05 to $4.95 per bushel.



Interpretation:



The wheat balance sheet saw no meaningful revisions in March. However, the increase in the season-average price reflects firm cash markets and ongoing support from global export demand.



\---



\### Global Corn Revisions



Brazil production: 132.0 MMT (+1.0 MMT)



Ukraine production: 30.7 MMT (+1.7 MMT)



Argentina production: 52.0 MMT (-1.0 MMT)



Global ending stocks: 292.8 MMT  

Trade expectation: \~289 MMT



Interpretation:



The nearly 4 MMT increase in global corn stocks reinforces the narrative of comfortable world supply. Production gains in Ukraine and Brazil more than offset yield losses in Argentina, suggesting global corn availability remains ample heading into the Northern Hemisphere growing season.



\---



\### Global Soybean Revisions



Argentina production: 48.0 MMT (-0.5 MMT)



Interpretation:



The small reduction in Argentina’s soybean crop reflects yield losses tied to earlier dryness. However, global supply expectations remain largely unchanged as Brazil continues to harvest a large crop.



\---



\### Global Wheat Revisions



Australia production: 36.0 MMT (-1.0 MMT)



Global ending stocks: 277.0 MMT



Interpretation:



Despite the modest reduction in Australian output, global wheat inventories remain historically high and continue to reflect comfortable supply levels.



\---



\### Market Interpretation



Soybeans:



The soybean balance sheet showed an important underlying signal despite unchanged ending stocks. Rising crush demand and stronger soybean meal pricing indicate continued domestic feed demand strength that is helping support the market.



Corn:



The increase in global corn stocks was the most bearish signal in the report. Production upgrades in Ukraine and Brazil highlight the continued abundance of global corn supplies.



Macro context:



Grain markets are currently taking additional direction from outside markets. Volatility in crude oil prices, driven by geopolitical developments in the Middle East, has introduced significant swings in energy markets that are influencing broader commodity sentiment.
