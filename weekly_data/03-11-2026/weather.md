\### United States



Weather patterns across the United States remain mixed heading into mid-March. The latest 6–10 day outlook calls for above-normal temperatures across the eastern half of the country, including the Mississippi Valley, Southern Plains, Southwest, and California, while cooler-than-normal conditions are expected from the Pacific Northwest into the Northern Plains. Precipitation forecasts favor above-normal rainfall from the Mississippi Valley to the East Coast and across the Northern Rockies, while much of the central Plains and the Southwest are expected to remain near to below normal.



Drought conditions remain a growing concern across several key agricultural regions. As of early March, roughly 54.9 percent of the contiguous United States is experiencing some level of drought, a notable increase from the prior month. Dryness has expanded across Mississippi, Oklahoma, and Texas following limited winter precipitation and above-normal temperatures. Snowpack levels in parts of the western United States, including the Cascades and the Great Basin, also remain below normal, raising concerns about water availability ahead of the spring growing season.



\### Brazil



Weather conditions in Brazil remain a central focus as soybean harvest progresses and producers continue planting the safrinha corn crop. National soybean harvest progress has reached 50.6 percent of the planted area, advancing from 41.7 percent the previous week but still trailing last year’s pace of 60.9 percent. Even so, the current pace remains slightly ahead of the five-year average of 48.5 percent.



Safrinha corn planting has accelerated alongside the soybean harvest, reaching 75.9 percent of the projected area compared with 64.9 percent the prior week. Planting remains behind last year’s pace of 83.1 percent but continues to run slightly ahead of the five-year average of 71.5 percent.



In Mato Grosso, producers are working to complete both soybean harvest and safrinha planting within increasingly narrow weather windows. Fieldwork has progressed during intermittent dry periods, but forecasts indicate a significant increase in rainfall beginning around March 15 and continuing through at least March 19. These rains could disrupt field operations and slow the completion of planting. Excess moisture has already caused localized interruptions in harvest activity in parts of the Médio-Norte region, while persistent humidity increases the risk of grain damage and complicates crop protection efforts, raising pest and disease pressure.



\### Argentina



Weather conditions across Argentina have improved somewhat following recent rainfall events that replenished soil moisture across several agricultural regions. Precipitation benefited areas including northwest Argentina, central Santa Fe, southern Córdoba, and western Buenos Aires, stabilizing crop conditions after earlier dryness.



Approximately 74 percent of Argentina’s soybean area is currently rated between normal and excellent condition. Around 27 percent of first-crop soybeans are now in the grain-filling stage, where continued rainfall will be important to sustain yield potential. Roughly 40 percent of second-crop soybeans remain in yield-definition stages and still face moisture constraints in parts of the core production regions.



Corn conditions remain broadly favorable as well, with roughly 87.4 percent of the crop rated between normal and excellent. Early harvest activity has begun and currently covers around 7.2 percent of the planted area. Reported yields average approximately 9.5 tons per hectare in the North Core region and roughly 6.5 tons per hectare in eastern Entre Ríos. \### Global



Across the broader global agricultural landscape, weather conditions remain generally supportive for crop development in most major producing regions. Rainfall across Brazil during February helped maintain soil moisture levels across much of the central production belt stretching from the Amazon region through the Center-West and into the Southeast.



However, rainfall patterns have been less favorable in southern Brazil, where below-normal precipitation has limited soybean development in Rio Grande do Sul during critical reproductive stages including flowering and grain fill.
