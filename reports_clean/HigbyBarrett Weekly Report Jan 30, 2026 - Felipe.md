Macro / Policy – Crude Slips, Dollar Steady, Freight Tightens

Crude oil softened throughout the week, with West Texas Intermediate closing near $62.39 per barrel and
Brent near $67.57, easing energy input costs across the supply chain. The dollar index hovered near 96,
softening modestly and providing slight tailwinds to US export competitiveness. Barge freight surged as
ice disrupted river movement on the Upper Mississippi and Illinois, driving seasonal highs in tariff rates.
With macro indicators largely stable, logistics and basis continue to guide market tone.

Weather – Plains Freeze, South America Dries

A second Arctic front swept across the US Plains, with subzero temperatures and uneven snow cover
heightening winterkill risk for hard red winter wheat. River ice buildup slowed barge flows along key
tributaries. In South America, central Brazil received consistent rainfall, while the southern states and
much of Argentina remained dry. Argentine crop ratings deteriorated sharply, raising the probability of
irreversible yield loss as February approaches.

Corn Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: March corn rose eight cents on the week to close at $4.30, holding near the upper
end of its January range. The March–May spread held steady at minus 7 cents, representing
approximately 50 percent of full carry. The curve remains in carry, though not at maximum, a reflection of
firm front-end commercial pull rather than any shift in the broader balance sheet. Volatility remained low
and open interest ticked higher, suggesting quiet accumulation rather than new directional conviction.

Funds & Positioning: Managed Money held a net short of 95,867 contracts as of January 20, trimming
exposure only modestly on the week. Gross shorts stood near 305,000, with longs just above 209,000.
While still elevated historically, the speculative short has stopped expanding. With futures failing to break
lower, the pressure to add new shorts has eased. In the absence of a fresh macro driver or shift in export
flow, funds appear comfortable maintaining a defensive bias without further buildup.

Cash & Basis: Cash markets tightened materially. CIF basis jumped to +97H as river freight constraints
intensified, and interior processor bids strengthened across key ethanol regions. River basis firmed in
response to limited barge capacity and ongoing seasonal delays. FOB Gulf offers held steady near
+109H for February, signaling persistent downstream demand. The basis strength is not speculative, it
reflects real logistics, confirmed execution, and continued slow farmer selling.

Export Sales & Inspections: Export inspections totaled 1.51 million metric tons for the week ending
January 22, the highest weekly volume since mid-December. Cumulative shipments for the marketing
year reached 31.44 million metric tons, 53.4 percent above last year’s pace. Two flash sales were
confirmed during the week, totaling 345,000 metric tons (split between Colombia and unknown). The
weekly report and flash sales align with USDA’s revised 2025/26 US corn export forecast and reinforces
the on-pace execution theme.

Global Context: Brazil’s January corn export projection was revised to 3.45 million metric tons, according
to ANEC, with FOB offers from Paranaguá near $205 per metric ton. The Argentine crop remains under

Soybean Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: March soybeans settled at $10.74, gaining approximately 11 cents on the week.
The March–May spread closed at a 12-cent carry, steady week over week. While the flat price remains
constrained by global supply prospects, the nearby curve still reflects meaningful domestic pull. Open
interest increased, and daily trading ranges narrowed, a sign of less directional conviction but ongoing
positioning around crush-led strength. Full carry percentages were not computed due to lack of published
storage assumptions, though the structure remains comfortably in contango.

Products & Crush: NOPA’s December crush, reported on January 15, totaled 225.0 million bushels, the
second-highest monthly volume on record. National totals likely exceeded 230 million bushels. Soybean
oil stocks rose to 1.642 billion pounds, a seven-month high, while soymeal demand remained firm. The
crush margin continues to be meal-driven, as soy oil faces policy-linked headwinds. These fundamentals
confirm USDA’s January pivot toward domestic demand as the primary source of use.

Cash & Basis: CIF basis held firm at +109H, supported by limited interior movement and ongoing barge
disruptions. FOB Gulf offers for February–March remained steady, quoted between +115 and 118H,
underscoring continued export interest. Processors across the Eastern and Western Corn Belts
maintained strong bids: central Illinois stayed near +5H, while select Iowa plants were reportedly paying
up to +10H. The national average basis firmed by 1 cent to -67H. Despite seasonally soft futures, interior
processor bids remain elevated, backed by firm soymeal demand and limited farm sales.

Funds & Positioning: Managed Money reduced net length to 14,624 contracts as of January 20, a
modest pullback from earlier January highs. Gross longs dipped to around 99,000 contracts, while gross
shorts ticked up to approximately 84,000. The positioning profile now looks lighter and more responsive,
not aggressively long, but still tilted constructively due to domestic demand resilience.

Export Sales & Inspections: Weekly inspections for the period ending January 22 totaled 1.32 million
metric tons, led by shipments to China. A daily flash sale of 192,350 metric tons to unknown was
confirmed by USDA on January 21. However, cumulative US soybean exports are now approximately
20.67 million metric tons, down about 37.4 percent year over year, reflecting Brazil’s dominant position in
early-season trade. While total commitments remain solid, the broader US export program continues to
lag.

Global Context: Brazil’s January soybean export forecast has been revised down to 3.23 million metric
tons, per ANEC, slightly below earlier expectations. FOB Santos offers hovered around $402 per metric
ton, still undercutting US Gulf equivalents into Asia. Brazilian harvest progress reached just under 5
percent, modestly behind average pace, slowing the short-term flow of new-crop supply but not yet
altering expectations for a heavy first quarter shipping window. In Argentina, dryness persisted and crop
condition ratings declined further, increasing sensitivity to mid-February rainfall. Chinese demand signals

Wheat Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: March Chicago SRW closed at $5.35, up approximately 28 cents on the week. The
March–May spread held steady at a nine-cent carry, reflecting a storage-led market. Full carry
percentages were not computed due to lack of posted assumptions. The SRW curve remains sluggish,
anchored by ample global supply and limited urgency from end users. Price action responded briefly to
midweek winterkill headlines but lacked follow-through. Minneapolis and Kansas City contracts were
steadier, with modest HRW strength helping firm basis-adjusted tone.

Cash & Basis: FOB Gulf SRW offers held near +100H (equivalent to about $6.16 per bushel), while
HRW with 11 percent protein hovered around +110K. Interior Plains basis was mostly unchanged:
ordinary protein held near +90K and 12 percent protein near +106K. Pacific Northwest soft white wheat
remained steady around $6.55 per bushel. Overall, basis remains stable but lacks directional energy.
Export buyers continue to be selective, with US origin still priced out of competitive tenders due to Black
Sea pressure.

Export Sales & Inspections: Weekly inspections for the period ending January 22 totaled 0.35 million
metric tons, slightly behind the same week last year. However, marketing-year-to-date exports are 18.2
percent ahead of last season. Weekly sales figures for this window posted after we had written this
week’s edition, but the previous report showed a strong 632,000 metric tons booked, primarily to
Southeast Asia and Latin America. No daily flash sales were reported during the period, adding a little
more weight to the weekly report to support the recent export volume momentum, or to slow it.

Global context: Black Sea supply continues to set the tone for global pricing. Russian FOB wheat with
12.5 percent protein was offered near $229 per metric ton, with January exports tracking close to three
million metric tons, strong, though slightly below December levels. Ukrainian shipments via Danube and
rail remained undisrupted. French wheat held near €240 per metric ton (roughly $260), which continues to
limit EU competitiveness in global tenders. Argentine Up-River wheat was quoted near $210 per metric
ton. With no policy or logistical barriers in major origins, low-cost supply continues to cap upside.

Funds & Positioning: Managed money traders extended net shorts in Chicago SRW to 113,560
contracts as of January 20, adding to bearish bets by nearly 4,000 contracts from the previous week.
Gross shorts rose to approximately 199,000 contracts, while gross longs remained near 85,000. The book
remains the most asymmetrically short of the major grain complexes. Despite the position size, there is no
evident catalyst forcing broad fund repositioning.

So What? Wheat remains trapped in a familiar cycle, large speculative shorts, ample global supply, and
no structural break to change the tone. Basis is flat, spreads lean toward full carry, and US origin remains
noncompetitive versus Black Sea flows. Until a structural shock emerges, whether from weather, freight,
or geopolitics, this remains a defensive, short-led market. Futures are too bearish to collapse further

US Cash Market & Basis

Corn: CIF Gulf basis surged to +97H for January barges, up sharply on the week due to ice-related river
delays. February CIF was quoted near +92H. FOB Gulf offers for February held steady around +109H.
River logistics tightened materially: Illinois River freight jumped to between 640 and 685 percent of tariff,
significantly higher than both the prior week and year-ago levels. Interior basis remained firm, the DTN
Corn Index averaged -35H nationally, up one cent week over week. Eastern Corn Belt processors,
particularly ethanol plants in Iowa and Illinois, were bidding as strong as +20 to 30H, underscoring interior
competition for spot bushels.

Soybeans: CIF NOLA spot barges held near +109H, with February quoted at +103H. FOB Gulf
premiums remained steady, ranging from +115H to +118H for February to March loadings. The national
average basis firmed one cent to -67H, supported by a strong interior crush pull. Central Illinois
processors continued to post flat or slight premiums to March, while other Midwest plants reportedly paid
+5 to +10H to secure ownership.

Wheat: FOB Gulf offers for SRW held near +100H (equivalent to approximately $6.16 per bushel), while
HRW with 11 percent protein remained around +110K. Interior Plains basis was stable: ordinary protein
held near +90K and 12 percent protein near +106K. Pacific Northwest soft white wheat bids were
unchanged around $6.55 per bushel. Export competitiveness remains limited, but basis structure is
holding firm in the absence of new disruptions.

So What? Despite heavy carryout projections on paper, physical markets remain firm across all three
majors. Corn basis is being driven by river constraints and aggressive ethanol demand, not speculative
positioning. Soybeans continue to reflect tight crush-linked pull, even as exports lag. Wheat basis is
stable but uninspired, propped up by interior structure rather than global demand. The cash market
continues to defy futures' bearish tone, until basis softens or logistical pressures ease, physical tightness
will remain the dominant front-end signal.

showed robust movement through Santos, Barcarena, and northern arc corridors. Early soybean harvest

official exchange rate held near 390 Argentine pesos per US dollar, though parallel rates continue to
influence farmer behavior. Government discussions around export quota management are ongoing and
likely contingent on February rainfall outcomes.

Weather & Crop Conditions

United States
Lows dropped below 15 degrees Fahrenheit in parts of Kansas and Nebraska, and subzero wind chills
recorded across Iowa, Illinois, and Indiana. Snow cover remained variable, sufficient across the Central
Plains and Delta, but patchy in northern Missouri and eastern Nebraska, raising localized winterkill
concerns in hard red winter wheat zones. NOAA’s six-to-ten day outlook projected above normal
temperatures across the Southern Plains and Southeast, with colder anomalies lingering over the
Northern Tier and Great Lakes. Precipitation remained below normal across much of the Corn Belt, while
wetter anomalies were centered along the Lower Mississippi Valley. River logistics continued to tighten,
as ice buildup on the Upper Mississippi and Illinois Rivers slowed barge movement and pushed freight
rates sharply higher, though no closures were reported. Gulf loading remained steady under ongoing draft
limitations.

Brazil
Harvest pace improved modestly but remains behind average, estimated at approximately 5 percent
complete nationwide as of January 25. The north to south weather divide persisted, as Mato Grosso,
Goiás, and Tocantins received steady rainfall, supporting pod fill and early harvest, while southern states
remained critically dry. Rio Grande do Sul, Paraná, and western Santa Catarina recorded less than 30
percent of normal January rainfall, and crop scouts flagged irreversible damage to late-planted soybeans
and first-crop corn. Forecast models offered little relief through early February. While no port disruptions
have been reported, southern crop stress is now central to Brazil’s forward supply tone and export pace
heading into the second quarter.

Argentina
Rainfall deficits worsened across key production zones, notably Córdoba, La Pampa, and central Buenos
Aires. Temperatures stayed high, with frequent daily highs exceeding 95˚ Fahrenheit, accelerating crop
stress. The Buenos Aires Grain Exchange cut corn good to excellent ratings to 15 percent and soybeans
to 20 percent, marking the lowest levels of the season and confirming widespread early yield loss.
Scattered showers reached parts of Santa Fe and Entre Ríos, but coverage was limited and insufficient to
shift crop trajectories. With most fields entering critical reproductive stages, the next ten days are pivotal.
If February opens dry, markets may begin pricing in material production losses.

Global
Winter wheat across Russia, Ukraine, and the European Union remained in dormancy under generally
sufficient snow cover. A mid-January cold snap brought temperatures of -25˚ Celsius to southern Russia,
but insulating snow layers prevented significant winterkill. No new threats emerged across Europe.
Ukrainian logistics via Danube ports and rail corridors continued without disruption. Australia’s wheat
harvest is complete, and Canadian grain export infrastructure remains stable. Outside South America,
global weather is broadly stable, with no major production alarms in play.

So What?
South America is now the core weather risk. Brazil’s north supports record soybean potential, but
southern stress is hardening, and crop loss risk is climbing without rainfall. In Argentina, confirmed yield
