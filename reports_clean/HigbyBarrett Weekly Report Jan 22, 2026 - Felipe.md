Corn – Funds Press Shorts, but Cash and Logistics
Keep the Front End Anchored
March corn finished near $4.25/bu, little changed on
balance, as funds built their largest net short since October
(–90,658 contracts) following USDA’s upward revision to
production and carryout. The Mar/May spread widened
modestly to 7¢, still short of full carry and consistent with a
front end that remains supported by real-world movement
rather than pure storage economics. Export inspections
totaled 1.48 MMT for the week ending January 15, slightly
below the prior week’s level but still reinforcing strong
execution. Cumulative shipments now stand at 29.92 MMT,
keeping pace 55–60 percent ahead of last year, consistent with USDA’s 3.2 bbu forecast. CIF Gulf basis
softened to +88H, still within a firm +85–90H range, while interior river and rail basis held mostly steady.
Freight premiums rose amid colder weather, and barge movement slowed further as ice developed
across the Upper Mississippi. Despite a looser balance sheet, steady exports and replacement costs
continued to prevent wider carries from emerging.

Wheat – SRW Stays Rangebound as Russian Flow Cements Ceiling

March SRW wheat settled near $5.10/bu, down about 7¢ from the prior week, with the Mar/May spread
steady at an 11¢ carry, reflecting ample stocks and a storage-led structure. USDA data confirmed global
and US ending stocks were higher year-over-year, with Russian FOB 12.5 percent wheat holding at
$225–227/MT and January exports expected near 3.4 MMT. US wheat inspections totaled 0.39 MMT, up
modestly on the week and keeping cumulative shipments at 15.58 MMT, or 19 percent ahead of last
year, in line with USDA’s 900 mbu export projection. Speculative short positioning remained extreme,
with managed money holding a –108,247 contract net short. Broadly, the market remains capped by
global supply and Black Sea competitiveness, but cash flow and short saturation continue to support
structure.

Soybeans – Demand Splits Sharply as Export Book Cedes to Domestic Crush

March soybeans ended the week near $10.63/bu, firmer on balance, but demand remains bifurcated, with
export share continuing to cede to Brazil while domestic crush does the heavy lifting. The Mar/May
spread traded near 11¢, slightly wider but still reflecting a relatively supported front end given firm
processor pull. Weekly inspections totaled 1.34 MMT, down from the prior week’s 1.59 MMT, with
cumulative shipments at 17.93 MMT, or roughly 42 percent behind last year’s pace. While China
remained the top destination, the lag versus last year continues to underscore Brazil’s competitive edge.

Macro / Policy – Ethanol Print Hits Record; Argentina Cuts Export Taxes

The US dollar index ended the week near 98.62, slightly lower than reported earlier, while the Brazilian
real weakened modestly to R$5.37/USD, modestly supporting Brazilian exports. US ethanol output
surged to a record 1.196 million bbl/day in early January, boosting corn grind even as stocks rose to
24.47 million bbl. In Argentina, the Milei administration formalized export tax cuts, 24 percent on
soybeans, 8.5 percent on corn, and 7.5 percent on wheat, in a bid to accelerate farmer sales and FX
inflow. No new US biofuel or grain trade policies were reported during the week.

Weather – US Cold Snap Slows Logistics; Argentina Drier, Brazil Mixed

NOAA’s 6–10 day outlook showed below-normal temperatures across the Midwest, above-normal across
the Southern Plains, and dry conditions across the Delta and Pacific Northwest. River traffic slowed
further on ice buildup along the Illinois and Upper Mississippi, though no full closures were reported. In
Brazil, central and northern zones received 100–200 percent of normal rainfall, stabilizing soil moisture
and supporting early harvest (1 percent complete). In contrast, southern Brazil (Rio Grande do Sul,
Paraná) remained drier, raising localized stress concerns. In Argentina, subpar rainfall persisted across
Córdoba and Buenos Aires, placing early corn under yield pressure. While Santa Fe saw isolated relief,
the moisture gap remains wide and the window for crop recovery is narrowing.

Corn Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: March corn futures settled near $4.25/bu, ending modestly firmer on the week.
While still near the softest levels since mid-October, the move was not the sharper break originally
framed. The Mar–May spread widened slightly to 7¢, roughly 47 percent of full carry using the report’s
storage/interest assumptions, keeping structure supported relative to storage economics. Open interest

rose modestly, with positioning indicating increased participation from the short side after USDA’s bearish
January reset, but the front end continues to resist a full carry liquidation.

Funds & Positioning: As of January 13, Managed Money extended its net short to –90,658 contracts,
the most bearish positioning since October. Gross shorts surged to 293k, while gross longs declined
slightly, signaling conviction in a looser Q1 corn balance. However, with basis still holding and exports
firm, the market remains exposed to short-covering risk should demand or weather conditions tighten
unexpectedly.

Cash & Basis: Cash markets reflected the same balance between increased supply and steady demand.
Gulf basis firmed modestly, with CIF corn trading near +84¢ over March and FOB values around +110¢,
signaling continued exporter interest despite lower futures. Interior bids were generally steady to slightly
weaker following the Grain Stocks report, consistent with a market that is better supplied but not
burdensome. Logistics remained orderly, with stable river conditions and freight holding near recent
levels, allowing basis to cushion the futures decline rather than amplify it.

Export Sales & Inspections: Export inspections reached 1.48 MMT for the week ending January 15, per
USDA, slightly below early trade estimates but still robust relative to seasonal norms. Cumulative
shipments reached 29.92 MMT, putting the US 55–60 percent ahead of last year’s pace. Japan and
Mexico remained key buyers. For the week ending January 8, net export sales totaled 1.14 MMT, and
while flash sales were absent during January 15–22, forward commitments stand near 52.0 MMT,
tracking closely to the 3.2 bbu USDA forecast.

Global Context: Global corn flows remain competitive, led by Brazil’s robust January export program.
ANEC projects corn exports at 3.27 MMT, up from 2.85 MMT the prior week, supported by carryover and
favorable FOB values around $205/MT at Paranaguá. Argentina’s Up-River corn is offered near $210/MT,
but early yield concerns have surfaced as dryness persists across Córdoba and Buenos Aires. Ukrainian
corn continues to move via Danube and rail, priced near $215/MT FOB, sustaining Black Sea
competitiveness. US exporters retain a logistical edge into Latin America and Japan, but global offers
remain aggressive into Asia and the Middle East. As South America ramps, execution pace, not board
structure, will dictate the export share US corn can hold.

So What? Corn continues to show a resilient physical market despite bearish futures. Spreads remain
below full carry, basis is firm, and barge logistics are tightening, all signs of spot demand anchoring the
front end. Speculative short positioning has grown, but commercials are not loosening their bids. Until
demand falters or Brazil’s export ramp accelerates, the market is more likely to trade sideways with firm
spreads than unwind sharply.

Soybean Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: March soybeans settled near $10.63/bu, finishing firmer on the week and remaining
rangebound at the front end. The Mar–May spread widened to 11¢, approximately 50 percent of full carry
under the report’s storage/interest assumptions, reflecting a market that is still being anchored more by
domestic processor pull than by storage economics. Futures volume and open interest were steady,
suggesting the move was driven more by positioning and basis behavior than broad liquidation, even as
export follow-through remains soft.

Products & Crush: NOPA’s December crush totaled 225.0 mbu, second highest on record, with national
totals estimated above 230 mbu. Soybean oil stocks rose to 1.642 billion lbs, the highest since May 2024,
while soymeal demand remained firm. The crush margin continues to be meal-driven, as soy oil faces
policy-linked headwinds. These fundamentals confirm USDA’s January pivot toward domestic demand as

Wheat Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: March Chicago SRW wheat futures closed near $5.10/bu on January 21, marking a
weekly decline of 7¢ from January 13, not flat. The Mar–May spread held near an 11¢ carry, roughly 69
percent of full carry using the report’s storage/interest assumptions, reinforcing the market’s storage-led
tone. HRW futures were flat to slightly firmer in basis-adjusted terms, and Minneapolis spring wheat was
little changed. Despite heavy global fundamentals, particularly from Black Sea origins, the futures curve
suggests low urgency for nearby coverage, with speculative saturation continuing to anchor the board.
The absence of bullish catalysts has kept flat price soft, but the market’s refusal to break sharply lower
shows bearish positioning is already priced in.

Cash & Basis: FOB Gulf SRW basis remained near +100H ($6.18/bu), and HRW 11 percent protein
basis held at +110K, showing no material movement. Soft White wheat in the PNW traded near $6.55/bu,
while HRW 11.5 percent in the PNW held at +155K. Interior basis across the Southern and Central Plains
remained steady, with ordinary protein bids near +90K and 12 percent protein around +106K. Cash
behavior remains surplus-driven: basis is stable but lacks momentum. With global offers undercutting US
levels, domestic and export buyers remain price-sensitive and tactical, rather than volume-seeking.

Funds & Positioning: As of January 13, Managed Money was net short –108,247 contracts in Chicago
SRW, unchanged from the prior week. Gross shorts stood near 198.5k, while gross longs were stable at
90k. This is the most asymmetric positioning among major grains, creating vulnerability to short covering
on any surprise demand or weather event. Still, with no current trigger, speculators are content to
maintain bearish exposure amid heavy global stocks and export disadvantages.

So What? Wheat remains the heaviest grain complex, weighed down by both ample supply and
saturated fund shorts. Basis and spreads reflect full carry conditions, and while exports are outperforming
last year, global pricing keeps US offers uncompetitive. Futures trade within a narrow range: too bearish
to break down further without new supply, but too expensive to rally amid current global offers. Without a
weather, policy, or logistics shock, wheat will continue to trade defensively, anchored by carry and short
positioning, not bullish conviction.

US Cash Market & Basis

Corn: CIF Gulf basis dipped 2¢ to +88H, while FOB Gulf for February held at +107H, reflecting continued
export flow despite weaker futures. Interior movement diverged: St. Louis River basis firmed 3¢ to +24H,
helped by restricted barge flow, while Western processor bids (IA/NE) were steady between –15H and –
12H. PNW rail corn held at +112H, supported by Asian demand. Freight premiums rose across both
barge and secondary rail markets, as ice on the Upper Mississippi and Illinois Rivers hampered logistics.
Overall, cash remains resilient and reflects tight front-end pull even as the board trades heavy.

Soybeans: CIF Gulf soybean basis rose 3¢ to +99H, supported by Chinese buying and a thin supply
pipeline. FOB Gulf held steady at +118H, and PNW bids remained at +110H. Interior strength persisted:
Decatur, IL held near +5H, while Spiritwood, ND increased 5¢ to –65H, driven by soymeal demand. River
basis in St. Louis remained near +26H despite colder logistics conditions. Overall, the soybean cash
market continues to lean on firm crush margins, with crushers anchoring the front end against a backdrop
of sluggish exports and rising South American competition.

Wheat: FOB Gulf SRW stayed near +100H ($6.18/bu), while HRW 11 percent basis held at +110K. In the
PNW, HRW 11.5 percent was quoted at +155K, and Soft White wheat remained near $6.55/bu. Interior
HRW bids in the Southern Plains ranged from +90K to +106K, depending on protein. Despite the stable
tone, global competition continues to suppress momentum, and US mills are well-covered. With Russian
and Argentine wheat undercutting US offers by $10–$15/MT, the US cash wheat complex remains
steady, but static.

International Cash & Trade Flows

Brazil: Brazil’s early-season program remains the dominant global flow driver, with export commitments
staying aggressive into late January. ANEC projects January soybean exports at 3.73 MMT, while corn is
pegged at 3.27 MMT, keeping Brazil in clear control of nearby export share. FOB Santos soybeans were
reported near $402/MT, and Paranaguá corn near $205/MT, levels that preserve Brazil’s pricing
advantage into Asia and the Middle East even before the bulk of the harvest reaches peak pipeline.

Argentina: Argentina’s flow is steady but increasingly weather sensitive. Up-River FOB corn remains
near $210/MT, with wheat quoted in a similar neighborhood as post-harvest shipments continue. The
government formalized export tax cuts (24 percent soybeans, 8.5 percent corn, 7.5 percent wheat),
aiming to accelerate farmer selling and FX inflow; the real question is whether improved economics
translate into a meaningful near-term pickup in producer marketing given dryness across Córdoba and
Buenos Aires. For now, offers remain competitive, but forward volume will hinge on February rainfall and
seller participation.

China: China’s return to US soybeans for April–May delivery (at least 10 cargoes) provided near-term
support to US basis and helped keep the front end from loosening further, but the buying is still widely
viewed as reserve-driven rather than a broad commercial pivot. Corn and wheat demand signals remain
muted in this window, and there is no clear evidence yet of a structural change in import appetite.

EU / Black Sea: Black Sea pricing continues to define the global ceiling for wheat. Russian 12.5 percent
FOB wheat held near $225–227/MT, with January exports expected in the 3.0–3.4 MMT range, keeping
tender markets anchored to lower-cost supply. EU wheat remains disadvantaged into key destinations,
and without a logistics or policy disruption, Russia and Argentina continue to cap upside in global wheat
pricing through late January.

So What? The global market remains dictated by the lowest-cost origins: Brazil in soybeans and corn,
and Russia in wheat. Argentina remains competitive but increasingly tied to weather and seller response
after tax cuts. China’s soybean presence is supportive on breaks, but not yet decisive. Until FOB spreads
realign meaningfully or weather forces a supply reprice, US export competitiveness will remain tactical
rather than structural.

Weather & Crop Conditions

United States
A deep Arctic air mass swept across the Midwest late in the week, bringing wind chills below –30°F to
Iowa, Illinois, and Indiana. Despite the extreme cold, snow cover remained adequate across most winter
wheat zones, limiting winterkill risk and preventing any confirmed crop damage. NOAA’s 6–10 day
outlook continues to show a split pattern: above-normal temperatures across the Southern Plains and
Southwest, colder-than-normal conditions across the Upper Midwest and Northeast. Precipitation is
expected to remain below average across much of the Corn Belt, with wetter anomalies focused along
the Tennessee Valley. On the logistics front, barge traffic slowed on the Upper Mississippi and Illinois
Rivers due to increased ice formation, though no system-wide closures occurred. Freight rates remained
firm amid seasonal tightness, but no major transport disruptions have materialized.

Brazil
A north–south divide persists in Brazilian weather patterns. Central and northern regions, including Mato
Grosso, Goiás, and western Bahia, received 100–200 percent of normal rainfall over the past two weeks,
stabilizing soil moisture and supporting pod fill. Southern states remain under pressure: Rio Grande do
Sul and Paraná recorded below-normal rainfall again this week, with late-planted soybeans in
reproductive phases showing signs of stress. Crop scouts have grown more cautious as models project
continued dryness into late January. National harvest remains slow at 1 percent complete, and while no
logistical problems have surfaced yet, crop potential in the South is now being actively reassessed.

Argentina
Dryness has intensified across core production zones, especially Córdoba, Buenos Aires, and La Pampa.
The Rosario Exchange reported escalating stress in first-planted corn and early soybeans, while
scattered storms in Santa Fe and eastern areas provided only partial relief. Planting is nearly complete

So What?
Weather has reemerged as a directional risk factor, particularly in South America. Brazil’s Center-West
continues to support record soybean potential, but southern dryness is flirting with irreversible yield loss if
the dry pattern persists. In Argentina, confirmed moisture deficits are beginning to impact early crops at a
critical phase. In contrast, the US remains stable, with no immediate weather-related threats to
overwintering crops or logistics. As January closes, the tone of the global grain market will hinge
increasingly on rainfall outcomes in southern Brazil and Argentina. Those regional developments are now
the primary wildcards for supply-side recalibration.

Synthesis & Outlook

Futures were mixed to modestly softer in tone this week, but cash markets and spreads continued to
show firm commercial behavior rather than broad liquidation. March corn settled near $4.25/bu, soybeans
finished firmer near $10.63/bu, and SRW wheat dipped to $5.10/bu. Despite the heavy headline balance
sheets, calendar spreads remain short of full carry under standard storage/interest assumptions, CIF and
processor basis levels are steady to firm, and both ethanol and export execution remain supportive,
especially for corn. Fund positioning has turned more defensive, with managed money extending corn’s
net short to its deepest since October, yet physical market behavior continues to suggest commercials
are not forcing the front end into a storage-only structure.

Corn continues to exhibit the widest divergence between futures pressure and physical resilience.
Ethanol output surged to a record 1.196 million bbl/day in early January, CIF basis dipped only marginally
to +88H, and inspections remained strong at 1.48 MMT. Soybeans remain a split market: NOPA’s 225
mbu crush (second highest ever) and interior bids in key crush zones underscore solid domestic pull, but
export flows remain light, down 42 percent YoY, with Brazil dominating early-season shipments. Wheat
remains driven by global oversupply, not US fundamentals. Russian FOB values below $230/MT and
January exports of 3.0–3.4 MMT continue to cap upside despite firm US inspections and saturated short
fund exposure.

Bullish triggers: Upside risk is concentrated in South American weather and continued domestic
demand. Southern Brazil remains dry, with late soybeans now flowering under stress and little relief in the
10-day outlook. In Argentina, conditions are tightening across Córdoba and Buenos Aires, with early corn
already showing stress as reproductive windows open. If rainfall fails to materialize, the global supply
tone could shift quickly. US crush and ethanol grind remain historically strong, with processors and
blenders continuing to pay up. If China expands soybean purchases beyond reserve-driven buying, or if
Argentina’s outlook deteriorates further, spreads could tighten and flat price would likely need to reprice
risk accordingly.

Bearish triggers: The downside still hinges on actual supply realization and execution. Brazil’s harvest,
while slow, is underway, and early yields in the Center-West remain consistent with record projections. If
weather stabilizes in the South, the market could begin to price in full-season potential. In Argentina, the
dryness is impactful but not yet irreversible. On the demand side, any pullback in ethanol production or
renewed weakness in CIF bids could loosen spreads and widen carries. Wheat remains capped by
aggressive FOB offers from Russia and Argentina, and without a step-change in US exports, upside is
likely to remain positioning-based rather than fundamental.

Ethanol for America Act

The major barrier to a higher Renewable Fuel Standard (RFS) is the fuel versus food debate.  Now is the
perfect time to move to E15.  Corn prices are extremely low, and the latest USDA WASDE reported a record
crop and a record crop expansion.

USDA WASDE forecast corn production at 16.7 billion bushels, with the yield at 188.8 bushels per acre and
ending  stocks  at 2.1 billion.  The season-average corn price dropped  30 cents to $3.90 a bushel.   Most
importantly, 202526 crop year is expected to have 1.8 billion more bushels than 2024/25.

With current ethanol production consuming 5.6 billion bushels, a 10 percent increase in consumption would
consume an additional 560 million bushels.  The reality is that year around E15 would add approximately
50 million bushels and regulatory streamlining would add another 50 million bushels.  At some point over
the  next  ten  years,  the  additional  corn  consumption  could  be  2.8  billion  bushels  minus  lower  fuel
consumption resulting from electric vehicles.  The ethanol corn consumption increase is likely to be more
of a S curve as new compacity comes online.

With  Brazil  and  the  US  corn  and  soybean  production  increasing,  100  million  bushels  of  ethanol  corn
consumption will barely move the needle.  The ethanol production capacity expansion that would be three
to four years from now is when the corn consumption impact to the corn market would be felt.
