Macro / Policy – WASDE Week Dominates as Biofuels and Trade Headlines Stay in the Mix

Macro drivers remained secondary to WASDE positioning this week, though biofuel and trade narratives
influenced sentiment at the margin. Ethanol fundamentals softened in the latest weekly print, with
production declining to 0.956 million barrels per day and stocks at 25.136 million barrels for the week
ending January 30. The decline appears weather-driven rather than indicative of structural demand
erosion, interrupting but not reversing the broader stabilization trend. Within the soy complex, renewable
diesel policy continues to shape the tone of the market more than daily export flow, with attention
centered on how federal credit structures translate into tangible margin support for soybean oil demand.
Trade headlines circulated but lacked confirmation, and the market remains reluctant to price in policy-
driven demand until transactions appear in the export data. With spreads and basis continuing to provide
clearer real-time signals across grains, macro remains a backdrop rather than a driver, even as
participants monitor policy follow-through following WASDE adjustments.

Weather – US Logistics Improve; Argentina Remains the Volatility Point

In the United States, near-term weather remains operationally supportive. Warmer temperatures have
improved winter logistics and reduced freeze-related constraints, while wheat dormancy conditions
remain stable with adequate snow cover across portions of the Plains and no meaningful winterkill
headlines emerging.

In South America, Brazil continues to experience broadly supportive conditions, with harvest accelerating
in Mato Grosso and safrinha planting advancing under generally favorable moisture profiles. Localized
excess rainfall in southern states has slowed fieldwork intermittently but has not introduced systemic risk.
Argentina remains the focal point for volatility, with moisture deficits persisting across portions of Buenos
Aires, La Pampa, and Córdoba as corn enters pollination and soybeans move through pod-setting.
Forecast models indicate scattered 10–20 mm rainfall over the next 7–10 days, offering partial relief but
not a full recharge of soil moisture. No confirmed large-scale yield losses have been embedded into
official estimates, yet the coming two weeks remain critical in determining whether stress translates into
measurable production impact. Weather therefore represents a modest and conditional upside risk
layered on top of an otherwise supply-heavy baseline.

Corn Market Deep Dive

Post-WASDE Balance Sheet: February WASDE introduced a targeted demand-side adjustment to the
US corn balance sheet while leaving production unchanged at a record 17.021 bbu on 186.5 bpa across
91.3 million harvested acres. The meaningful revision came on exports, which USDA raised 100 mbu to
3.300 bbu in response to sustained shipment strength through the first half of the marketing year. Feed
and residual use remained at 6.200 bbu, and the export increase reduced ending stocks to 2.127 bbu
from 2.227 bbu in January, trimming stocks-to-use to roughly 14 percent while keeping the balance sheet
firmly in surplus territory.

February did not reverse January’s supply reset. It acknowledged that demand, particularly for export
execution, has been stronger than initially assumed. The balance sheet remains loose by historical
standards, but the adjustment shifts the focus squarely toward whether shipment pace can remain strong
enough to prevent renewed stock accumulation into the back half of the year.

Futures & Spreads: March corn settled at $4.28/bu, down roughly five cents on the week following the
WASDE release and briefly testing the $4.30 area midweek. Despite softer flat price, structure remained
composed. The March–May spread held near an eight-cent carry, roughly half of full commercial carry
under standard cost assumptions, signaling recognition of surplus without forcing a full storage incentive.
The curve is paying carry, but it is not being forced wider. That stability suggests commercials remain
comfortable owning nearby coverage at current replacement values. The market is acknowledging
surplus, but it is not pricing distress

Funds & Positioning: According to the latest CFTC Commitments of Traders report (week ending
February 3), managed money holds a net short of 68,786 contracts in corn futures, marking a meaningful
reduction from prior extremes and reinforcing the ongoing short-covering dynamic rather than renewed
bearish expansion. Gross short exposure remains elevated relative to gross longs, yet February WASDE
did not trigger renewed bearish expansion. Positioning now reflects reduced downside conviction rather
than active bullish reallocation, leaving corn less vulnerable to disorderly fund-driven pressure than during
January’s supply repricing phase.

Cash & Basis: Cash markets continue to reinforce front-end stability. CIF Gulf corn barges were bid in
the 109–117¢ over March range, broadly steady versus prior prints and consistent with stable exporter
coverage. FOB Gulf export values remained near the low-$220s per metric ton equivalent, preserving
competitive positioning into key destinations. Interior river bids softened only marginally despite improved
winter logistics and more fluid movement. Freight conditions have stabilized, although residual low-water
constraints continue to limit full normalization in barge economics. Basis behavior suggests supply is
available but not burdensome, with cash absorbing futures weakness rather than amplifying it.

Export Sales & Inspections: Weekly export inspections totaled approximately 1.308 mmt for the week
ending February 5, lifting cumulative shipments to near 34 mmt and keeping pace materially ahead of last
year. Execution remains the most constructive component of the balance sheet and directly supported
USDA’s February export increase to 3.300 bbu.

Soybean Market Deep Dive

Post-WASDE Balance Sheet: February WASDE left the US soybean balance sheet largely unchanged
while lifting global supply expectations. US production was maintained at 4.262 bbu on 53.0 bpa across
80.4 million harvested acres, confirming that domestic supply assumptions are settled. Exports were left
at 1.575 bbu despite continued lagging shipment pace, while crush was maintained at 2.570 bbu,
underscoring USDA’s confidence in domestic processing demand. Ending stocks held at 350 mbu,
keeping stocks-to-use near 8 percent and confirming that January’s loosening remains intact.

The more meaningful adjustment occurred globally. USDA raised Brazilian production again, lifting world
ending stocks to roughly 125 mmt and reinforcing expanding South American availability entering peak
export season. February did not tighten the balance sheet. It reinforced that domestic crush must
continue to offset export underperformance.

Futures & Spreads: March soybeans settled at $11.20/bu, down roughly ten cents on the week as global
supply revisions limited follow-through buying after January’s weather-driven rally. The March–May
spread held in carry near 12–13 cents, a little over half of full commercial carry under standard cost
assumptions. The forward curve remains composed. Carry persists, but the market has not moved toward
a full storage incentive. With Brazil’s export program ramping into Q1, coverage looks adequate and the
market is not being pulled into inverse.

Products & Crush: NOPA January crush, released February 3, totaled approximately 225 million
bushels, one of the strongest January volumes on record and consistent with near-capacity processing
rates. Soybean oil stocks remain relatively tight relative to crush volume, reinforcing renewable diesel
demand as a durable absorber of incremental supply. USDA’s decision to maintain crush at 2.570 bbu in
February underscores domestic processing as the most stable demand component within the balance
sheet. Despite softer export performance, positive crush margins continue to support interior processor
bids and provide structural stability to flat price behavior.

accelerates and export capacity expands. ANEC estimates January soybean exports near 3.23 mmt, with

Weather & Crop Conditions

United States
Weather remains a secondary driver for US grain markets, though short-term patterns have been
operationally supportive. Temperatures across much of the Midwest and Plains have trended near to
slightly above seasonal norms, limiting freeze-related logistics disruptions and allowing river systems to
function more consistently following earlier winter constraints. Snow cover across key HRW wheat areas
in Kansas, Oklahoma, and Colorado remains adequate, reducing immediate winterkill risk despite
intermittent cold shots. NOAA’s 6–10 and 8–14 day outlooks continue to favor moderation rather than
extreme cold, reinforcing dormancy stability for winter wheat and limiting headline-driven volatility. With
harvest complete and no active fieldwork underway, US weather remains neutral from a supply
standpoint, functioning primarily as a logistics variable rather than a production catalyst at this stage of
the calendar.

Brazil
Brazilian weather continues to validate large production expectations, with harvest progress accelerating
during recent drier windows across the Center-West. Mato Grosso soybean harvest has moved into the
30–40 percent range, recovering from early-season delays and aligning more closely with seasonal
norms, while safrinha corn planting has expanded into the 20–30 percent range behind combines.

Argentina
Argentina remains the key weather-sensitive region within the global complex. Dryness persists across
portions of Buenos Aires, La Pampa, and Córdoba as corn enters pollination and soybeans progress

continues to suggest periodic precipitation opportunities, though coverage and intensity remain uncertain.
Weather in Argentina therefore introduces asymmetric risk, not confirmed loss, but elevated sensitivity
relative to Brazil and the United States.

Global
Outside South America, crop conditions remain broadly stable. Northern Hemisphere winter wheat across
the Black Sea, EU, and US Plains remains largely dormant without widespread stress reports. Russia and
Ukraine have not reported meaningful winter damage, and Australia’s harvest has concluded without
weather-related logistical disruption. Across Canada and Western Europe, conditions remain seasonally
normal. At the global level, weather outside Argentina remains a background variable rather than an
active driver of price formation.

So What?
Weather influence remains differentiated rather than uniform. Brazil reinforces supply confidence as
harvest and safrinha planting advance under generally supportive conditions. The United States remains
operationally neutral, with dormancy stability and improving logistics limiting friction. Argentina stands
alone as the primary volatility source, where reproductive-stage crops face uneven moisture profiles
during a critical window. For now, weather represents conditional upside risk rather than confirmed supply
loss, meaning markets remain anchored by February’s supply-heavy baseline unless Argentine stress
intensifies measurably in the coming weeks.

Synthesis & Outlook

February’s data flow confirmed that the grain complex has transitioned from balance-sheet discovery into
a phase defined by confirmation and execution. January delivered the supply reset. February validated it.
USDA revisions were incremental rather than structural, and international institutional updates reinforced
stability rather than introducing new tightening impulses. Prices have stabilized into defined ranges,
spreads remain orderly, and basis continues to carry disproportionate signaling weight. The market is no
longer repricing supply; it is testing whether demand can consistently absorb it without erosion.
