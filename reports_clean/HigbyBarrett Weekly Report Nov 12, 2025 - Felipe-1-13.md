Wheat – Board Gains Continue, Russian Offers Steady But Tightening

December SRW wheat settled at $5.34/bu, down 19¢ from $5.53 last week but still holding a portion of the
recent  three-week  rebound  as  Plains  dryness  persisted  and  speculative  shorts  trimmed  exposure.  The
Dec/Mar spread narrowed to 14.5¢ (72 percent of carry), while FOB Russia offers moved up $2/MT and
U.S. HRW premiums held firm. Weekly inspections were 290k mt (down 17 percent week on week), with
Indonesia and Mexico the top destinations. Analysts WASDE consensus sees little change to U.S. S&D but
a modest bump in Russian export estimates and tighter Argentine production outlook.

Soybeans – Futures Edge Higher on Verbal Framework Chatter, Gulf Basis Diverges
January soybeans rose 8¢ to $11.32/bu (about +0.7 percent), supported by market chatter about a U.S.–
China verbal purchase framework, though soybeans were not included in China’s tariff rollback and no
confirmed U.S. purchases have been reported. CIF bids were flat to two cents softer w/w as barge freight
in the Lower Miss rebounded, while processor bids in the East remained steady to firm. The Jan/Mar
spread widened to 14¢ (72 percent of full carry), consistent with steady crush margins despite subdued
export pull. Export inspections dipped to 1.09 MMT, up from 0.984 last week. Reuters expects USDA to
hold production and crush unchanged in the Nov WASDE, with a wide range of views on Chinese imports
(98–105 MMT).

Macro/Policy – Crude Weakens, Dollar Flat, China Tariff Cuts Drive Sentiment
Crude oil eased to around $59/bbl (down roughly four percent w/w), weighing on biofuel margins but
offering freight relief. The U.S. Dollar Index held at 99.6, while the BRL slipped slightly to 5.27 and the
ARS drifted to 1,410 officials. Markets absorbed China’s Nov 10 tariff suspension on U.S. corn and wheat
(soybeans remain under a 10–13 percent duty) and unconfirmed reports of a verbal framework for 12
MMT of late-2025 U.S. soybean purchases, which has not yet shown up in USDA daily sales.

Weather – U.S. Harvest Wraps, Central Brazil Sees Patchy Relief

Harvest was effectively complete as of Nov 10, with corn near 92 percent and soybeans near 96 percent
finished. Southern barge routes reopened with improved flow in the Lower Mississippi, but persistent
dryness remains in central Mato Grosso and Goiás. Rains early in the week improved seeding pace, but
deeper soil moisture deficits linger. In Argentina, cooler, dry weather aided corn planting and preserved
subsoil reserves, while wheat entered fill under mostly favorable conditions.

What’s Next (Nov 13 – Nov 19):

WASDE (Nov 14): Watch for any yield revisions or China import shifts. Reuters median estimates show
steady corn/soy production but potential +75 mbu corn exports and slightly lower soybean ending stocks.

River Freight Trends: Monitor Cairo–Memphis freight index after recent barge draft improvements;
further relief could lift CIF basis.

Brazil Rainfall Outlook: Any confirmation of a dry bias through Nov 20 could accelerate speculative soy
and meal buying.

Export Sales Catch-Up: USDA’s backlog of sales data may reveal stronger soy activity in early
November, key for detecting any shift in China’s forward purchase behavior.

Ethanol Margins: With crude slipping, next EIA data (Nov 13) will indicate if weekly run rates remain
above 1.123 mbpd.

China Buying Pace: Given the unconfirmed verbal framework, any actual purchase volume or shipment
timing (Q4 vs Q1) will be crucial for basis and board strength.

Corn Market Deep Dive

Pre-WASDE Positioning: The trade enters Friday’s WASDE expecting minimal change to U.S. corn
supply but modest strength on the demand side. According to the Reuters poll conducted through
November 10–12, the average analyst forecast holds yield at 183.5 bu/acre and production at 15.557
billion bushels. Ending stocks are seen near 2.100 billion bushels, with the key expected change being a
+75 mbu increase in exports to 2.300 billion, justified by strong inspections and firm Gulf basis. Ethanol
use is projected flat at 5.4 billion bushels, with no consensus for feed or residual adjustments. On the
global side, trade looks for Russia’s crop at 59 MMT, Argentina around 52 MMT, Brazil at 132.8 MMT,
and China’s imports near 7 MMT. If realized, the report would validate stronger front-end demand and
justify Dec/Mar firmness, though futures may need actual Chinese demand or South American weather
stress to extend higher. Market tone remains balanced, not bearish, but lacking a fresh bullish trigger

Soybean Market Deep Dive

Pre-WASDE Positioning: The trade expects few changes to the U.S. soybean balance sheet in the Nov
14 WASDE. Reuters’ survey shows the average yield estimate at 53.0 bu/acre (unchanged from
October), keeping production at 4.300 billion bushels. Analysts see 2025/26 ending stocks at 304 million
bushels, with no change to crush (2.400 billion) or exports (1.800 billion) in most forecasts. The range of
estimates is narrow and reflects little pressure for adjustment without new China sales or a major yield
revision. Globally, Brazil’s crop is expected to stay near 165 MMT despite early-season dryness, while
Argentina’s 50 MMT outlook may come under scrutiny in future reports. China’s import forecast is
expected to hold near 100 MMT. The market bias is neutral to slightly supportive, with few catalysts
unless USDA surprises on exports or if traders front-run dryness in Mato Grosso.

Futures & Spreads: January 2026 soybeans closed at $11.32/bu, gaining 8¢ on the week after posting a
one-year high the prior Friday. The Jan/Mar spread widened to 14¢ carry, equal to 72 percent of full
commercial carry (6 percent interest, $0.05/bu/month), reflecting a well-supplied front end with active
crushers but soft export pull. Trade volume stayed steady, and volatility remained contained, suggesting
commercial buying rather than fund re-entry.

Products & Crush: Board crush margins remained positive with processors still favored by strong meal
basis and seasonal oil yield improvements. Soybean oil traded near 50.6¢/lb, tracking weaker crude oil,
while soymeal prices hovered around $322/short ton. Gross crush margins stayed above seasonal
averages. NOPA’s official October report was not released within the window, but private estimates
pegged crush above 200 mbu, up roughly 5 percent from last year.

Funds & Positioning: CFTC data remain unavailable due to the shutdown, but private positioning
estimates show managed money net long around 30k contracts, up roughly 8k w/w, as traders added
light longs on China tariff optimism and Brazilian weather risk. CME open interest increased modestly,
with most growth concentrated in the Jan/Mar spread. The curve remains well-behaved, with no inversion

WASDE. Reuters’ survey shows analysts forecasting ending stocks near 862 million bushels (USDA Oct:
844), with no adjustments expected to production or demand lines. HRW planting progress and dryness
remain background risks but are unlikely to prompt supply-side revisions of this cycle. Globally, Pre-
WASDE polls point to slightly higher Russian and Argentine wheat crops versus USDA October, though
most expect USDA to move gradually rather than fully matching private estimates. No changes are
anticipated for EU or Australia. World ending stocks are forecast at 266 MMT (Oct: 269), indicating a flat
tone. Trade attention remains centered on Black Sea logistics and Southern Hemisphere weather, not
U.S. shifts.

cents to $5.19/bu, while Minneapolis HRS slipped 2.2¢ to $5.69/bu. Volatility stayed near 20 percent and
open interest was steady, reinforcing that the move was largely technical rather than fundamentally
driven.

Key Fundamentals & Reports (Nov 6 – Nov 12)

Government Data & Reporting: Federal operations remained paused early in the week, delaying USDA
and CFTC data releases amid the extended shutdown. The regular Crop Progress update was not
published on Nov 10 due to the Veterans Day schedule, leaving harvest progress to private estimates.
WASDE and Crop Production remain on schedule for release Friday, Nov 14, alongside updated CFTC
Commitments of Traders through Nov 5. Most private forecasts and trade positions were set in
anticipation of that report, limiting new directional inputs.

Crop Progress (estimated as of Nov 2): Industry surveys and state updates suggest U.S. corn harvest
near 92 percent complete and soybeans roughly 96 percent, both ahead of five-year averages (81 and 88
percent, respectively). Producers in Illinois, Iowa, and Minnesota are largely finished, with only northern
fringes of the Corn Belt still active. Winter wheat planting was effectively complete, with emergence
estimated near two-thirds to three-quarters of intended acreage. Dryness persisted in western Kansas
and Oklahoma, maintaining slight stress in early HRW stands.

Export Inspections (USDA FGIS – week ending Oct 30, published Nov 3): Corn inspections reached
1.42 MMT (–17 percent w/w), continuing to outpace last year’s pace by nearly 76 percent. Soybean
inspections fell to 1.09 MMT, the weakest in five weeks, reflecting limited Chinese pull and ongoing
competition from Brazil. Wheat loadings totaled 290k tonnes (–7.4 percent w/w), concentrated at the Gulf
and PNW but still below seasonal norms. Aggregate flow confirms solid corn performance, subdued soy
export pace, and persistently weak wheat competitiveness.

Export Sales (USDA FAS, not published): Weekly sales remained unpublished through Nov 12, though
private trackers cited light new soybean sales to “unknown” destinations, likely China, and steady corn
commitments to Mexico and Japan. No USDA daily flash sales were reported. Market participants expect
catch-up data releases next week, possibly showing backlogged soybean bookings from late October.

Ethanol Production & Stocks (EIA – week ending Oct 31 / published Nov 5): Ethanol output stayed
unchanged at 1.123 mbpd. Stocks rose to 22.7 million barrels (+0.3 million w/w), reflecting steady
blending and seasonal drawdown. The implied corn grind was around 100 mbu, consistent with firm
ethanol margins and processor demand across the Western Corn Belt.

NOPA Crush: No new data were published within the window. Private estimates continued to point toward
another  historically  strong  month,  with  October  crush  seen  above  200  mbu,  supported  by  firm  soymeal
demand and high board crush margins. Eastern Corn Belt processors maintained strong bids, confirming
steady throughput.

CFTC Commitments of Traders: No CFTC reports were released due to the ongoing federal shutdown,
leaving the market without official visibility into managed money positioning. Traders relied on private
estimates and CME open interest changes, which pointed to only light adjustments across the complex.

Weather & Crop Conditions

United States
Crop Progress & Rainfall: Most harvest activity wrapped up across the Midwest under open skies and
warm temperatures. Private estimates place corn harvest around 92 percent complete and soybeans
near 96 percent, both ahead of normal pace. Light showers early in the week helped replenish surface
moisture in parts of the Ohio and Lower Mississippi Valleys, improving barge draft conditions but having
little agronomic effect. In the Plains, winter wheat establishment remains mixed, topsoil moisture deficits
persist in western Kansas and Oklahoma, where much of the region received 10-20 mm of precipitation
over the past two weeks.

River Levels & Logistics: The Mississippi River system continued to stabilize following late-October rains.
Gauge readings at Memphis and Baton Rouge rose modestly, allowing standard barge drafts to resume
and freight rates to hold near $19.53/ton on the Cairo–Memphis route. Traffic flows normalized, but carriers
reported  that  water  levels  remain  below  average,  keeping  transport  margins  tight  and  delivery  windows
sensitive to rainfall patterns.

Temperatures:  Temperatures  stayed  seasonally  mild,  with  daytime  highs  in  the  60s–70s°F  across  the

down but accelerated topsoil moisture depletion in the HRW belt. No frost damage or crop-quality issues
were reported.

dryness across the Plains. Temperatures are expected to remain above normal, further slowing soil

Synthesis & Outlook

The second week of November marked the first real transition point for the grain complex since early
September, as USDA data remained partial amid the ongoing shutdown and the November WASDE
approached. After months of trading on basis, inspections, and freight clues, the market now faces the
return of official figures that could validate or contradict the story physical markets have been telling.
Futures stayed narrow through Wednesday, December corn near $4.36/bu, January soybeans at
$11.27/bu, and December Chicago wheat around $5.34/bu, as traders avoided new positions ahead of
the release. Spreads stayed firm but orderly, with corn Dec/Mar at 72 percent of full carry, soy Jan/Mar
near 72 percent, and wheat Dec/Mar at 72 percent. CIF corn basis pushed higher, processors held firm
across soy and ethanol channels, and volatility remained subdued. The market tone was one of calm
before confirmation, as traders braced for the first official U.S. balance sheets in nearly two months.

Speculative flows remained minimal, with managed money trimming shorts in corn and wheat and adding
modest longs in soybeans. CME open interest rose slightly but continued to show a commercial-led
market dominated by hedging and spread roll activity rather than speculative conviction. The return of
data restores transparency, but it also resets risk: after relying on anecdotal strength from CIF, crush, and
barge freight, the market will once again anchor to measurable fundamentals. If the WASDE confirms
resilient U.S. demand, the tone of quiet stability could give way to renewed directional conviction; if not,
the calm of recent weeks may be exposed as complacency.
