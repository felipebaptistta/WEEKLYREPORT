Corn – Supply Reset Pressures Futures, but Export
Flow Continues to Anchor the Front End
March corn settled at $4.22/bu on January 13, down nearly
25¢ on Monday, as the January USDA reports finalized a
record 2025 crop and lifted it expectation for new-crop US
ending stocks to 2.227 bbu. The scale of the production
revision (+269 mbu) forced futures sharply lower, but nearby
structure softened only modestly. The Mar/May spread held
near an eight-cent carry (about 40 percent of full carry),
signaling that commercial interest in nearby ownership
remains intact despite a looser balance sheet. Export
execution continues to validate that signal, with inspections

confirming strong early-season movement and cumulative shipments running well ahead of last year.
January data effectively removed the pre-report tightening narrative, yet steady export flow and firm
replacement values continue to limit downside follow-through.

Wheat – Global Supply Overhang Dominates, While US Movement Remains Orderly

March SRW wheat closed at $5.13/bu, down six cents w/w, with structure reflecting a storage-driven
market rather than nearby stress. The Mar/May spread held near a 10¢ carry (roughly 35 percent of full
carry), consistent with ample supply and limited urgency for immediate ownership. USDA’s January
WASDE lifted US ending stocks to 926 mbu and raised global ending stocks to 278.3 mmt, driven
primarily by larger crops in Argentina and Russia. Despite the heavier balance sheet, export execution
remains supportive at the margin, with weekly inspections at 0.32 mmt and cumulative shipments running
about eight percent ahead of last year. Wheat continues to trade the contrast between burdensome
global availability and a physical market that has not materially weakened.

Soybeans – Export Downgrades Shift the Burden to Domestic Crush as Brazil Advances

March soybeans settled at $10.43/bu on January 13, down 13¢ on the week, as January WASDE
reduced US exports to 1.575 bbu and raised ending stocks to 350 mbu. At the same time, domestic crush
was increased to 2.57 bbu, partially offsetting weaker export demand. Structure reflects that balance, with
the Mar/May spread holding near a 10¢ carry (about 45 percent of full carry), signaling adequate nearby
supply without forced liquidation. While weekly inspections showed episodic strength, cumulative
shipments remain roughly 40 percent behind last year, reinforcing that domestic crush, not exports, now
carries the balance sheet as Brazil’s record 178 mmt crop advances.

Macro / Policy – Dollar Slightly Firmer, BRL Weaker; Energy Quiet

Macro conditions remained largely neutral over the past week, allowing grain markets to focus squarely
on the January USDA data. The US dollar index held near 98.95, while the Brazilian real weakened
modestly toward 5.37 per dollar, supporting Brazilian farmer sales at the margin without materially altering
global trade flows. Energy markets were range-bound, with WTI crude trading near $60 per barrel,
stabilizing biofuel economics but not generating new demand signals for corn or soybean oil. With no
material policy shifts or macro shocks during the period, price discovery across grains was driven
primarily by balance sheet adjustments rather than cross-asset influences.

Weather – Warm/Dry US Pattern; Argentina Dryness vs Brazil’s Generally Favorable Setup

In the United States, a warm and generally dry pattern persisted across key growing regions. NOAA’s 6–
10 day outlook continued to show above-normal temperatures and below-normal precipitation across the
Plains and Midwest, minimizing winterkill risk for winter wheat but limiting soil moisture recharge in parts of
the  HRW  belt.  River  conditions  remained  stable,  with  no  major  weather-related  disruptions  to  grain
movement.

In South America, conditions remained favorable for Brazil, reinforcing expectations for a record soybean
crop. Core growing areas continued to receive adequate rainfall, supporting crop development as harvest
advances, with Mato Grosso harvest estimated near eight percent complete. In contrast, dryness in parts
of  Argentina  intensified,  particularly  across  southern  production  areas,  raising  localized  concerns  for
soybeans during the critical pod-setting stage of development. While wheat production is already locked in
at  a  record  27.5  mmt,  Argentine  weather  remains  a  key  variable  for  oilseed  and  corn  yield  risk  moving
forward.

Corn Market Deep Dive

Post-WASDE Balance Sheet: The January USDA data delivered a decisive supply reset for corn,
anchored by USDA’s final production estimate for the 2025 crop confirming a record 17.021 bbu crop on
a 186.5 bpa yield across 91.3 million harvested acres. USDA’s December 1 Grain Stocks report
reinforced that outcome, with total inventories reported at 13.28 bbu, up 10 percent year over year,
pointing to larger-than-expected availability entering the first quarter. USDA’s January WASDE
predictions translated those findings directly into the 2025/26 balance sheet, lifting beginning stocks to
1.551 bbu and raising production by 269 mbu from December. On the demand side, stronger September–

Even with the feed adjustment, supply growth overwhelmed use, pushing US ending stocks up 198 mbu

demand can absorb the record crop than by further supply surprises. Absent a meaningful acceleration in
exports or a weather-driven shock in South America, the January data firmly re-establish corn as a
supply-led market.

Futures & Spreads: Futures adjusted quickly to the revised balance sheet, with March corn settling at
$4.22/bu on January 13, down 25¢ on the week, primarily due to the drop on Monday. Forward structure
softened in a controlled manner rather than collapsing. The Mar/May spread widened to an eight-cent
carry, roughly 40 percent of full carry, reflecting improved nearby availability without forcing full storage
economics. Structure suggests urgency to own nearby corn has eased, but the market is not discounting
supply aggressively enough to signal distress.

Funds & Positioning: Managed money positioning in corn shifted modestly following the January data
release. On a futures-only basis, funds held a small net short of roughly 13,000 CBOT contracts as of
January 6, reflecting a reduction in long exposure alongside only limited short-covering. While positioning
has improved from late December, it is not yet consistent with aggressive bearish conviction nor with
meaningful short-covering pressure. As a result, corn remains less sensitive to fund-driven volatility,
leaving spreads, basis, and export replacement values as the primary near-term drivers.

Cash & Basis: Cash markets reflected the same balance between increased supply and steady demand.
Gulf basis firmed modestly, with CIF corn trading near +84¢ over March and FOB values around +110¢,
signaling continued exporter interest despite lower futures. Interior bids were generally steady to slightly
weaker following the Grain Stocks report, consistent with a market that is better supplied but not
burdensome. Logistics remained orderly, with stable river conditions and freight holding near recent
levels, allowing basis to cushion the futures decline rather than amplify it.

Export Sales & Inspections: Export execution remains the key stabilizing factor. Corn inspections
totaled 1.49 mmt for the week ending January 8, lifting cumulative shipments to roughly 24.1 mmt,
approximately 60 percent ahead of last year’s pace. That performance supports USDA’s decision to leave
exports unchanged in January despite the supply revisions. Daily sales activity during the period,
including a 310,000 mt sale to unknown destinations and an additional 204,000 mt to South Korea,
reinforced the pattern that demand re-emerges on price breaks even as weekly sales volumes moderate
seasonally.

Global Context: On the global side, January WASDE added further supply weight, raising world corn
production to 1,296.0 mmt, up 13.1 mmt from December, with notable increases in the United States and
China. World ending stocks climbed to 290.9 mmt, reinforcing the perception of ample global availability
and limiting the scope for balance-sheet-driven rallies. With global supply comfortable, marginal price
discovery increasingly depends on execution and weather rather than structural shortages.

So What? The January data marked a definitive shift in the corn narrative from tightening risk to supply
management. Futures repriced lower, but spreads, exports, and basis behavior suggest the downside
remains disciplined rather than disorderly. Record production and higher stocks cap upside potential, yet
strong export execution and firm replacement values continue to establish a floor. Corn is now trading a

Soybean Market Deep Dive

Post-WASDE Balance Sheet: January USDA data clarified the soybean balance sheet by decisively
shifting the demand burden away from exports and toward domestic processing. USDA’s final 2025 crop
production estimate confirmed US soybean output at 4.262 bbu on a 53.0 bpa yield across 80.4 million
harvested acres, USDA modestly refined its acreage estimate higher from its December estimate.
USDA’s assessment of December 1 grain stocks reinforced a looser starting point, with total stocks
reported at 3.290 bbu, above last year and slightly above trade expectations. USDA’s January WASDE
incorporated those findings while cutting its prediction of new-crop exports by 60 mbu to 1.575 bbu,
reflecting a sluggish shipment pace and intensifying South American competition, while raising its
2025/26 US crush prediction by 15 mbu to 2.570 bbu on resilient processor demand.

Despite the anticipated increase in US new-crop crush, total use declined as the export cut dominated,
lifting USDA’s estimate of new-crop US ending stocks by 60 mbu to 350 mbu and pushing stocks-to-use
up to 8.2 percent. That adjustment marked a clear loosening from December and removed much of the
residual tightening narrative that had been tied to export assumptions. The balance sheet is now firmly re-
centered on domestic demand strength rather than export recovery, leaving price direction increasingly
dependent on sustained crush performance and South American weather risk rather than a rebound in
US shipment pace.

Futures & Spreads: Futures adjusted modestly relative to corn, with March soybeans settling at
$10.43/bu on January 14, down 13¢ week over week. However, prices remain in the upper end of the
extended trading range established between $10.20 and $10.90 per bushel that the market traded within
for the majority of 2025. Structure reflected adequate nearby supply without distress, as the Mar/May
spread held near an 10¢ carry, roughly 45 percent of full carry. The curve continues to balance softer
nearby export demand against resilient domestic crush, signaling that concerns about demand
outstripping supply have eased decreasing the urgency for ownership that drove prices up to almost
$11.75 in mid-November has eased. However, the market is not pricing excess supply aggressively,
leaving prices likely rangebound until something changes in the outlook for supply. As a result, the
soybean market structure remains functional rather than punitive following the January revisions.

Products & Crush: Soybean products provided steady underlying support, with both meal and oil firming
modestly on the week. The latest available NOPA crush data (November) printed 216.0 million bushels,
exceeding last year’s pace and reinforcing the view that domestic processing demand remains resilient
despite slower export shipments. Crush margins remain positive and continue to incentivize run rates
near capacity, keeping processor bids engaged across the interior. USDA’s changes to its predictions in
the January WASDE highlight USDA’s crush projection as the most defensible demand line item, raising
the question of whether USDA offsets any export caution by leaning more heavily on domestic processing
rather than reducing total use.

Cash & Basis: Cash markets softened modestly alongside futures, particularly in export channels. CIF
NOLA soybean bids eased to roughly +95¢ over January, while FOB Gulf values slipped to near +100¢,
reflecting reduced urgency from exporters as Brazil advances toward peak availability. Interior basis was
steadier in processing regions, supported by firm crush margins and consistent plant demand. Basis
behavior continues to highlight the divergence between export weakness and domestic demand
resilience.

Funds & Positioning: In soybeans, managed money continues to hold a net long, but positioning has
moderated meaningfully. As of January 6, funds were net long approximately 66,000 CBOT futures-only
contracts, after a sizable reduction in gross length over the past week. That adjustment aligns with
weaker export signals and advancing Brazilian harvest pressure. While positioning is no longer crowded,
it remains vulnerable to additional long liquidation if demand does not improve, keeping fund flows
reactive rather than supportive.

Export Sales & Inspections: Export execution remains the weakest leg of the US soybean balance
sheet. Weekly inspections totaled 1.53 mmt for the week ending January 8, but cumulative shipments
reached only about 19.1 mmt, roughly 40 percent behind last year’s pace. The lag justified the January
export downgrade and reinforced the shift in USDA’s demand assumptions. Last Thursday, USDA
reported that weekly export sales for the period totaled 877,900 mt, while cumulative commitments
remained near 32.7 mmt, approximately 30 percent below last year. While prior Chinese purchases
continue to provide episodic support, shipment pace remains insufficient to offset the growing influence of
South American supply.

Global Context: Globally, January WASDE reinforced the supply-heavy backdrop. World soybean
production was raised to 425.7 mmt, up 3.1 mmt from December, driven primarily by Brazil, where
production was increased to a record 178.0 mmt. World ending stocks rose to 124.4 mmt, adding weight
to the forward outlook and increasing competitive pressure on US exports. As Brazil’s harvest
accelerates, global availability becomes a dominant factor in shaping nearby trade flows and limiting
upside for US prices absent a demand shock.

So What? January data repositioned soybeans as a market supported by domestic crush rather than
export strength. Higher ending stocks and slower shipment pace cap rallies, while structure and
processor demand continue to provide a floor. With Brazil’s record crop looming and global stocks rising,
soybeans remain range-bound with a defensive tone, requiring either a material shift in export flow or a
weather-driven disruption to change the sentiment of the market. Until then, price discovery is likely to
remain anchored between resilient domestic demand and expanding global supply.

Wheat Market Deep Dive

Post-WASDE Balance Sheet: January USDA data delivered only marginal numerical changes to the US
wheat balance sheet, but reinforced an already heavy supply backdrop. USDA held its assessment of the
2025 US wheat crop steady at 1.985 bbu on a 53.3 bpa yield across 37.2 million harvested acres,
confirming that production was not the source of adjustment this month. Instead the changes were driven
by USDA’s quarterly Grain Stocks report. USDA’s December 1 inventory assessment pointed to weaker-
than-expected disappearance early in the marketing year, prompting its analysts to cut estimates of
2025/26 feed and residual use by 20 mbu to 100 mbu in the January WASDE. With exports and food use
left unchanged, that demand-side revision pushed US ending stocks up to 926 mbu.

The resulting stocks-to-use ratio climbed to 45.6 percent, underscoring how little balance-sheet support
prices have absent an external shock. While the domestic changes were modest, they validated the
broader narrative that supply continues to outrun demand. Globally, that tone was reinforced by higher
production in key exporters, with world output raised to 842.2 mmt and ending stocks lifted to 278.3 mmt,
led by Argentina and Russia. Taken together, January data left wheat firmly anchored in a surplus-driven
environment, where rallies remain dependent on positioning or headline risk rather than fundamentals
tightening.

International Cash & Trade Flows

Brazil: Brazil remains the dominant supplier in global grain trade as harvest advances. ANEC estimates
January soybean exports near 3.7 mmt, while corn shipments are projected around 3.27 mmt, reflecting
continued strength from the prior safrinha cycle. FOB Santos soybean values were quoted near $420/mt,
while corn FOB traded around $220/mt, preserving Brazil’s pricing advantage into Asia and the Middle
East. Harvest progress reached roughly 8 percent in Mato Grosso, with logistics reported as fluid and no
major congestion at key ports. Adequate rainfall across core producing areas continues to reinforce
expectations for Brazil’s record 178 mmt soybean crop, keeping Brazil firmly positioned as the preferred
origin early in the year

Argentina: Argentina’s export flows remain active but increasingly weather-sensitive. Up-River corn
values were quoted near $218/mt, while soymeal offers traded around $345/mt, supporting Argentina’s
role as a key supplier of feed ingredients. Wheat production is locked at a record 27.5 mmt, with exports
flowing steadily following harvest completion. Dryness in southern production areas remains a point of
monitoring for corn and soybeans, but export channels continue to function normally, aided by a weaker
peso and competitive FOB pricing relative to US origin.

China: Chinese buying behavior remains tactical rather than structural. Cumulative US soybean
purchases tied to China are estimated near 10 mmt, largely interpreted as state-directed procurement
rather than broad commercial restocking. Domestic futures reflected stable demand, with Dalian corn
trading near ¥2,395/mt, but no sustained arbitrage window has opened to materially accelerate US grain
imports. On corn and wheat, Chinese participation remains limited despite elevated domestic prices,
keeping US export opportunities episodic rather than persistent.

EU / Black Sea: Black Sea origins continue to set the global price ceiling. Russian wheat FOB values
were reported near $230–235/mt, maintaining a competitive edge in key import markets. Ukrainian corn
offers near $173–175/mt continue to pressure EU feed markets, while French wheat remains less
competitive in export tenders. With export corridors functioning and supplies ample, Black Sea flows
remain a central constraint on US and EU export competitiveness.

Key Fundamentals & Reports (Jan 8 – Jan 13)

USDA Crop Production – Final 2025 (released Jan 12): USDA finalized the 2025 US corn crop at
17.021 bbu, driven by a 186.5 bpa national yield across 91.3 million harvested acres, confirming a record
outcome and exceeding late-year expectations. Soybean production was finalized at 4.262 bbu on a 53.0
bpa yield and 80.4 million harvested acres, while US wheat production held steady at 1.985 bbu with a
53.3 bpa yield on 37.2 million harvested acres. The final data removed residual uncertainty around yield
and acreage and set a firmer baseline for January balance-sheet revisions across the complex.

USDA Grain Stocks – December 1, 2025 (released Jan 12): December 1 stocks confirmed ample on-
hand supplies entering the first quarter. US corn stocks were reported at 13.28 bbu, up 10 percent year
over year, implying stronger early-season disappearance but also larger available supply. Soybean
stocks totaled 3.29 bbu, up six percent y/y, while wheat stocks reached 1.675 bbu, up 7 percent y/y. The
stocks data played a key role in shaping January WASDE feed/residual and use assumptions, particularly
for corn and wheat.

USDA WASDE – January 2026: January WASDE absorbed the final production and stocks data,
delivering a clear supply-side reset. For corn, beginning stocks were raised to 1.551 bbu, production
increased by 268 mbu to 17.021 bbu, feed and residual use was lifted to 6.200 bbu, and ending stocks
rose to 2.227 bbu, pushing stocks-to-use to 13.6 percent. Soybeans saw exports cut to 1.575 bbu, crush
raised to 2.570 bbu, and ending stocks lifted to 350 mbu (stocks-to-use 8.2 percent). Wheat adjustments
were more modest, with feed and residual lowered to 100 mbu and US ending stocks increased to 926
mbu, keeping stocks-to-use elevated at 45.6 percent. Globally, WASDE raised world ending stocks
across all three commodities, reinforcing a looser supply backdrop.

Export Inspections (USDA FGIS – week ending Jan 8): Export inspections provided a counterweight to
the heavier balance sheets. Corn inspections totaled 1.49 mmt, lifting cumulative shipments to roughly
950 mbu, approximately 60 percent ahead of last year. Soybean inspections reached 1.53 mmt, but
cumulative loadings remain near 19.1 mmt, roughly 40 percent behind last year, reinforcing USDA’s
export downgrade. Wheat inspections totaled 0.32 mmt, bringing season-to-date shipments to
approximately 16 mmt, about 8 percent ahead of last year, keeping the 900 mbu export forecast
defensible for now.

Export Sales & Daily Announcements (USDA FAS – latest through week ending Jan 1): Weekly export
sales reflected a mixed demand picture. Corn net sales totaled 377,600 mt, a slower pace but consistent
with strong execution. Soybean net sales reached 877,900 mt, while wheat sales totaled 118,700 mt.

Weather & Crop Conditions

United States
Weather remains a secondary input for US grains at this stage. Conditions across the Plains and Midwest
stayed warm and generally dry, keeping winterkill risk low and allowing logistics to operate without
interruption. With harvest complete and no active fieldwork underway, weather has not introduced new
supply or demand signals. River systems remained stable, and there were no weather-related constraints
on grain movement during the period. For the US, weather currently functions as a neutral backdrop
rather than a market driver.

Brazil
Brazilian weather continues to reinforce confidence in large production rather than introduce risk. Core
soybean areas across the Center-West maintained favorable moisture, supporting steady crop

pollination windows, the next 10–15 days carry disproportionate yield sensitivity if dryness persists. At this

entered dormancy without widespread stress, and no major weather disruptions were reported across
secondary exporting regions. Australia’s wheat harvest concluded without logistical issues, and no new
weather-related threats emerged during the week.

Synthesis & Outlook

January’s USDA data bundle decisively shifted the grain market narrative from late-2025 tightening
concerns to a supply-managed environment. Final crop production and December 1 stocks confirmed that
US corn and soybean availability is larger than previously assumed, while global balance sheets were
also revised higher. Futures adjusted lower to reflect that reality, but the reaction has been measured
rather than disorderly. Structure, basis, and export execution continue to signal that commercial demand
remains present, even as balance sheets loosen.

Corn sits at the center of that tension. Record production and a 2.227 bbu carryout cap upside, yet strong
inspections, steady ethanol grind, and firm Gulf replacement values continue to define a floor. The market
has repriced supply but has not lost its physical anchor. As long as export flow remains consistent,
downside extensions are likely to be incremental rather than impulsive.

Soybeans remain more internally divided. January WASDE shifted the burden of demand squarely toward
domestic crush, which continues to perform at historically strong levels, while export execution lags amid
Brazil’s advancing harvest. That split keeps soybeans range-bound: supported by processors,
constrained by global availability. Without a clear acceleration in exports or a material South American
disruption, price discovery is likely to remain defensive but orderly.

Wheat remains the heaviest balance sheet in the complex. Global production increases and rising stocks
continue to cap rallies, even as US shipment pace has quietly improved. Structure remains firmly in carry,
reinforcing storage rather than scarcity. Any near-term strength is likely to be positioning-driven rather
than fundamentally led, unless global supply assumptions are challenged.

Bullish triggers: Upside risk would require a meaningful shift in either demand execution or South
American supply. For corn, continued inspections at or above recent pace, combined with sustained
ethanol grind, would tighten the effective nearby balance despite a larger carryout. In soybeans, any
disruption to Argentina’s crop or a more persistent return of Chinese buying beyond tactical state
purchases would materially change the demand calculus. Wheat upside remains the most conditional,
relying on export flow surprises or supply friction in key exporting regions rather than US fundamentals
alone.

Bearish triggers: Further downside would likely emerge if global supply confidence increases rather than
erodes. For corn, a deceleration in export flow following the January repricing would expose the market to
wider carries and deeper storage economics. Soybeans remain vulnerable to Brazil’s harvest pressure if
logistics continue smoothly and export pace fails to improve. Wheat remains exposed to aggressive Black
Sea competition, with global stocks limiting the market’s ability to absorb additional supply without price
concession

In summary: With January’s supply reset now largely priced in, grain markets are transitioning back to a
phase where physical signals matter more than balance-sheet revisions. Basis behavior, spread
structure, and export execution will determine whether prices stabilize or drift. Risk remains two-sided, but
asymmetric: upside requires disruption, while downside depends on demand follow-through. Until one of
those conditions materializes, markets are likely to remain range-bound, disciplined, and driven by
commercial rather than speculative conviction.
