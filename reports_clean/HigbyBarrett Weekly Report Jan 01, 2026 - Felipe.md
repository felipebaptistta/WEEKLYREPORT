less than placement. Brazil’s Center-West has kept enough rainfall rhythm to support early stands, while
the South remains the swing zone where interruptions can still translate into premium as the crop
advances. Argentina remains generally supported on moisture, but timing is now the risk: any sustained
drying in the core belt would start to matter quickly as crops move deeper into their critical development
window.

Corn Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: Corn spent 2025 rotating between two realities: board-level comfort and cash-level
insistence. The year’s tone was set early when March futures pushed into the high $4.60s/low $4.70s
(mid-January) on risk premium and fund participation, but the market couldn’t sustain it as weather fear
faded and supply confidence rebuilt, by late June, the trade was probing the low-$4.20s ($4.18) again.
The back half of the year repeated the same lesson in smaller steps: futures struggled to trend, but
whenever the board tried to lean too bearish, the physical market tightened the conversation through
spreads, basis, and shipment follow-through.

Into the finish, March corn closed at $4.50 and Mar/May ended near –5¢ (33 percent of carry),

Price Trajectory and Volatility Patterns: The year’s price path ultimately behaved like a mean-reverting

practice, corn spent most of 2025 trading “in pockets,” where the board could drift but the front-end
structure and cash replacement values repeatedly defined where the market found traction.

Cash & Basis: Cash markets delivered the steadiest signals all year, and the close of 2025 captured that
resilience clearly. By late December, CIF Gulf bids lingered in the mid-70s over March (around +74¢),
buoyed by winter frictions and spot barge constraints that limited downside. Interior spots shone where
processors pulled hardest, with Western Corn Belt bids ranging from flat to a few cents under March.
National average basis ended around –30¢ under March, about 20¢ stronger than typical for the season,
mirroring how basis repeatedly propped up the front during 2025’s softer board moments.

Funds & Positioning: Speculative flows influenced 2025 but seldom overrode cash and spread cues.
The early-year bullish turn in funds sparked short-covering flurries on logistics or weather pops. By year-
end, managed money had swung to a modest net short (around –53k contracts, as of Dec 16), with
recent moves more about trimming longs than bold bets, balanced by rising producer/merchant hedges
that maintained the market’s equilibrium.

Export Sales & Inspections: Demand came down to execution over hype in 2025. Year-end inspections
provided a solid gauge: corn ran 1.74 MMT (Dec 18) and 1.30 MMT (Dec 25), lifting cumulative exports
past 25 MMT, on track for USDA’s 3.2 bbu outlook and up 66 percent y/y. Sales data, though lagged,
showed net sales around 1.2 MMT (week ending Dec 11), with Mexico, Japan, and Colombia as steady
takers, plus reliable PNW flows to Asia.

So What? Corn is stuck in a familiar stalemate: the board keeps leaning on “ample stocks” and South
America optimism, but the cash market still refuses to fully agree. Gulf basis continues to hold up, ethanol
grind stays active, and inspections have not rolled over in a way that matches a truly comfortable front
end, spreads are still doing most of the talking. Commercials look like they’re quietly extending coverage
rather than waiting for a deeper break, while specs remain relatively muted, keeping selling pressure
more controlled than it could be.

Soybean Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: Soybeans spent 2025 stuck in a recurring loop: rallies needed export proof, while
breaks kept finding a domestic floor. Early-year strength had January futures near the upper-$10.80s/low-
$10.90s ($10.94) on weather risk and headline optimism, but mid-year trade pressure pulled values
toward the low-$10.00s ($10.20 late June) as supply confidence rebuilt and Brazil regained control of the
forward export window. By year-end, the complex was still living inside that same tug-of-war, export
uncertainty overhead, but meal-led crush and cash strength keeping the front end from fully loosening.

January closed at $10.52 (–14¢ vs Dec 17) with Jan/Mar –6¢ (27 percent of carry) and Gulf CIF steady in
the low-90s over Jan (+93¢), consistent with a year where commercials stayed involved even when
futures lacked a demand catalyst. The clearest “why it mattered” signal in 2025 was the domestic engine:
NOPA’s record 216.0 mbu November crush captured how aggressively the system ran, while exports
remained the limiter with inspections still running 46 percent behind y/y on the season.

Price Trajectory and Volatility Patterns: Soybeans carried a higher risk premium than corn through
2025 because the market repeatedly had to price South American weather and demand uncertainty at the
same time. Implied volatility ended the year near 18 percent, elevated versus corn but still contained
compared with prior spikes, reflecting a market that stayed sensitive to headline turns yet struggled to
hold follow-through. The board repeatedly responded to the same triggers, Brazil rain placement,
shipment pace, and meal-led crush signals, creating sharp swings that often reverted once the catalyst
passed. Spec flows fueled ups and downs in 2025. Headline optimism, like US-China talks, piled on
length prone to unwinds on Brazil improvements. Year-end echoed that: rains and soft data left the setup
ripe for more liquidation, biasing the board down absent fresh sparks like South American hiccups or
demand revs.

Cash & Basis: Cash acted as the steady hand through 2025, evident again at close. CIF Gulf basis clung
to low-90s over January (around +93¢), propped by nearby needs and river tweaks. Processor bids mixed
but solid, Eastern Corn Belt spots near flat to premium in primes, interiors with discounts. National
average basis around –20¢ under January, firm for late December and true to the year’s motif: cash
resisted the board’s bear vibes often. Processor hunger stabilized the front amid export lulls.

Funds & Positioning: Funds drove much of soy’s narrative in 2025, with the complex holding “built-in
length” quick to shed on weather turns or export misses. Latest read: managed money heavily net long at
155k contracts (as of Dec 16), steady w/w. Less a weekly mover, more a closing state: it primes the board
for further unwinds on Brazil gains or US export softness. No sharp exit this time hints the dip was
fundamentals-first. Downside lingers if exports drag and South America cooperates; upside wants
catalysts like weather woes or China shifts.

Crush Margins & Domestic Demand: Crush anchored the complex in 2025, meal the powerhouse.
NOPA’s November hit 216.0 mbu, a record underscoring aggressive runs and meal uptake into year-end.
That echoed throughout: crushers bid through futures pressure. Oil lagged margins often, capping its
crush role, but meal strength kept plants humming. Overall economics held plants chasing coverage,
bolstering basis despite export weights.

Export Sales & Inspections: Exports capped rallies most of 2025, year-end data in line. Sales confirmed
to late November at 2.4 MMT, with 1.7 MMT to China (2025/26), but commitments remain materially
behind last year, focused on Mexico, smaller Asia over broad China buys. Flow stayed soft: inspections
0.75 MMT week ending late December, down w/w and 46 percent y/y cumulative at 15.4 MMT vs 28.7
MMT prior. China’s buys seemed reserve-tactical, clouding ahead and keeping the board touchy on
execution slips.

So What? Soybeans are still caught in the same tug-of-war: domestic crush is doing real work and the
cash market keeps proving it, but exports continue to be the cap on futures. Commercials remain steady
buyers and crushers are still bidding aggressively to secure nearby ownership, which is why basis and
spreads have stayed firm even when the board tries to leak lower.

The problem is the market still doesn’t have a clean catalyst on the demand side. Without a clearer signal
of sustained Chinese buying, or a weather-driven shift that meaningfully dents Brazil’s yield potential
futures remain vulnerable to pressure and liquidation on rallies. For the board to move higher in a durable
way, it likely takes either South America losing some production potential, or China demand improving
beyond “hand-to-mouth” coverage. Until then, the front end stays supported by crush/basis, while the
upside stays capped by export drag.

Wheat Market Deep Dive

Futures & Spreads: Wheat’s 2025 was a story of ceilings, not floors. Early-year pricing near the upper-
$5s ($5.70 mid-Jan) had room for weather-led pops, but the market repeatedly ran into the same
constraint: abundant exportable supply and a competitor complex (especially the Black Sea) willing to
defend market share. By late June, futures were back near the low-$5.30s, and the year’s final stretch
kept expressing the same pattern, short-lived rallies, limited follow-through, and a steady reliance on
niche demand lanes rather than broad tender dominance. Chicago March ended at $5.19 (–4¢ vs Dec 17)
with Mar/May –12¢ (75 percent of carry), while US shipment execution remained a stabilizer with
cumulative inspections 22 percent ahead y/y.

Price Trajectory and Volatility Patterns: Wheat’s volatility stayed relatively muted into year-end (implied
vol near 13 percent), consistent with a market dominated by global supply competition rather than
domestic scarcity. Most 2025 moves were less about sustained US fundamentals and more about
whether Black Sea offers, freight, and FX shifts temporarily opened (or closed) competitiveness windows.
That kept the board reactive and range-bound, with rallies struggling to extend unless a clear disruption
forced repricing.

Cash & Basis: Wheat cash in 2025 mostly cleared on dips, not frantic owns, with late December
mirroring that calm. Basis stayed stable, aligning with a carry-heavy board and subdued nearby push.
Gulf SRW FOB in the mid-$6s/bu ($220–$235/MT), premium to Black Sea and curbing US tender wins.
Southern Plains HRW firmed a bit on feed swaps, while PNW soft white stayed supported by consistent
Southeast Asia pulls, a standout US Lane all year. National basis remained typical of excess: –60¢ to –
72¢ under March for SRW, –74¢ under KC March for HRW, with movement opportunistic over systemic.

Export Sales & Inspections: Exports let the US edge in niches in 2025, seldom leading the world book.
Latest weekly net sales were 450k MT (week ending Dec 11), steady if not breakout. Flow was brighter:
weekly inspections 302k MT ending late December, season-to-date 15.06 MMT (+22 percent y/y). PNW
soft white to Southeast Asia shone, Gulf HRW held steady, decent runs, yet priced above
Russia/Argentina, keeping the US program tactical rather than market-making.

Funds & Positioning: Specs capped wheat’s upside in 2025, amplifying supply bears. Managed money
in Chicago SRW remained net short at roughly –49k contracts (as of Dec 16), leaving room for short-
covering rallies on any catalyst even as the broader surplus keeps follow-through limited. The setup is still
bearish by structure, but the short base means wheat doesn’t need a full bull story to pop, just a trigger
that forces covering.

So What? Wheat closed 2025 under the weight of a global surplus, with Russia and Argentina still setting
the ceiling and keeping US wheat boxed into premium, niche demand rather than market-making export
flows. US execution improved enough to provide a cushion at times, but the structural headwinds have
remained the same, wide price gaps versus competitors and plenty of exportable supply that makes it
hard for rallies to extend. That’s why the year’s better tells were often structure and basis, not the flat
price: we started in the upper $5s ($5.70 mid-Jan), bled into the low $5s ($5.30 late-Jun), and finished
near the mid-$5s ($5.19 late-Dec).

The market is still operating in surplus mode, and until Russia/Argentina offers lose their grip, through
logistics disruption, weather risk, or a meaningful currency shift, futures are likely to stay anchored near
recent lows. The one consistent upside risk is positioning: specs are short enough that wheat doesn’t
need a full bull story to rally, it just needs a catalyst that forces short covering. Until that happens,
commercials are more likely to buy breaks than chase breakouts, and the curve/basis will remain the
cleanest read on where US demand is absorbing wheat despite the global overhang.

US Cash Market & Basis

Corn: Corn basis demonstrated notable resilience throughout 2025, often outpacing board expectations,
and wrapped the year maintaining that cash-driven firmness. CIF Gulf bids closed in the mid-70s over
March (around +74¢), historically robust for December, bolstered by persistent winter logistics challenges
and constrained spot barge supply that intermittently hampered Gulf inflows and upheld premiums.
Interior markets were most robust where processor demand dominated, with Western Corn Belt ethanol
needs solidifying local spots. Iowa and Nebraska bids typically ranged flat to slightly under the board for
immediate delivery, as facilities vied for bushels amid minimal farmer sales in regions with strong basis
and uninspiring flat prices. National average basis concluded around –30¢ under March, roughly 20¢
above the five-year norm, encapsulating 2025's overarching dynamic: despite paper carryout signaling
comfort, end-user rivalry for prompt ownership consistently tightened the front and curbed any loose
tendencies.

Soybeans: Soybean basis mirrored the complex's year-long balancing act, export shortfalls frequently
weighed in, yet domestic forces reliably steadied the front. CIF Gulf barges for January held in the low-
90s over futures (around +93¢) with scant retreat amid board softness, underscoring blended support
from nearby pull, river dynamics, and hesitant sellers. Processor bids in prime Eastern Corn Belt areas
remained among the firmest, with spot premiums echoing vigorous soymeal demand and urgent crush
requirements. River basis saw slight gains as barge flows normalized, though freight volatility lingered as
a pivotal element into Q4 close. National average basis settled near –20¢ under January, about a dime
stronger than usual, and the setup signaled ongoing commercial eagerness to hold nearby stocks despite
sluggish exports. Brazilian forwards stayed cheaper heading into Q1, tempering Gulf upsides, but 2025's
key takeaway endured: crushers consistently bid to prevent front-end erosion, even as futures remained
exposed.

Wheat: Wheat cash markets held steady yet aimless for much of 2025, in line with a carry-oriented
landscape where worldwide rivalry set ceilings and US wheat targeted selective channels. Gulf SRW FOB
values lingered in the low-$6s/bu ($220–$235/MT equiv.), sustaining premiums over Black Sea rivals and
restricting US tender participation. Russian 12.5 percent protein anchored globally near $230/MT,
subduing benchmarks and affirming surplus vibes. Southern Plains HRW basis edged firmer in spots via

International Cash & Trade Flows

Brazil: Even late in December, Brazil is still the reference point for nearby coverage. ANEC currently
points to December loadings near 2.81 MMT soybeans and 1.62 MMT corn, with soymeal 1.5 MMT
keeping protein flows steadier than whole beans into the turn. On price, Brazil remains the cheaper
nearby origin: Santos January beans around $10.05–10.10/bu leave the U.S. Gulf carrying a clear
premium, and Paranaguá corn near $204/MT keeps Brazil competitive into parts of Asia/MENA.
Operationally, nothing suggests major port gridlock. Agronomically, the market is still trading geography:
Center-West rainfall has been “good enough” for early development, while southern consistency is the
piece that can still inject risk if it turns choppy at the wrong time.

Argentina: The story is no longer the decree, it’s whether it converts into farmer selling. The duty cuts
and currency reset mechanically improve offer values (corn $178–182/MT; soybeans $428–432/MT), but
volumes will tell the truth as the new framework settles. Planting progress remains constructive (soy/corn
59 percent), supported by workable moisture, though the watch item is any meaningful drying trend
developing in Córdoba and nearby areas as crops move deeper into the critical window. Wheat is the
near-term lever: with harvest at 60 percent complete and production ideas near 27.7 MMT, Argentina has
the supply to show up, participation and logistics decide the pace.

China: Demand still looks selective rather than rebuild-mode. Crushers continue to prefer South
American arrivals for Q1 on pricing, and recent U.S. soybean business (1.707 MMT for 2025/26 through
the Dec 11 reporting week) still reads more strategic than broad commercial restocking. Corn and wheat
remain the louder absence, tariff suspensions may still be on the books, but follow-through buying has
been limited. Domestic pricing is firm, yet not consistently strong enough to force a durable import
arbitrage that pulls the U.S. into the center of the story.

EU / Black Sea: Wheat stays capped by the Black Sea’s ability to defend share. Russian FOB remains
the benchmark in the upper-$220s to mid-$230s/MT, while Ukraine continues to move grain through
Danube/rail channels despite periodic infrastructure risk. Ukraine corn $205/MT keeps pressure on EU
feed economics, and France around $229/MT leaves Europe competing for demand rather than setting it.
Net effect: the global hierarchy still favors the cheapest, most consistent origin, and that keeps rallies
hard to extend without a disruption.

Other Regions: Australia is wrapping harvest without major logistical drama, though output expectations
below 25 MMT reflect WA dryness even as eastern areas performed better. Canada continues routine
spring wheat movement, and India remains a non-factor on exports given its restrictive stance. No
secondary-origin shock is emerging that would meaningfully tighten the global balance into early Q1.

So What? Into year-end, the marginal price signals still come from the same places: Brazil for oilseeds
(nearby coverage) and the Black Sea for wheat (the ceiling). Argentina has improved competitiveness on
paper, but the timing depends on farmer selling and export execution. China remains present, but
uneven, enough to influence day-to-day tone, not enough to flip the balance.

Export Sales (USDA FAS): The latest full weekly report in the window covered the week ending Dec 11

Ethanol Production & Stocks (EIA): Biofuel metrics stayed constructive but cooled slightly into the

spreads/basis as the cleaner real-time demand read.

CFTC Positioning (latest available Disaggregated COT snapshot): The most recent snapshot (as of
Dec 16) fit the year-end structure: specs were not positioned in a way that forces a strong directional
move, but the bias differs by market. Corn showed managed money 234k long vs 288k short (net –53k),

money stayed net short across classes (HRW: 62k long vs 89k short, net –27k; HRS: 10k long vs 30k

Weather & Crop Conditions

United States
With U.S. harvest finished, weather is no longer a yield headline, it’s a winter setup and logistics story.
The market’s two practical watch points are how winter wheat goes into dormancy and whether Plains
moisture pockets recharge ahead of spring, and whether cold snaps and short-term river level swings
create friction in barge flow as normal business resumes. South America remains the bigger pricing
variable, but even there the emphasis is shifting from “total rainfall” to where the rain lands, especially the
South vs Center-West split in Brazil and any meaningful drying trend in Argentina’s core belt as crops
advance into their more sensitive stages.

Brazil
Brazil’s weather signal into year-end has been less about “nationwide threat” and more about regional
dispersion, the market keeps weighting rainfall consistency in the Center-West and how quickly southern
areas can stabilize as reproductive stages expand. USDA’s Weekly Weather and Crop Bulletin (covering
the week ending Dec 21) noted beneficial heavy rain across central and southern Brazil, a constructive
backdrop for early crop development and stand establishment in key production zones. With planting
essentially wrapped up (DATAGRO indicated Brazil soybean planting near completion by late December),
attention naturally shifts toward the first concrete field reads that start to show up in January, especially
where rainfall distribution has been uneven.

Synthesis & Outlook

Grain and oilseed markets closed out 2025 much like the latter half unfolded: futures in tight ranges, with
cash flows and basis offering the sharpest insights. Corn stood out as the steadiest on demand delivery,
soybeans leaned on crush amid export drags, and wheat navigated surplus pressures while US
shipments provided quiet backing. Holiday thinness amplified some moves, but spreads, basis, and
verified flows drove the core narrative over broader macros.

Recent weekly reads into late December highlight the divides. Export inspections for the week ending
Dec 25 came in at 1.30 MMT for corn, 0.75 MMT for soybeans, and 0.30 MMT for wheat. Marketing-year-
to-date, corn flows lead last year (25.6 MMT vs 15.4 MMT for corn last year), wheat edges ahead (15.1
MMT vs 12.4 MMT), and soybeans trail sharply (15.4 MMT vs 28.7 MMT). Ethanol held constructive but
eased: output at 1.095 mbpd (down from 1.131 prior), stocks steady near 22.5 million barrels. In short,
corn pull persists, but 2026 opens with less urgency to chase processor volumes than early December
suggested.

Weather remains the prime swing factor into the new year, though signals grew subtler lately. USDA's
bulletin flagged helpful Dec 21 rains in Brazil's central and southern zones through Dec 21, drier

rain placement, southern Brazil vs Center-West, and Argentina's hold on current levels, over blanket
bull/bear calls

Ethanol for America Act

The major barrier to a higher Renewable Fuel Standard (RFS) is the fuel versus food debate.  Now is the
perfect time to move to E15.  Corn prices are extremely low, and the latest USDA WASDE reported a record
crop and a record crop expansion.

USDA WASDE forecast corn production at 16.7 billion bushels, with the yield at 188.8 bushels per acre and
ending  stocks  at 2.1 billion.  The season-average corn price dropped  30 cents to $3.90 a bushel.   Most
importantly, 202526 crop year is expected to have 1.8 billion more bushels than 2024/25.

With current ethanol production consuming 5.6 billion bushels, a 10 percent increase in consumption would
consume an additional 560 million bushels.  The reality is that year around E15 would add approximately
50 million bushels and regulatory streamlining would add another 50 million bushels.  At some point over
the  next  ten  years,  the  additional  corn  consumption  could  be  2.8  billion  bushels  minus  lower  fuel
consumption resulting from electric vehicles.  The ethanol corn consumption increase is likely to be more
of a S curve as new compacity comes online.

With  Brazil  and  the  US  corn  and  soybean  production  increasing,  100  million  bushels  of  ethanol  corn
consumption will barely move the needle.  The ethanol production capacity expansion that would be three
to four years from now is when the corn consumption impact to the corn market would be felt.
